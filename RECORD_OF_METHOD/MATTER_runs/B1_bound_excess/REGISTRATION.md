# B1 — does a bound pattern's speed excess follow its parts' masses or its whole mass? (registered 13 Nov 2026)

**Why it matters.** A massive pattern on CO's walk outruns light by s ≈ 0.077 θ (θ = rest energy per tick) and falls at g c²(1 + s)² (run G5). If a nucleus carries s of its whole mass, titanium and platinum fall apart by about 3 × 10⁻¹⁴ at the Crab bound on ℓ, and MICROSCOPE (10⁻¹⁵) bounds ℓ ≲ 5 × 10⁻³³ m, tighter than the Crab. If it carries s of its parts (quarks, electrons), the effect is far below any test. Most of a nucleon's mass is not its quarks' masses but their motion and binding field.

**Model.** Two distinguishable patterns on CO's 1D palindromic walk (w = 0.3), each with a small mass flip θ, held by a confining force: a phase e^{−iσ|r|} per step on their separation r (a linear potential, string tension σ). For σ ≪ 1 the bound state is much larger than a hop and its mass M is mostly motion and binding, M ≫ 2θ: the nucleon-like case. Fixed total momentum K reduces the problem to the separation on a ring of L places with 4 spin states. The bound state's quasi-energy E(K) at small K gives c_eff² from E² = M² + c_eff² K², and s_comp = c_eff/c₀ − 1.

**Predictions compared.**
- *Parts:* s_comp ≈ s(θ), small when θ is small.
- *Whole:* s_comp ≈ s(M), the excess of a single pattern of rest energy M.

**Pre-check.** σ = 0 (a free pair at threshold): s_comp = s(θ) exactly, since E = 2E₁(K/2).

**Criteria.** With θ ≤ 0.01 and M ≥ 5θ, s_comp within 30% of s(θ) and far from s(M): the excess follows the parts. Within 30% of s(M): it follows the whole. Anything else: neither, reported as found. Size check: the bound state's rms separation at least 10 hops; L at least 8 times that.

**What it decides.** Whether MICROSCOPE becomes CO's sharpest bound on ℓ (whole), or the composition question closes with no new bound (parts). One dimension only; 3D and a gauge-field binding are not modelled.
