# I4: direction dependence of induced light: registration (14 Nov 2026, before the run)

**Reading under test.** The direction dependence found while preparing the bending test (Z_E = +0.029, +0.034, −0.049 along x, y, z at θ = 0.03) lives only in finite (lattice-scale) parts: the leading log is isotropic, the finite parts are not, because the palindrome's nesting order (x outermost, z innermost) breaks cubic symmetry. If so, induced light's speed depends on direction and polarisation by Δ/(2 ln(1/θ)), with Δ finite.

**Measure.** Patched pair method (filled–filled pairs dropped), nested-cube quadrature (9 cells), each coefficient fitted as C₀ + c₂q̂² + c₄q̂⁴ at q = θ/20, θ/10, θ/5. Permittivity ε_j = −4c₂ for a potential varying along j (j = x, y, z). Magnetic coefficient Z_B(i, j) = 4c₂ for a phase on j-hops varying along i (six pairs i ≠ j). Induced light propagating along i with polarisation j: c_ij = √(Z_B(i, j)/ε_j). D_ij = 2 ln(1/θ)·(c_ij/c₀ − 1).

**Cases.** θ = 10⁻³, 3×10⁻³, 10⁻², 3×10⁻²; w = 5π/16.

**Outcomes (stated first).**
- Anisotropic (finite parts direction-dependent): at θ = 10⁻³ the spread of D_ij over the six (max − min) exceeds 0.01, and it does not fall toward zero across θ (spread at 10⁻³ ≥ ½ the spread at 3×10⁻²).
- Isotropic: the spread at 10⁻³ is below 0.01.
- Otherwise inconclusive.
At ln(1/θ) = 53 a spread of 0.01 in D means 10⁻⁴ in speed, far above birefringence and anisotropy bounds; any finite spread would need direction-dependent counter-terms.
