# G3 — G1 on the lattice carriers themselves

**Question.** Run G1 assumed the ten carriers form a smooth tensor h_μν acted on by continuum gauge changes. Here the carriers sit where CO puts them: the lapse h_00 and the shear h_ij (i ≠ j) at places; the stretch h_ii and the one-way part h_0i on the links along i. "Moving places" and "reparametrising time" are built on the network: a place shift ξ_i and a time shift ξ_0 at places, acting through lattice differences (symbol s_j = 2 sin(k_jℓ/2) across a link, c_j = cos(k_jℓ/2) for averaging to a link midpoint, s_j c_j for a centred difference at a place). Time is kept continuous (symbol k_0). Count the quadratic costs, with two difference symbols times even cosine factors up to second degree (nearest-neighbour range), that are exactly invariant under these lattice gauge changes and under the cube's symmetries and time reversal.

**Outcome unknown, and what would change.** (a) No exactly invariant cost exists: the lattice gauge changes do not close on these carriers, and G1's identification fails as built; the carriers' placement must change. (b) Invariant costs exist and all reduce to Fierz–Pauli at long wavelength, isotropic at leading order: G1's identification is confirmed on the lattice, with anisotropy only at higher order (its size measured). (c) Invariant costs exist but some do not reduce to Fierz–Pauli, or are anisotropic at leading order: G1's result does not survive the lattice.

**Post-run check.** The k → 0 limit of each invariant cost compared with the Fierz–Pauli form; tensor-wave speed along an axis and a body diagonal at small and moderate k.

**Added before running variant B (after variant A returned no invariant cost).** Variant B places the shear h_ij on the face (square) spanned by i and j instead of at the place, so its gauge change is s_i c_j ξ_j + s_j c_i ξ_i (difference along one side, average along the other), the placement linearised Regge calculus uses. Same count, same check.
