### 3. Why these, and not the alternatives

**What a renewal cannot be** (every massive thing renews about E/(aħ) times a second: 1.4×10²⁴ s⁻¹ per nucleon at a = 1):
- **A localisation at the hop.** It would fix a body's position 10²⁰–10²⁸ times a second, which kills all interference of massive objects. Its stamps would also tell apart branches at different counts.
- **A localisation at any interaction scale.** At 10⁻¹¹ m it heats matter at about 7×10⁴ W per nucleon, against Earth's 8×10⁻¹² W/kg, and it destroys the measured entanglement of drums and bulk acoustic modes.
- **A kept count.** That would put which-path information into time.
- **A gravity deposit.** That would give λ_a ≈ 10²⁴ s⁻¹ and remove the lunar and gas bounds.
- **Switched off in flight.** That fails for committed bodies, and the rule would depend on future records.

**Where a deposit lands.** Option (i), drawing the place with a state update, heats matter. Option (ii), drawing it without one, is nonlinear in the state: the (S)/Gisin signalling that Reading I0 excluded. Option (iii) is linear and causes no heating.

**Why not whole-body deposits (reading (b)).**
- **Timing.** A hit at one end of a rod would put deposits at the far end at a time the near-end party chooses: (T2)-type.
- **Placement.** A surface hit does not fix interior positions. A fixed template of the body's shape is excluded by the measured gravity of a vibrating beam (Brack et al. 2022). The state's mean is nonlinear, and fresh sampling heats matter.

**Precedent.** This is the structure of GRW flash gravity (Tilloy 2018): each particle's spontaneous collapse sources only its own mass.

### 4. What follows: solids gravitate through route (b)

**A solid's interior is recorded only internally.** Lattice scattering hands its recoil to vibrations or to the whole crystal. Conduction electrons are about 2×10⁻⁴ of the mass. Cosmic rays and decays are far too rare. So the interior's gravity is laid down by route (b) alone, smeared at r_C:
$$
g(d)=\frac{GM}{d^2}\Big[(1-f)+f\,w(d/r_C)\Big],\qquad f=\frac{\lambda_b}{\Gamma_{\rm ext}+\lambda_b}\to1 ,
$$
with w the fraction of a Gaussian of standard deviation √1.5 r_C that is enclosed. Newton holds only at d ≫ r_C.

**The consequences:**
- **The short-range cap.** The 52 μm inverse-square test (Lee et al. 2020) requires r_C ≲ 5.1×10⁻⁵ m, uncertain by about ±20% (43 μm at a 1% criterion).
- **The floor from gravity's graininess.** A test mass's own deposits kick it by Δv = μ g_TM(x)/M, with μ = ħ/(κ_b c²). Against LISA Pathfinder's 1.74 fm s⁻² Hz⁻¹ᐟ²:
$$
\kappa_b\ \ge\ \frac{4\hbar J}{c^2M_{\rm TM}^2{\rm ASD}^2},\qquad J_{\rm self}=1.222\,(G\rho L)^2\rho L^3 ,
$$
  which gives 3.63×10⁻³⁶ from the self term alone, and 5.6×10⁻³⁶ with a solid electrode housing. This is conservative: it counts the whole plateau as room for route (b).
- **Symmetrisation.** Under GRW-type symmetrisation, identical units within r_C share a localisation, and heating, emission and break-up fall by
$$
R=\frac{1}{2n(2\pi r_C^2)^{3/2}},
$$
  which is exact on a lattice; the suppression 1/R is about 10¹⁶ at 10⁻⁵ m in D₂O. 'indep' and 'com' then share one window.
- **Photons (ruling 11).** Light does not commit on its own. It commits only through route (a). So the photon-coherence and X-ray bounds no longer apply.
- **Photons' gravity after recombination** is kept by Thomson scattering on leftover and reionised electrons. With ruling 9, the population's mean draw is exact for any time-varying rate (Campbell's theorem). A Monte Carlo along the real Thomson history gives 1.00000.
- **Neutrinos' gravity** is kept by rare weak-interaction records after decoupling, for the same reason. There is no N_eff deficit, and no neutrino-mass prediction. The weak-field validity condition needs only m₁ ≳ 10⁻¹⁴–10⁻¹⁰ eV.
