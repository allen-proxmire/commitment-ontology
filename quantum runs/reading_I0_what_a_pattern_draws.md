### Reading I0: what does an uncommitted pattern draw? (the first half of Note I's test I0; a reading, no run)

*27 October 2026. A reading of the record against established physics. Nothing is run. An independent check found errors and omissions in the first draft, and its corrections are adopted here. The rulings it led to (27 October) are at the end.*

**Question.** Does a pattern of possibility draw before it commits? If it is in a superposition, does it draw:
- as one source, spread over its branches;
- as separate branches;
- or only through its commitments?

The answer decides what the commitment trigger can read (Note I §5) and what Note I's later tests compute.

**What the record says.**
1. **Patterns draw.** This was ruled on 19 October in Run 33, adopting the earlier ruling D30 ("Radiation gravitates"; consistent, not derived).
   - The adopted rule, R-b, counts a pattern's energy, which was added, and its momentum flux, which is borrowed through the Lorentz input of Run 34. Together they make the Tolman–Komar source T⁰⁰ + ΣTⁱⁱ.
   - The memo's measured grounds are:
     - binding energy (Δm = E/c², MICROSCOPE, the lunar crust and mantle);
     - radiation: the radiation-era expansion counts photons and neutrinos at full weight, N_eff = 2.99 ± 0.17 (Planck 2018).
   - Run 33's alternative, R-c ("only commitments pull; patterns and radiation do not"), was set aside because it fails radiation, and because its stress weight is 0 against 3p. The record attributes R-c to the earlier ontology's §12 (11 October): superpositions do "not generate curvature". R-c works for bound patterns only if they "are renewing commitments whose rate includes their binding".
2. **What the engine draws.**
   - **Run 36** used the earlier clock-half equation, −∇²ψ + m²N²ψ = ω²ψ, with draw ψ²(4m²N² − 2ω²) + 6g. Its pattern was one stationary quantum, the ground state, solved self-consistently with the field it draws (to 10⁻¹¹), at a test strength too small to matter: doubling κ changes the result by 10⁻⁵.
   - **The full form, R6,** was adopted on 20 October after Run 46:
$$
d=\kappa\big(4\omega^2N^{-4}-2m^2N^{-2}\big)\psi^2 ,
$$
     with R5's variational factor (1 + g) multiplying it. In both, "one quantum" is a normalisation of a classical Klein–Gordon field.
   - **No superposition has ever been run.**
3. **The superposed case is open in the record,** in two places:
   - the write-up's Note A (19 October), item 2: "whether a superposed pattern draws by its average energy (semiclassical) or only on commitment (ontology §12). This decides gravity's response to quantum superpositions";
   - the suggested next steps, item 18.

So Note I's A6 said more than the record does. Ruled: patterns draw. Implemented: one stationary pattern draws by its Tolman–Komar density (∝ ψ²). For such a pattern, which has one branch, every reading below gives the same draw. Open: a superposition.

**Four readings.**

**(S) By its average.** The draw is the pattern's energy density spread over all its branches. With CO's commitment added (route (a), a measurement), the field then follows the outcome.
- **The field equation.** With R4's field fixed each tick and Run 16's wave equation, a draw ∝ |ψ|² gives the Schrödinger–Newton equation at weak field and low velocity:
$$
i\hbar\,\partial_t\psi=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\nabla^2\psi-Gm^2\!\int\!\frac{|\psi(\mathbf x',t)|^2}{|\mathbf x-\mathbf x'|}\,d^3x'\;\psi .
$$
  Giulini and Großardt (2012) derived it this way for the classical, spherically symmetric Einstein–Klein–Gordon system. CO's own limit would be a calculation.
- **What it predicts.** Self-gravity slows a single particle's wavepacket from spreading from about 10⁹–10¹⁰ u, depending on the packet's width (Giulini and Großardt 2011). For composite bodies the centre-of-mass form differs.
- **The cost: signalling.** The dynamics is nonlinear. With a collapse, nonlinear deterministic dynamics signals faster than light (Gisin 1990; Polchinski 1991). For semiclassical gravity, Bahrami et al. (2014) put it this way: "together with the standard collapse postulate, fundamentally semi-classical gravity gives rise to superluminal signalling". So (S) with CO's commitment fails Note I's Q4 in principle, though the signal is far too small to measure.
- **(S) without any commitment** is excluded by Page and Geilker (1981). That experiment does not test how a pattern draws, since its masses were committed matter throughout.

**(B) Each branch draws its own field.** The clock field is then correlated with the branches, as the operator-valued Newtonian potential is in quantum mechanics.
- It can mediate entanglement.
- Penrose's E_G, the difference of the branches' self-energies, belongs to this reading.

**(C) Only through its records.** A pattern draws where and when it commits, as matter draws through its renewals (ONTOLOGY §1), and the draw is deposited at the place the local stamp records.
- **The plain form fails.** In Run 33's R-c a free pattern that never commits never draws. That fails the radiation era. Neutrinos, freely streaming since about 1 s, are the clean case: the microwave background shows their gravity directly, through the phase shift they put on the acoustic peaks (Follin et al. 2015).
- **So isolated free patterns must commit at a rate.** The draw is then the time-averaged density of commitments.
  - This is CO's counterpart of sourcing gravity from spontaneous localisations (Tilloy and Diósi 2016). It avoids nonlinear signalling because the ensemble dynamics stays linear.
  - Tilloy and Diósi find three things:
    - there is no one-particle self-interaction;
    - a smearing cut-off is needed;
    - the field's noise adds a back-action decoherence that grows as the localisation rate falls.
  - In the discrete, GRW-type form (Tilloy 2018), the 1/r law fails below the smearing length, and the gravitational decoherence goes as 1/λ.
- **What a commitment must deposit.** To keep the record's results, each commitment must deposit the full Tolman–Komar weight: 2E for radiation, normalised by 1/λ, with the momentum current (Run 34's weights). On average that gives the same draw as a continuous one only if the deposit carries the full Tolman–Komar density and r_C lies below the pattern's size, or if bound patterns count as matter (Run 33's condition for R-c). Runs 36, 40, 42 and 45–46 stand under (C) only in those cases.
- **No self-interaction before commitment.** Under (C) an uncommitted pattern does not move in its own field. So Note I's self-sourced threshold (§5) and reading (i) (§6) have no quantity to read, and route (b) has to be a rate.

**(H) By its average until it commits, then by the outcome.** Since (S) above already includes CO's commitment, (H) is the same reading as (S).

**Entanglement.** Under (S) and (C) the draws are c-numbers, an expectation value or a committed record, and the clock field is fixed from them. Such a field acts as an external potential:
- a mean-field coupling keeps product states product;
- feedback from a committed record is local operations and classical communication (LOCC);
- neither creates entanglement (Struyve 2026).

Being fixed each tick does not by itself make the field classical. The instantaneous Newtonian potential, fixed by operator-valued sources, does entangle masses, which is reading (B). So "gravity cannot entangle masses" holds for (S) and (C), and only if the medium itself holds no branches. Tests of gravitationally mediated entanglement (Bose et al. 2017; Marletto and Vedral 2017) have been proposed but not yet carried out.

A disputed claim (Aziz and Howl 2025) is that classical gravity can entangle quantised matter at a tiny level. Diósi (2025) and Marletto, Oppenheim, Vedral and Wilson (2025) dispute it, and it does not change this conclusion at the level such tests would reach.

**The rulings (27 October, stated by Allen).**
1. **(C): a pattern draws only through its records.**
2. **Records come from both routes.**
   - **Route (a):** a pattern that interacts with committed matter is recorded at its surroundings' resolution. Laboratory test masses, decohered all the time by gas and thermal radiation, therefore draw at fine resolution.
   - **Route (b):** an isolated pattern commits at random, at a rate λ, localising with a coarseness length r_C without being absorbed: "commit and continue".
3. **The medium holds no branches.** Its count N = P/P∞ has one value at each place. Patterns carry amplitudes and phases; the medium does not.

**What follows from the rulings.**
- **Gravity in CO cannot entangle masses.** If a test of gravitationally mediated entanglement finds entanglement, the rulings fail, and with them CO's account of how quantum matter draws. That is a sharp kill condition (Note I §8).
- **Route (b) is a rate, not a threshold.**
- **The first calculation is the (λ, r_C) window.** Because lab test masses draw through route (a), the inverse-square tests down to about 52 μm (Lee et al. 2020) do not bound r_C. Route (b) is bounded as follows:
  1. **Radiation's gravity.** The draw must follow the radiation's energy over the acoustic times, so λ ≫ c/(physical sound horizon at z ≈ 1100) ≈ 7×10⁻¹⁴ s⁻¹. And r_C must lie below the sub-Mpc acoustic scales.
  2. **Photon coherence.** Each localisation turns a photon by about λ_γ/(2πr_C). Diffraction-limited images through an aperture D, and interferometric fringes on a baseline D, after N localisations need
$$
r_C\gtrsim\sqrt N\,\frac{D}{2\pi},
$$
     which gives r_C ≳ 10⁶–10⁸ m for the longest radio baselines (Event Horizon Telescope, RadioAstron) if photons share the same r_C.
  3. **Gravity's back-action.** The decoherence from the field's noise grows as λ falls (Tilloy and Diósi 2016; Tilloy 2018). A classical field coupled to quantum matter also obeys a decoherence–diffusion trade-off (Oppenheim et al. 2023). Together these give a lower bound on λ for isolated mesoscopic masses.
  4. **Interference.** Route (b) must not decohere what is seen to interfere (Note I's Q1). Decoherence is suppressed by about (Δx/r_C)².
  5. **Heating** must stay under Note I's Q2. It falls as r_C grows.

  A large r_C satisfies 2, 4 and 5. Whether 1 and 3 leave a window open for one (λ, r_C) shared by photons and isolated massive patterns is the calculation's question. If r_C must be 10⁷ m or more, route (b) is invisible in a laboratory, and the origin of so long a length in CO's rules becomes the open question.
