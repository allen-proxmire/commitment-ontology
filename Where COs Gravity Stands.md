# Where ED's gravity stands: passed, failed, open

Oct 19, 2026 · @Allen Proxmire · Runs 1–60 of the commitment-ontology lattice record

## The short version

The lattice engine matches every gravity test it has been set against, as far as it can resolve, with one new tension. The one failure found along the way, patterns bound inside a body falling wrongly (Run 45), was fixed by the full pattern rule. With matter in exact hydrostatic balance, adding energy to a star raises its far-field mass by that energy, as in GR, to within about 0.5% on the engine's grids (Runs 50, 53, 56, 58). In a medium with no built-in scale, bound matter falls exactly like its energy (Run 48). ED's medium does have a scale, so, taking a body's inertia to be its total energy (Allen's ruling), compact bodies slightly break the strong equivalence principle (Runs 52–57); nothing in ED's papers fixes how much, and the pulsar triple bounds it. **The new tension:** ED's neutron stars hold only about three quarters of GR's maximum mass, so with standard nuclear matter ED cannot reach the measured 2 M☉ pulsars; it can with matter near the causal limit, and that matter passes nuclear theory and QCD (Runs 57, 59, 60).

- **Matched:** Newton, clocks slowing in gravity, everything falling alike, light bending, Mercury's orbit, the Earth–Moon test, frame dragging, binary-pulsar energy loss, bound energy pulling as mass, black-hole ringdowns and images.
- **How:** about a third come from ED's own rules. The rest build on established physics, as every new theory does: mostly "physics looks the same to a moving observer" (which GR also takes as a starting principle) and GR's black-hole exterior (which GR derives). They are kept as bookkeeping, marking where a later derivation could go deeper.
- **Failed:** many earlier ideas failed and were replaced. That is the record working as designed: each failure is kept, with what replaced it.
- **New predictions:** ED's central claim, that time runs one way at the bottom and the familiar time-symmetric physics is recovered by averaging, passed the engine's own registered test (Run 29). One new prediction already meets data, as a bound: compact bodies slightly break the strong equivalence principle, with a fixed sign (Run 55); the pulsar triple limits its size, which ED does not yet fix. ED's other claims (the arrow, a vacuum that does not pull, no singularities) have not yet been compared with data at physical scales.

## Failed, not yet fixed

One failure is still open.

- **The strong-field mass bookkeeping (Run 40b).** In a very strong field, the engine misses a bound pattern's binding by about one binding. In ordinary gravity what is left is tiny (Run 44). With GR's exterior adopted for black holes, it matters only inside compact objects and possibly in neutron stars.

## Open

Nine questions are open.

1. **Bound energy at neutron-star strength.** *Pulling:* with matter in exact point-by-point hydrostatic balance, ED's static rules come from a single energy principle, and adding a quantum raises a star's far-field mass by its energy. With four grids the registered test passes on the line: the continuum miss is within about 1% of the quantum's energy, and how the grid error shrinks is not settled (Runs 53, 56). The old shortfall was 4%. That is the same first law as GR's; it does not mean a neutron star's mass and radius match GR's, which is still open. *Falling:* in a medium with no built-in scale, bound matter falls exactly like its energy (Run 48). In ED's own medium a compact body is pulled slightly harder than its inertia, Δ ≈ C·p\_s/P₀, with C ≈ 0.33–0.49 for the pulsar in the triple system (Run 55).
2. **What sets the medium's scale, P₀/p\_s.** The draw rule that comes from one action (adopted 20 October) and inertia = total energy (ruled 20 October) turn ED's medium scale into a prediction: compact bodies slightly break the strong equivalence principle, Δ ≈ C·p\_s/P₀ (Runs 55, 57). **A reading of ED's clock and medium papers found nothing that fixes P₀/p\_s or either scale:** bandwidth has no upper bound or quantum, and the vacuum is only ever normalized. One tie turned up inside the engine: its Newton constant scales as 1/p\_s, so ED's identification of the hop with the Planck length would conflict with the engine's clocks unless the hop is about 300–1,000 Planck lengths.
3. **Neutron stars.** At first order, the pulsar triple tightens the medium's scale to P₀/p\_s ≳ 10⁵. A body's own binding passes its registered fall test (Run 46c, within 1 part in 1,000). With realistic equations of state, ED's own medium gives the pulsar C ≈ 0.40–0.49, so the triple needs P₀/p\_s ≳ 1.7–2.1×10⁵ (Run 57). **Maximum mass, a requirement on dense matter:** ED's static field holds about 0.75–0.8 of GR's maximum mass, and Allen ruled that ED's interior equations bind for neutron stars. With the standard equations of state SLy, APR4 and MPA1 ED's heaviest stars are 1.65, 1.69 and 1.91 M☉, short of PSR J0740+6620 (2.08 ± 0.07). With matter allowed only by nuclear theory, causality and QCD it passes (Run 60), but only if the sound speed is near the causal limit just above where nuclear theory is trusted: c\_s² ≥ 0.33 from 1.1 times nuclear density, or ≥ 0.8 from twice nuclear density. GR needs neither. Next: radii and tidal deformability, once ED's exterior beyond first order and its Love numbers are derived.
4. **Deriving the inputs:** Lorentz invariance (now extended to strongly self-gravitating bodies, so inertia = total energy), GR's black-hole exterior (option B, long-term), stress pulling, proper size.
5. **Signals through the instant field.** Shown to carry no signal only at first order.
6. **Black holes evaporate (Allen).** This rests on two assumptions: the core acts like a horizon for quantum fields, and its pull drops while its record stays. The endpoint is open: a Planck-mass remnant, or full evaporation (Allen leans this way).
7. **Does the radiation carry information out?** Paper 050 says yes, by two postulates. Mainstream physics has not settled this either.
8. **Superposed patterns.** Does a superposed pattern pull by its average energy, or only when it commits?
9. **The vacuum energy's size.** Its coefficient has not been computed.

## Where ED says something new (not yet tested)

ED makes six claims GR and quantum mechanics do not. Only the last two concern data already in hand: one as a bound, one as a demand on nuclear physics.

- **Time's direction is fundamental.** This is ED's central claim, and it passed the engine's own registered test (Run 29): commitments are strictly one-way, and averaging over time recovers the time-symmetric-looking records that standard physics describes. What remains is comparing it with data at physical scales, where the leftover order (about c/T in a clock's record) is not yet known.
- **The vacuum never commits, so it does not pull.** That matches how small the observed cosmological constant is, but its size has not been computed.
- **No singularities.** Possibility per place is finite, so a well ends where it runs out (Paper 042).
- **Information is kept as record.** What falls into a black hole becomes part of the history, never lost. Whether the radiation carries it back out is not yet derived.
- **Neutron stars differ from GR in two ways.** (1) A small strong-equivalence-principle violation: ED's medium has a built-in scale, so, with inertia = total energy, a neutron star is pulled slightly harder than its inertia, Δ ≈ C·p\_s/P₀, positive and growing with compactness (C ≈ 0.40–0.49 for the pulsar in PSR J0337+1715, Run 57). GR gives exactly zero. The pulsar triple bounds it (P₀/p\_s ≳ 1.7–2.1×10⁵); nothing in ED fixes its size. (2) Heavy stars need very stiff matter: ED holds about three quarters of GR's maximum mass, so the measured 2 M☉ pulsars require nuclear matter near the causal limit above about twice nuclear density (Runs 57, 59). Nuclear theory and neutron-star radii can test that, once their inferences are redone without assuming GR.

One surprising prediction that checks out would matter more than any number of matches.
