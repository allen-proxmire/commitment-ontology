# The beginning: what it does and what it still owes

Shapes and readings, graded as such. Numbers from RECORD_OF_METHOD/MATTER_runs/C12_first_ball/ (the visible universe's part of the ball; the standard hot history run back to light at the hop's energy; ℓ between 5.7 × 10⁻³⁵ m and the candidate 1.0 × 10⁻³² m).

## Inflation's four jobs, in the ball's units

| job | the standard need | in CO |
|---|---|---|
| one temperature everywhere | without inflation, the ball at the end of the epoch is 10⁸¹–10⁸⁸ regions never in contact (Hubble length 260 hops at the largest ℓ, 1.5 at the smallest) | every place commits at once, with no order among the first commitments (the beginning's shape): sameness is put in by the rule, not made by contact |

The epoch itself is short: about 6 e-folds in length (dark_matter.md), not inflation's 60. At its end space grows by 0.4% per tick in length at the largest ℓ and 66% at the smallest.

## Flatness (21 Nov 2026, desk)

*The tuning in its usual form* treats the expansion rate and the density as two numbers that must agree to 1 part in 10⁵⁶–10⁶¹ at the epoch's end. For uniform space Einstein's constraint is Friedmann's equation, and the one free datum is the curvature k, whose scale stretches with space: today's bound |Ω_k| < 0.002 says the curvature radius is at least 7 times the visible universe's radius, at every epoch.

*On the network.* A uniformly curved space is a flat one with a stretch that bends slowly from place to place (conformally flat; ψ² ∝ 1/(1 + kr²/4)); curvature is the stretch's large-scale bending (its second derivative; a plain gradient is flat). On the cubic graph, k = 0 is the stretch being uniform, and today's bound asks that it vary by no more than about half a percent across the visible ball.

*The flat rate in CO's constants.* With G = δc³ℓ²/(4πħ) and a place holding energy ε·ħc/ℓ, the constraint gives

$$(H\tau)^2=\frac{2}{3}\,\delta\,\varepsilon,\qquad \tau=\ell/c,$$

the growth per tick in length; births per place per tick are 3Hτ. (ε ≈ 0.66 reproduces the standard history's 0.4% and 66% per tick.)

*What this does to the problem.* It moves the tuning; it does not remove it. Two things must hold:
1. *The births obey the constraint.* The earlier rule for the epoch (births at the commitment rate of over-capacity places) cannot: its rate is fixed per tick, while the constraint's falls as √ε through the epoch, by about 10⁴. Chosen (candidate): expansion is carried by the shift, an outward river b = H(x − x₀), and, in the comoving description, births follow it, τ∇·b = 3Hτ per place per tick (Λ's birth chance its constant part). With the stretch uniform the constraint is then 6H² = 16πGρ + 2Λc², so the birth rate is the constraint: an identity, not a match. On the step a ball sourcing its own outward river expands as flat dust (run F7: shells on the k = 0 path within 2.3%, one growth factor for all shells with a spread of 1.1%; the river and the uniform ball are entered). The river was entered at zero energy, the flat case; why the beginning makes that river is item 2.
2. *"At once" covers the stretch,* not only the temperature: the places' stretch uniform to half a percent across the ball.

For expansion the births are what happens and the river is the bookkeeping (chosen, candidate): the two agree only at long wavelength, and a river through fixed places would shift a distant pattern's lattice terms by its speed (E → E + b sin k, so c₂ → c₂ − b/2 along the river), a shift that grows with distance from x₀ and so gives the observer a special centre the universe does not have. Near a mass at rest on the network the river flows through fixed places; nothing is born or removed there, the shift of the lattice terms is real and the mass is a real centre. How a general river splits into the two parts is open.

*If births cannot exceed one per place per tick:* 3Hτ ≤ 1 needs δε ≤ 1/6; at ε ≈ 0.66, δ ≤ 0.25 and ℓ ≥ 1.15 × 10⁻³⁴ m. Conditional on item 1 and on that cap; not a bound.

*The river and the tick.* The river grows with distance from the chosen centre, and the tick's limit on a drift (about π/τ_f − 3 times light's speed along an axis) is reached at that many Hubble lengths: 4.9 at τ_f = 0.4, 13 at 0.2, 28 at 0.1 (about 2.8 along a body diagonal at 0.4). Since τ_f is a convention and any place can be the centre, this is a limit on one description's reach, not an edge of the network; in the comoving description (births, no river) it does not arise.

Grade: reading. With births following the river (chosen, candidate), the birth rate is gravity's constraint and flatness is the stretch being uniform across the ball; that uniformity rests on the beginning and is not shown.

## What the ripples ask of the first event (21 Nov 2026, desk; run C13)

Working picture: the ripples are part of the beginning itself; the one event was not perfectly even. Read on the network, the curvature ripple ζ is the unevenness of the places' stretch (equivalently the count of places in a region, uneven by 3ζ). What the sky asks of it at the epoch's end, in the ball's units (ball radius 4.7 × 10²⁹ hops):

| asked | size |
|---|---|
| unevenness of the stretch, per e-fold of size | 4.6 × 10⁻⁵ (count of places: 1.4 × 10⁻⁴) |
| over which sizes | every size seen, from 10³⁰ hops (the whole sky) to 7 × 10²⁵ hops (2 Mpc today): 10⁷⁷ to 10⁹⁰ places |
| change with size | 4% larger for each tenfold larger size (n_s = 0.965) |
| independent noise from place to place, for comparison | 10⁻³⁹ to 10⁻⁴⁶ on those sizes; it reaches the need only at 5 × 10⁷ places, 375 hops across, 0.35 m today |
| shear (gravitational-wave) part | below 9 × 10⁻⁶ |
| bell-curve shape | skew below about 5 × 10⁻⁴ of the unevenness |
| matter-to-light ratio | the same everywhere to about 10⁻⁵ beyond the ripple itself |

So the first event's unevenness does not average away with size: any region, of 10⁷⁷ places or 10⁹⁰, is off in its count by about one part in seven thousand. That is a correlation across the whole ball, which a rule for the beginning has to supply. The epoch as now ruled decelerates (H falls faster than 1/a: by 10⁴ over about 6 e-folds, H ∝ a^−1.5), so it cannot stretch small jitter to these sizes as inflation does. Grade: silence, with the targets stated.

## Can the growth make the ripples? (21 Nov 2026, desk; run C14)

Four growth rules, one measure: how uneven the count of places in a region is, against the region's size.

| growth rule | unevenness against size | against the need |
|---|---|---|
| every first place grows its own family, family sizes random | falls as size^−½ | excluded: a family (2 × 10⁷–2 × 10⁸ places) is under 600 hops across; the smallest size seen holds 10⁷⁷ places |
| random births: any place may bear the next, the child beside it | the same at every size, of order one | excluded: about 10⁴ times too uneven |
| a conserved amount in whole units, each unit sent one way or the other at random | falls as size^−½ | excluded |
| a conserved amount divided in two again and again, each division uneven by a fixed fraction s | the same at every size: each halving adds s² to the variance | meets the size and tilt with s = 3√A_s · √(ln 2 / 3) = 6.6 × 10⁻⁵ (one part in 15,000), larger by 0.4% per halving toward the larger sizes |

*In the broad start* (about 7 × 10⁸⁰ first places at once) no growth rule reaches the sizes seen: a tree's correlations live inside one family. The nearness of 5 × 10⁷ places to the family's size is then a coincidence, and the ripples stay with the first event (the working picture).

*In the seed start* (on file; space and gravity record, 13 Nov) the visible ball is one tree of 292–296 halvings (68 e-folds in length) and the sizes seen are its top 37–42 levels. There the ripples reduce to one number of the birth share: how unevenly a place's possibility divides when it bears a new place, with the tilt as that number's slow drift. This is a cascade with logarithmic correlations and a bell-curve shape to first order in s (a sum of many small independent terms), which the 13 Nov read asked for and did not have. It asks two more things of the births: every place divides in step, to better than s (births at random times bring back the second row's order-one unevenness), and the unevenly divided amount must end as an uneven count of places. It says nothing of the shear part, and it leaves the seed start's two costs standing (no source of matter; too many gravitational waves unless births are slow).

Grade: the first three rules excluded as the ripples' source; the uneven division a shape, for the seed start only. The tree is idealised: regions are taken as one ancestor's descendants, so the equal unevenness holds in tree distance, not yet in space (where a tree is neither the same from every place nor the same in every direction), and the match to A_s carries a factor of order one.
