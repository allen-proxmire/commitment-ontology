# Where CO's choices bite: a prediction map (14 Nov 2026; reading)

A prediction here means a number or a sign CO must stand behind before it is measured. Ordered by how close it is to a test.

## 1. The speed of ultra-high-energy protons relative to light

**Two lattice effects.** (a) A massive pattern's limiting speed exceeds the massless walk's by s = κ_w mcℓ/ħ, with κ_w = ½ sin w (numerical, to 10⁻⁴; isotropic; 0.35 to 0.46 in the window). (b) The massless walk's group speed falls at high momentum as c₀(1 + c₂(kℓ)²), with c₂ = −0.086 along an axis, −0.068 along a face diagonal and −0.062 along a body diagonal (w = 5π/16, inside the window π/4 < w < 3π/8 that the extra species need).

**Why (a) alone gives no test.** For a species of fixed mass, s is part of that species' limiting speed, which a counter-term per species would set to light's along with the loop shift. So (a) survives only under a further rule, that the counter-terms equalise the massless walk's speed with light's and are blind to mass; and it sits far below the precision the counter-terms need (α/π down to 10⁻²⁰). At 10²⁰ eV, (b) is 10³ to 10⁵ times larger than (a) in any case (partons at kℓ ~ 10⁻⁸ at the floor ℓ ≥ 3.5 ℓ_P).

**What decides it.** The proton's speed against light's at 10²⁰ eV is fixed once light's lattice form (the link field's own cost) and the tuning rule are fixed; it would have to match the walk's quadratic term direction by direction to 10⁻³–10⁻⁵ for protons at 10²⁰ eV (if seen) to survive. A primary proton above the Cherenkov threshold E_th = mc²/√(2s) bounds s_p < 4 × 10⁻²³ at 10²⁰ eV (Coleman and Glashow 1999). The Pierre Auger Observatory finds the composition heavier and purer up to about 10²⁰ eV (model-dependent); standard propagation also predicts few protons there, so the data could falsify CO's eventual number but not confirm it. **Grade:** under straight light, agreement only (see §2 for the test light's form leaves).

**Light's lattice form decides it** (MATTER record, 14 Nov). Composites bend with c₂ Σ⟨x³⟩ ≈ −4 × 10⁻⁴, so light's quadratic term must be no lower than that in every direction (needed); two-mode light and the standard link field fail it. With light running straight (posited inside that window), protons, nuclei, pions and neutrinos are safe, and the proton test is agreement only.

## 2. Photons that split into electron pairs

With straight light, photons split into an electron pair above E_th = C√(mₑc²·ħc/ℓ), C = 5.4–6.1 by direction if the mass-linear excess s survives the counter-terms (3.8–4.1 if not): 10–11 PeV at the Crab bound (ℓ ≲ 3.0 × 10⁻³² m) and 2.3–2.6 × 10¹⁷ eV at the smallest ℓ. LHAASO's highest photon was then 2.5 PeV (3.7 PeV since; see below). A photon above about 11 PeV would bound ℓ below the Crab bound; a confirmed photon above about 3 × 10¹⁷ eV would exclude straight light at every allowed ℓ, under either rule. **Grade: candidate test, conditional on the tuning rule, B1's import and gravity's quadratic term (which gravitational Cherenkov also constrains).**

## 3. No entanglement through gravity alone

Amounts are classical, so gravity cannot entangle two masses (the falsifier; shared with semiclassical gravity). Gravity-mediated entanglement experiments (the Bose and Marletto–Vedral proposals) test it directly. **Grade: candidate** (shared with other classical-gravity models; consistent with Page and Geilker 1981). CO's own refinement, that gravity's source follows a body's engagements rather than its unresolved state, separates it from mean-field (Schrödinger–Newton) gravity only for unengaged superpositions; a number for a concrete set-up is owed.

## 4. A stopping number for measurement

N is a constant set only by experiment (N ≳ 50 under the stated readings). The prediction is a form: as the coupling to a channel falls, the breadth of loss should stop shrinking once the chain needed to reach N quanta stops forming. **Grade: shape** (the value free; the observable to plateau needs naming).

**The photon-splitting test: its mark and who can see it (15 Nov 2026).**
- *The record photon.* LHAASO's Cygnus X-3 photons (LHAASO Collaboration, arXiv:2512.16638): 3.73 ± 0.41 PeV and 3.08 ± 0.34 PeV, at about 9 kpc, cosmic-ray misidentification chances 2.3 × 10⁻⁴ and 3.5 × 10⁻⁴. Under CO a photon that crossed 9 kpc was below its threshold, so ℓ < C²mₑc²ħc/E²: 2.4 × 10⁻³¹ m at 3.32 PeV (1σ low) and the weakest direction (C = 5.10), δ ≳ 6 × 10⁻⁸ (1.9 × 10⁻³¹ m, δ ≳ 9 × 10⁻⁸ at the central energy; 7.8 × 10⁻³² m at 3.32 PeV if the photon ran along a lattice axis). Each factor 2 in a surviving photon's energy cuts the bound on ℓ by 4.
- *The mark.* Above threshold a photon splits within a short distance, so the cutoff does not depend on a source's distance: no source in a given direction is seen above the same energy, near or far (the band just above threshold where a photon could cross kiloparsecs is far narrower than any energy resolution). Ordinary absorption on the microwave background depends on distance (mean free path about 10 kpc near 2 PeV). The cutoff energy varies across the sky with C, from 2.9 along the lattice axes to 5.1 along the body diagonals (s kept): a factor of up to 1.75 (1.5 if the counter-terms absorb the excess) in the cube's pattern, fixed to CO's rest frame, its orientation unknown (one scale and three angles).
- *Who can see it.* LHAASO at 3–10 PeV from galactic sources: at the candidate synchrotron bound the cutoff sits at 5–9 PeV, so CO there predicts a common cutoff just above today's highest photons, and a photon above about 10 PeV in any direction would push ℓ below the synchrotron value. The Pierre Auger Observatory above 2 × 10¹⁷ eV (hybrid searches from its low-energy extensions; AugerPrime improves them): standard physics expects a small flux there (cosmic rays striking galactic gas; photons from cosmic rays on the microwave background lie mostly above 10¹⁸ eV), below present sensitivity, so seeing none separates nothing; one confirmed photon above about 2.1 × 10¹⁷ eV, from any direction, excludes CO's straight light (above about 1.1 × 10¹⁷ eV along a lattice axis).
- *The second test, N.* The many-body echo test (RECORD_OF_METHOD/MATTER_notes/N_signature_note.md): present data give N ≳ 50 (recovery after about 10² correlated spins under the secular dipolar Hamiltonian); the cheapest qualifying run adds spin counting to an existing adamantane echo setup.

**Gravity and superposition: what can test CO, and when (desk survey, 15 Nov 2026).** CO's gravity rule (gravity's source set by engagements; SPACE_AND_GRAVITY.md, Classical source) separates CO from other gravity models, not from quantum mechanics in any experiment now running.
- *Entanglement through gravity alone (the falsifier).* Bose et al. and Marletto–Vedral (2017) need a picogram-scale mass in a micrometre-scale superposition with a coherence time near a second (ERC CLOSEtoQG's statement of the requirement). The largest spatial superposition so far is a sodium cluster above 170,000 u, about 3 × 10⁻²² kg (Pedalino et al., Nature 2026): some eight orders of magnitude short. At the doubling-per-year pace the Vienna group aims for, decades. Simplified schemes with one mass in superposition (Saldanha, Marletto and Vedral, 2026) or accelerated entanglement with inverted potentials (Fujita et al. 2025) shorten it but keep the mass gap. CO predicts no entanglement in every variant; quantized gravity predicts entanglement.
- *Self-gravity of a monitored oscillator.* A continuously measured, quantum-limited low-frequency oscillator would show a line at √(ω_c² + ω_SN²) under Schrödinger–Newton-type semiclassical gravity (Liu et al. 2022). CO predicts no such line, as quantum mechanics does: it separates CO from semiclassical gravity, not from quantum mechanics. Proposals use torsion pendulums near the quantum regime; years to a decade.
- *Classical-gravity consistency.* Any classical gravity acting on quantum matter faces the decoherence–diffusion trade-off (Oppenheim et al. 2023), already squeezed by interferometer coherence and torsion-balance noise. CO's source jumps at engagements made by other interactions, so its decoherence is supplied by those engagements, not by gravity; how the trade-off applies to a weakly engaged body is open (record: costs, open).
- *Reading.* No near-term experiment separates CO's gravity from quantum mechanics plus general relativity; the entanglement test separates CO from quantized gravity and is decades out. The near-term tests stay the photon ceiling (LHAASO, Auger) and the stopping number N (many-body echoes).
