# Where CO's choices bite: a prediction map (14 Nov 2026; reading)

A prediction here means a number or a sign CO must stand behind before it is measured. Ordered by how close it is to a test.

## 1. The speed of ultra-high-energy protons relative to light

**Two lattice effects.** (a) A massive pattern's limiting speed exceeds the massless walk's by s = κ_w mcℓ/ħ, with κ_w = ½ sin w (numerical, to 10⁻⁴; isotropic; 0.35 to 0.46 in the window). (b) The massless walk's group speed falls at high momentum as c₀(1 + c₂(kℓ)²), with c₂ = −0.086 along an axis, −0.068 along a face diagonal and −0.062 along a body diagonal (w = 5π/16, inside the window π/4 < w < 3π/8 that the extra species need).

**Why (a) alone gives no test.** For a species of fixed mass, s is part of that species' limiting speed, which a counter-term per species would set to light's along with the loop shift. So (a) survives only under a further rule, that the counter-terms equalise the massless walk's speed with light's and are blind to mass; and it sits far below the precision the counter-terms need (α/π down to 10⁻²⁰). At 10²⁰ eV, (b) is 10³ to 10⁵ times larger than (a) in any case (partons at kℓ ~ 10⁻⁸ at the floor ℓ ≥ 3.5 ℓ_P).

**What decides it.** The proton's speed against light's at 10²⁰ eV is fixed once light's lattice form (the link field's own cost) and the tuning rule are fixed; it would have to match the walk's quadratic term direction by direction to 10⁻³–10⁻⁵ for protons at 10²⁰ eV (if seen) to survive. A primary proton above the Cherenkov threshold E_th = mc²/√(2s) bounds s_p < 4 × 10⁻²³ at 10²⁰ eV (Coleman and Glashow 1999). The Pierre Auger Observatory finds the composition heavier and purer up to about 10²⁰ eV (model-dependent); standard propagation also predicts few protons there, so the data could falsify CO's eventual number but not confirm it. **Grade: silence, with a test waiting** (light's lattice form and the tuning rule).

**Light's lattice form decides it** (MATTER record, 14 Nov). Composites bend with c₂ near zero, so light must bend no more than they do: two-mode light (bending c₂/4) is likely excluded by the highest-energy cosmic rays (protons and nuclei outrun it), and a separate link field of the standard form is excluded. Light running straight (c₂ = 0 in every direction, an improved link action) passes, and gives one number: photons split into an electron pair above E_th = (5.4 to 6.1)√(mₑc²·ħc/ℓ), 8–9 PeV at the Crab bound on ℓ and 2.3–2.6 × 10¹⁷ eV at the smallest ℓ. Photons are seen to 2.5 PeV (LHAASO). **Candidate test, if light runs straight:** a photon above about 9 PeV bounds ℓ below the Crab bound; one above about 3 × 10¹⁷ eV excludes it.

## 3. No entanglement through gravity alone

Amounts are classical, so gravity cannot entangle two masses (the falsifier; shared with semiclassical gravity). Gravity-mediated entanglement experiments (the Bose and Marletto–Vedral proposals) test it directly. **Grade: candidate** (shared with other classical-gravity models; consistent with Page and Geilker 1981). CO's own refinement, that gravity's source follows a body's engagements rather than its unresolved state, separates it from mean-field (Schrödinger–Newton) gravity only for unengaged superpositions; a number for a concrete set-up is owed.

## 4. A stopping number for measurement

N is a constant set only by experiment (N ≳ 50 under the stated readings). The prediction is a form: as the coupling to a channel falls, the breadth of loss should stop shrinking once the chain needed to reach N quanta stops forming. **Grade: shape** (the value free; the observable to plateau needs naming).

## Not predictions (agreement only, or below every test)

Weak-field gravity on the walk (G5–G8, G12); the composite excess at MICROSCOPE's level (B1); the extra species (not particles); the first ripples (a silence).

**Reading.** The lattice makes the highest-energy cosmic rays CO's natural test, but the number waits on two choices: light's lattice form and what the counter-terms equalise. The nearest-term CO-specific number may be classical gravity's decoherence-or-diffusion trade-off (Oppenheim et al. 2023), applied to gravity sourced by engagements.
