## Reading

The largest hollow is A, under twelve of twenty-one items. A is not a lack of technique: lattice field theory does loops routinely. It is that CO's lattice is physical, so loops on it split species' speeds at α/π against bounds near 10⁻²⁰. The frame problem is A's core, and it sits under gravity's nonlinear rule, every loop effect, colour's dynamics and the nucleon's mass. B is second (six), and it is today's: colour, the weak form and the charges all wait on it. C and H ride with B on the weak interaction.

By the frame note's exits table, every exit from A has been read except two: a real-time symmetry mixing the tick with the hops (unexplored), and a common origin for walk and link (shape: emergent gauge fields inherit the light cone of the fermions that make them, Volovik). Today's parton poke for colour is a common-origin move. It makes a gauge field (the gluons) out of matter's parts, at each place. So the second hollow (B) and the one open corner of the first (A's common-origin exit) point at the same move: gauge fields made from the walk rather than carried beside it.

**Where work should go, by the shape.** The common-origin exit, now with a concrete instance: if the gauge fields are made from the walk's parts, do they inherit the walk's light cone, so that walk and link speeds cannot split? What remains is the split among fermion species of different mass. That one question would chip A and B together.

## Update: the common-origin move, tested (same day)

Closed (frame note, "The common-origin exit, tested"). Fields made from the walk's parts get their cost from loops fed up to the hop scale, where the walk is not Lorentz invariant, so their speed splits from matter's at about 1/ln(1/(mℓ)) ≈ 1/40. Also, the fields CO needs must carry their own cost to be weak at the hop scale. So the edge shared by A and B is not a way through A. A's exits are now: tuning (permitted, not accepted), or a real-time symmetry that mixes the tick with the hops, the one unexplored entry. The map's largest hollow narrows to that single question.
