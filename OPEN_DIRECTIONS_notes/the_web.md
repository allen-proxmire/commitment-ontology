# The web behind the lattice

The lattice is a stand-in. What CO has is a web of relations; the cubic grid is the simplest web to compute on. The picture held is a grid drawn by hand, wiggly, not graph paper and not a crystal; perhaps set more evenly where things commit.

**No drawing.** With no backdrop a place has no position apart from the web: the web's geometry is its hop strengths, and a drawing adds nothing a pattern can read. Even hops with the grid's neighbours, on any drawing, are graph paper. So the question is only how the strengths vary. Varying smoothly over several hops is gravity's kind: not felt as roughness (run c15i), and bending patterns only as gravity does (candidate; not run here). Varying independently from hop to hop is felt, and held to the limits in the table below. The hand-drawn grid, in these terms, is a web whose strengths drift gently, not one that jitters from hop to hop.

**The candidate form.** The relations carry the lengths. A place's size along a line is the mean of the two relations that meet at it, V = (a_left + a_right)/2; a hop has the strength 1/√(VV′) of its two ends (for lengths varying smoothly, the same as ½{1/a, ·} at the places); the lifting term is built from the same lengths; and whether the web is flat or curved is read from the lengths alone. Checked in two dimensions on flat webs: in this form, lines relabelled alone with hop-to-hop unevenness (c15n, c15p); in the step's own form ½{1/a, ·} with lengths at the places, a fan and a smooth map (c15q). On a curved web (a mass's weak field in the lengths and the clock) a pattern bends as gravity bends light, with the grid's own excess on top: about 1 + 0.9(kℓ)², 8–9% at k = 0.3 and nothing at the wavelengths of real particles (c15v, c15s2). Where the lines are not at right angles, a diagonal per square gives the angle and the hop along a line feeds both spin matrices (c15t, one flat web). The lifting term carries a length, and with no drawing that is the relation's own (below). In three dimensions: one flat web (c15u2). Not yet: a curved web with shear, strong fields on this form, hop-to-hop unevenness in three dimensions.

Readings so far (run C15, RECORD_OF_METHOD/MATTER_runs/C15_wiggly_grid; two dimensions, massless pattern; r = ½ in c15a–d, plain hops with r = 0 in c15e–k).

## Flat or curved, from the strengths alone (c15l)

Matter's step with hop lengths a_x, a_y at each place (strength 1/length), H = Σ_i (1/a_i) σ_i sin k_i, has E² = Σ_i (k_i/a_i)²: its hops along the two lines are at right angles (by the form of the step, not derived), so the strengths are a geometry, ds² = a_x²dx² + a_y²dy², with Gauss's curvature

K = −(1/(a_x a_y)) [ ∂_x(∂_x a_y / a_x) + ∂_y(∂_y a_x / a_y) ],

taken with differences one hop wide. No positions enter. A web is flat where K = 0.

| strengths | variation of the lengths | curvature, rms per hop² |
|---|---|---|
| flat, redrawn by a smooth map | 0.029 | 5 × 10⁻¹² |
| flat, redrawn as a fan | 0.029 | 10⁻¹⁶ |
| curved, a bowl | 0.011 | 9 × 10⁻⁵ |
| curved, a ripple 16 hops long | 0.050 | 1.6 × 10⁻² |
| independent from place to place, 0.1 | 0.100 | 0.54 |
| uneven hop to hop by 0.1, but along x depending on x only and along y on y only (a relabelling of the lines) | 0.095 | 0 |

The lengths of the grid's own links alone, with no angles given, can be drawn flat, for small variations and locally (d numbers per place against d coordinates). The angles carry the rest, and matter's step sets them at right angles; shear between the lines needs the further carriers (the squares' diagonals). In three dimensions the same test is the Riemann curvature of ds² = Σ a_i²dx_i². Unevenness ε from place to place that is not a relabelling is curvature of order ε per hop². The last row is flat by this test and still felt in a run, less than independent unevenness of the same size (loss 0.014 against 0.020 at k = 0.3, 0.045 against 0.168 at k = 0.6; c15m): with numbers drawn independently for each place, flat by the test is not yet unfelt.

*A place's size from its relations (c15n, c15n2).* Give each link a length a and each place a size along a line V = (a_left + a_right)/2, the mean of the two links that meet at it. For the same flat, hop-to-hop uneven web (0.1), plain hops, the loss rate is 1.0 × 10⁻⁵, 7 × 10⁻⁵, 7.7 × 10⁻⁴ per hop time at k = 0.15, 0.3, 0.6, whether the hop's strength is written 1/√(VV′) over a link or 1/V at a place. With sizes drawn independently per place, in either form, it is 1–3 × 10⁻⁴, 1.3–1.7 × 10⁻³, 1.6–3.2 × 10⁻³: about 10 to 20 times higher at the longer wavelengths, 2 to 4 at the shortest. So where the strengths are written does not matter; what matters is that a place's size comes from the relations meeting at it, so that neighbours share a link and cannot differ freely. Candidate for the step's form on an uneven web; one seed, plain hops (their doubled states take part of every loss), the lifting term not included.

*The lifting term (c15p).* On the same web with r = ½, the lifting term built from the lengths is −(r/2)σ_z times the lines' own second difference, [(ψ(x+1) − ψ(x))/a_right + (ψ(x−1) − ψ(x))/a_left]/V. Over 150 hop times the forward share then falls by 1.2 × 10⁻⁴, 2.4 × 10⁻⁴, 3.7 × 10⁻³ at k = 0.15, 0.3, 0.6: one early drop, with no trend resolved after it at the two longer wavelengths. The plain hops on the same web and measure fall by 1.4 × 10⁻³, 1.1 × 10⁻², 0.11 (their doubled states take the rest). The even grid's lifting term added unchanged falls by 8 × 10⁻⁴, 1.0 × 10⁻³, 1.9 × 10⁻³: about 7 and 4 times more at the longer wavelengths, less at the shortest. So with sizes from the relations and the lifting term, a flat uneven web is close to unfelt at the two longer wavelengths. Reading; one seed, two dimensions, a web flat by relabelling its lines only.

*Webs flat in a less simple way (c15q).* A fan (a_x = 1, a_y = 1 + εx/L, the lengths varying by 25% either side across the web) and a smooth map (a = |1 + εz/L|), r = ½, the step ½{1/a_i, ·}. A packet's heading in the web's own labels turns by 8.3° and 5.1° over about 76 hops; in the true positions the lengths describe, its path is straight to 0.10 and 0.18 hop over 95 hops (even grid: 0.03), at speeds within 0.3% and 0.4% of the even grid's. Those leftovers are the size of the grid's own dispersion at k = 0.5 (its speed varies by about 1% with the angle to the lines and with the hop's length), so the turning is in the labels to within that; a longer wavelength was not run. Loss and spreading are not measured by a path. Reading; one launch each.

*A curved web (c15s).* A mass's weak field, softened, in a plane through it: hop length a = 1 + κ/ρ, clock rate N = 1 − κ/ρ (κ = r_s/2 = 1.5 hops, ρ² = r² + 5²), the step ½{N/a, H_W}, a massless packet (k = 0.3, 12 hops wide) passing at 40 hops. Its heading turns by 4.90° with the lengths alone, 5.52° with the clock alone, 10.87° with both. A packet that narrow spreads by 0.14 rad, so the answer to check against is a bundle of rays with the packet's own spread, traced in the index n = a/N: 4.47°, 5.05°, 10.20°. Pattern over bundle: 1.10, 1.09, 1.07 in a box 320 hops wide, where the spreading packet's tail wraps round the edge. In a box 640 wide (c15s2), with the rays run at the pattern's own speed: 4.425° against 4.44°, 4.996° against 5.01°, 9.962° against 10.11° (rays at the group speed, 0.964; the bundle's own scatter is 0.15%): within 1.5% of the rays. The step's own rays (H = w E_W(k), with the grid's dependence on direction) bend 6–7% more than light's here (c15s_rays3), and the pattern sat on light's. That agreement is a cancellation: in a smooth field with no core (w = 1 + 0.036 sin(2πy/256), c15v) the pattern turns by 5.73°, the step's rays by 5.66° and light's by 5.24°, so the pattern follows the step's own rays to 1.2%, 9% above light's; near the soft core, narrower than a wavelength, the wave bends less than any ray. The excess is the grid's: phase speed over group speed, 1 + 0.27k², times the grid's direction dependence, about 1 + 0.67k². The lengths and the clock each give about half, together the whole. Reading; one launch, weak field, two dimensions.

*Shear between the lines (c15t).* A web's data per square: the two link lengths and one diagonal d, which gives the angle between the lines, d² = a_x² + a_y² + 2a_xa_y cos θ. The step: H = Σ_i ½{E^i·σ, sin k_i} plus the lifting term, with E^x = (1, −cot θ)/a_x and E^y = (0, 1/sin θ)/a_y. Test web, flat: lines along y leaning more and more (X = x + εy²/2L, Y = y; angle from 68° to 112°). Gauss's curvature from lengths and diagonal (Brioschi's formula) is zero for it, and 7 × 10⁻⁴ and 9 × 10⁻⁴ per hop² for webs curved by a length or by the angle alone. A packet's true path is straight to 0.05 hop over 97 hops with the angle used. With the angle ignored, the path drawn in the leaning web's positions turns by 7.9° and leaves the straight line by 1.2 hops, about what the map alone gives: the same link lengths with right angles are another flat web, and in that web's own positions the path is straight to 0.04 hop (c15t2; in bare labels it departs by about 0.09, so this web tells the two apart only weakly). The pattern follows the web the step is given. So the diagonal is data the links' lengths do not hold. Not probed: this web's squares all have area 1; the lifting term ignores the angle; the diagonal was computed from the map, not given. Reading; one web, one launch, two dimensions.

*Three dimensions (c15u2).* A flat web with all three lines' lengths varying (a ball's own lines, far from its centre: a = (1, r/R, r sin θ/R)), four components, r = ½, the step ½{1/a_i, ·}, k = 0.6. Over 24 hops the heading in the web's labels turns by 8.4° (even grid: 0.2°), while the true path is straight to 0.06 hop (even grid: 0.01), at a speed 1.2% below the even grid's. The form carries over to three dimensions on this flat web. Reading; one launch, a short path in a small box (a longer first run, c15u, wrapped round the box and showed nothing).

*Which length the lifting term carries (c15r).* The term is r times a length times a second difference. c15p's form used one web-wide length (the mean hop, held at 1) with the true second difference, which is zero on patterns linear in position; with that length held at 1, an even web of hop length a gives r/a². With no drawing the only lengths are the relations' own: weighting each link's difference by that link's length gives −(r/2)σ_z[(ψ(x+1) − ψ(x)) + (ψ(x−1) − ψ(x))]/V, which goes as r/a, the step's own ½{1/a, r(1 − cos k)}, and leaves a first-order term where neighbouring links differ. On c15p's web the forward share then falls by 2.0 × 10⁻⁴, 9 × 10⁻⁴, 1.1 × 10⁻² over 150 hop times at k = 0.15, 0.3, 0.6, against 1.2 × 10⁻⁴, 2.4 × 10⁻⁴, 3.7 × 10⁻³: 2 to 4 times more, still small. Candidate: the relation's own length; the other form is set aside as needing one web-wide length, not each relation's own.

*Three dimensions (c15o, c15o2).* With ds² = Σ a_i²dx_i² the web is flat exactly where six expressions vanish (Lamé's equations), for (i, j, k) a turn of (1, 2, 3):

S_k = ∂_i(∂_i a_j / a_i) + ∂_j(∂_j a_i / a_j) + (∂_k a_i)(∂_k a_j)/a_k²,  M_i = ∂_j∂_k a_i − (∂_j a_k)(∂_k a_i)/a_k − (∂_k a_j)(∂_j a_i)/a_j.

Every difference one hop wide (c15o2). Largest of the six, rms per hop²: a fan 10⁻¹⁶; a ball's own lines 5 × 10⁻¹¹; space turned inside out about a far point 10⁻⁸; each line relabelled alone, uneven by 0.1 hop to hop, 0 (zero term by term, with no dependence across lines); a bowl 4 × 10⁻⁴; a ripple 16 hops long 10⁻²; lines relabelled alone and strictly alternating, 0; independent unevenness of 0.1, 0.45 with one number per place and 0.35 with one per place per line; a chequer of 0.1, 0.8.

Grade: reading (space only: a massless pattern reads a_i/N, and the clock's rate N is not included).

## Wiggles drawn and felt

*A wiggle in the drawing.* Places displaced and relations longer or shorter, with each hop's strength following the local geometry: its relation's length and direction and the size of the cells around it (length and direction alone are not enough beyond one dimension). To a long-wavelength pattern this should be the even grid in other coordinates, with scattering entering only at a higher order. Run so far (c15f–h, two dimensions, places displaced independently by 0.15 hops):
- With the grid's four neighbours no weights of this form (a size per cell, a vector per link) make patterns follow the true positions: a place has six conditions to meet (a constant pattern has no gradient; a pattern linear in the true positions has its true gradient) and five weights to meet them with. The best weights leave a first-order leftover, 0.08k on a plane wave, and the pattern scatters (power of k 3.6 to 3.9 in the runs).
- With eight neighbours (the diagonals linked too) weights meeting the conditions exactly exist, and the leftover on a plane wave falls to second order, 0.17k².
- The loss itself was not lower with them at k = 0.33 to 0.79. Cause open (candidates: the two leftovers are of one size there; scattering into the naive hops' doubled states, which first-order exactness does not suppress). Not pursued: these runs ask whether patterns can follow drawn positions, and with no drawing the question falls away.
- A wiggle alike over several hops, on the bare grid: with the displacement smooth over 2 hops the loss falls about 90-fold against an independent one of the same size (4 × 10⁻⁶ against 3.5 × 10⁻⁴ per hop time at k = 0.33; neighbour-to-neighbour difference 0.05 against 0.21, which alone would give 16-fold); smooth over 4 hops and four times larger, still about 10-fold lower (difference 0.105; 4-fold from that alone). One seed; a floor near 5 × 10⁻⁶ is not resolved (c15i). These wiggles are flat (a redrawing), so the run shows smooth variation is not felt as roughness; bending by a curved one is not tested here.

*A wiggle the patterns feel.* Hop strengths uneven from place to place by a fraction ε, with nothing following. This scatters as Rayleigh's law: in the run the loss rate is ½ε²k³ per hop time to about 20% (powers 3.09 in k and 2.04 in ε, against 3 and 2); in three dimensions the mean free path is 2π/(ε²k⁴ℓ³), to a factor of order one. For far-travelled patterns to arrive:

| | ℓ = 5.7 × 10⁻³⁵ m | ℓ = 1.0 × 10⁻³² m | ℓ = 2.4 × 10⁻³¹ m |
|---|---|---|---|
| 290 TeV neutrino over 1.3 Gpc (TXS 0506+056; association at about 3σ) | ε < 4 × 10⁻⁴ | ε < 2 × 10⁻⁷ | ε < 2 × 10⁻⁹ |
| 3.08 PeV photon over 9 kpc (Cygnus X-3), if light's carriers feel the unevenness as matter's hops do | ε < 1 × 10⁻³ | ε < 6 × 10⁻⁷ | ε < 5 × 10⁻⁹ |

The limits take the unevenness as independent from place to place; if it runs alike over ξ places they tighten by ξ^{3/2}. So the web may wiggle freely in its drawing, but hop for hop its strengths must follow its geometry to between a part in a thousand and a part in a billion. A web with no grid at all is allowed on the same terms only: hop strengths built from its geometry (as the weights on the random lattices of Christ, Friedberg and Lee are). P7f already says a hop's strength follows the geometry for gravity's smooth wiggles (N, ψ, b); the reading extends that need down to the single hop. Grade: reading.

## Seams

Two grids turned by 45° against each other, meeting along a line. Head-on, with only every other row linked across, about 3% or more of a packet is turned back at long wavelength for every link strength tried (six values, 0.7 to 2.0), rising to 57% at k = 0.8. With every row linked and the two strengths chosen (a coarse scan, its best value at the scan's edge), 0.2% is turned back at long wavelength, rising to 2% at k = 0.8. A seam can be made nearly clean by the strengths of its relations; a crude one cannot. At long wavelength half of a beam is lost after about 23 crude seams or 350 of the better ones. Limits: the turned grid here also has half the places, so the turn and the change of density are not separated. Grade: reading.

## What a rule for relations would have to give

- Each relation's hop strength following its length and direction, to the accuracy above.
- Which relations a newborn place has, and how places change neighbours where they slip or shear: silence.
- Whether the web is set more evenly where things commit: a local rule can do it, slowly, where a place has relations enough (run c15k: with eight neighbours the leftover falls round by round, about as n^−0.4; with four it sticks; the rule there steers toward drawn positions, so it is a stand-in). Commitments look too few to reach the voids: 7 × 10⁻⁶ per place over the universe's age, 4 to 5 orders short, counting nucleons at today's void density with each renewal reaching a nucleon's own size. That flips if a free nucleon's pattern spans its whole wave packet (about 10⁷ per place), and leaves out dark patterns, relic neutrinos and the denser past. If it holds, evenness to the table's limits has to come with a place's birth, since the far-travelled neutrino crossed voids. Candidate.
