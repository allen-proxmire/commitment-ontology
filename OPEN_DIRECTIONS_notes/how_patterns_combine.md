# How patterns combine: the gap under the ladder tops

**The gap.** CO's rules are written for one pattern walking and one commitment at a time. Almost every wall past 1930 needs a rule for several patterns sharing routes, with their number changing: the sea and pair creation; quantised light; the exchange sign (Pauli exclusion); gluons and the nucleon's source; how fields draw inside matter (pressure); loops. It is one gap seen from six sides.

**The nucleus so far (reading; sections at the end).** Mass is renewal rate, and nearly all of a nucleon's is transit, confined. Four steps on the 1D step: (1) a pattern with no mass held between walls ticks, redshifts and weighs as a mass does (run C10); (2) held walls get the inertia wrong, 1.99 E₀ for a weight of E₀, the old box-of-light problem: what confines has to be dynamical; (3) with the which-way field doing the confining (the Schwinger model), charges with no bare mass leave one neutral pattern of mass 0.68g (Schwinger's 0.564g; 21% above), its levels those of a free pattern at light's speed (run C16); (4) a whole charge cannot be pulled out alone: with heavy charges the field's string holds, then breaks into two neutral things, while a held half charge, which whole charges cannot match, keeps its string so long as the charges have mass (runs C16d, C16e). All in one space dimension, with a U(1) field standing in for colour; three dimensions and the three-state colour field are not touched.

**Three parts of it.**
1. *Binding (kinships).* How patterns form a composite that acts as one: atoms, nuclei, a crystal. The picture: a bound composite is a weave of kinships, and a stray quantum that matches the weave's channel rings the whole weave at once, like a bell, or like jumping on a made bed. Size sets how many stray quanta reach a weave; stiffness keeps each chain short. This touches measurement (the stopping number N) as much as binding.
2. *Identity (the exchange sign).* Two patterns of the same kind: why two electrons will not share a state. The smallest test is two electrons. Helium's ground state (−79.0 eV, reached with the two-pattern ruling) is symmetric in space and does not test the sign; helium's excited states do: the triplet (orthohelium) and singlet (parahelium) differ by the exchange energy, and the triplet exists only because the sign is negative. Reproducing that splitting would test whatever CO supplies for the sign.
3. *Number change.* Patterns made and unmade: pairs, photons emitted and absorbed. Light's number changes once the which-way field is quantised (capped link, shape); patterns' number: candidate (C6: the walk as a field with filled negative states, pairs made from light).

**What 1930 did.** The same wall, met by physics in its own order: Jordan–Klein and Jordan–Wigner (1927–28) quantised fields so particles became their excitations, with fermions by anticommutators; Heisenberg and Pauli (1929) built quantum electrodynamics; Dirac's hole theory (1930–31) and the positron (1932) made number change real; Fermi (1933–34) created and destroyed particles in beta decay; renormalisation (1947–49) tamed the loops. The move was to make the field primary and particles its excitations. CO's counterpart would be a rule for possibility in transit as something whose excitations are patterns, with number free and the exchange sign fixed. CO has a field-like thing already, the stock of possibility, but rules it classical.

**The direction of the move.** 1930 made the field primary and particles its excitations. CO's instinct runs the other way: a relation carries what is set by what passes (the branched-field ruling), so fields are made by patterns. The capped light field takes the 1930 direction for light (quanta as excitations of what relations carry). A rule for combining patterns has to choose a direction for patterns too, or reconcile the two. Number change (C6) settles it for matter, read both ways (matter record, 2026-11-11): a sea of actual patterns (Dirac's hole theory) is excluded, since newborn places, born with no commitments, would arrive either with empty negative states (antimatter everywhere as space grows) or with filled ones (patterns started other than in pairs, against C7); read as a field, the walk's vacuum is a state newborn places join, and patterns are its excitations, compatible with CO's definition of a pattern (possibility in transit, a wave). So CO is field-first for possibility in transit, matter and light, and pattern-set only for the Coulomb part of the which-way field, the standard division in Coulomb gauge.

**Permissive or selective.** A state space with number free (Fock space) allows; it does not pick. What picks, in standard physics, is a consistency condition: locality, Lorentz invariance and positive energy together force the exchange sign (the spin–statistics theorem). On CO's lattice locality alone selects nothing, and no lattice spin–statistics theorem exists. So the combining rule, posited, would be permissive; it becomes selective only if CO finds a consistency condition that forces the sign.

**What pivots together.**
- *Combining → loops → the frame.* As soon as patterns combine with number change, loops exist, and the lattice's frame gives species-dependent speeds at O(α/π). Climbing this ladder triggers the frame problem.
- *Amounts classical →* the falsifier, semiclassical gravity, harmless dents, Way B blocked.
- *No infinities →* a bounded degree, the frame, the capped light field, a finite self-energy.

**Clues, and the absence of clues.**
- In CO's favour: the hop is physical and data fix it (step order on the earlier walk; ℓ ≲ 2.4 × 10⁻³¹ m from photons, candidate 1.0 × 10⁻³² m from atmospheric neutrinos); the Aharonov–Bohm effect shows the phase on a path is physical where the field is zero, which the phase ruling (the which-way quantity a U(1) phase) requires and a real ratio could not give; the capped field's lineage (Born–Infeld, for a finite self-energy); kinship with Smolin's real-time programme and energetic causal sets.
- The absence: CO has produced integers and fixed values (three dimensions as a consistency condition; spin ½ and g = 2 through the Dirac walk; two polarisations), but no continuous dimensionless number from its primitives (no mass ratio, no 1/137, no reason for three colours). Each step up has needed standard physics' structure (Wald and Boulanger–Damour–Gualtieri–Henneaux, the Dirac walk, Kogut–Susskind). The primitives host structure more than they pick it.

**Can the primitives produce numbers?** The test is whether CO has anything that selects among possibilities, not only things that allow them. Numbers come from selection. Elsewhere: symmetry and consistency give integers (g = 2, fractional quark charges from anomaly cancellation, CO's three dimensions); dynamics with one scale gives ratios (lattice QCD's hadron mass ratios from one input scale); topology gives integers (windings, charges); no one derives 1/137. CO's best number-maker so far is a consistency condition (three dimensions from annealing). The way to strengthen the primitives is more constraints of that kind, not more posits that assert. The place to try first: a consistency condition fixing P_max/ħ, P∞/ħ or δ. One candidate is already in the records: the annealed degree in three dimensions (about 4–6, R7b) with degree ≈ P∞/ħ + 1 (R5) would put P∞ at a few ħ; whether that holds as a ruling, and what it then fixes, is unread. A continuous dimensionless number from the primitives would be the clearest sign they pick structure rather than host it.

**Where to start.** Two electrons: the smallest place the exchange sign shows (orthohelium against parahelium), with no number change and no loops, so no frame problem; read, the sign is now a candidate (P8b); orthohelium against parahelium would test it. For binding without the sign: positronium, two elementary patterns of different kinds. Its gross structure (−6.80 eV/n²) follows from the two-pattern ruling by identification; of its hyperfine split, the magnetic 4/7 is in reach and the annihilation 3/7 needs number change, as do its lifetimes. The line between gross binding and number change runs through one atom.

**Related.** Condensed matter is the nearest laboratory for relativity from a substrate: graphene's electrons are a Dirac walk on a lattice with their own limiting speed; Volovik, *The Universe in a Helium Droplet* (2003), develops emergent relativity and gravity-like effects from a quantum liquid and meets the same frame problem.

**Interaction as falling into step (sketch).** The picture: a star's commitments pulse in step, and patterns interact through what they can fall into step with. Two parts, graded separately.
- *Same rate (candidate, with the free-share rule).* Every commitment at a place runs at its own ω₀ times the same 1 − h, so all slow by the same fraction; a level difference (E_f − E_i)/ħ slows with them, so a resonance between patterns at one place survives. Same rate is not same phase.
- *Same beat (candidate, by identification; classical drive).* A bound pattern's states beat at E_k/ħ. With the walk (Dirac) and the which-way phase (minimal coupling), a classical drive at ω (the large-occupancy limit of the branched which-way field) moves amplitude from state i to state f with probability

$$P_{i\to f}(t)=\frac{\Omega^{2}}{\Omega^{2}+\Delta^{2}}\sin^{2}\!\left(\tfrac12\sqrt{\Omega^{2}+\Delta^{2}}\,t\right),\qquad \Delta=\omega-\frac{E_f-E_i}{\hbar},$$

with Ω = |⟨f|d·E₀|i⟩|/ħ, starting in i, two levels only (others far off step), rotating-wave (Ω, |Δ| ≪ ω), no decay. Off step (|Δ| ≫ Ω) the transfer stays small (peak Ω²/(Ω² + Δ²)); in step it is complete. Transfer also needs a nonzero matrix element (selection rules). So "interact through what you can fall into step with" is resonance: a drive's beat must match the difference between two of the pattern's beats, within a width.
- *Between two bound patterns.* Closer than about a wavelength, the bound part of the which-way field (Coulomb plus Breit) couples |e g⟩ to |g e⟩, and the transfer is resonant in the mismatch of their level differences (candidate, by identification; it rests on the two-pattern ruling carried to more patterns). At larger separations the transfer goes through the emitted part, which is number change.
- *Direction (candidate, by identification; classical drive).* A drive travelling along k carries the spatial phase e^{i(k·x − ωt)} and couples to each constituent at its own place, e^{ik·x_j}. With the two-pattern ruling the bound pattern is the same at every place, so the pattern's total lattice momentum obeys

$$\mathbf P_f=\mathbf P_i+\hbar\mathbf k\pmod{2\pi\hbar/\ell},$$

the recoil of absorption; a fixed external source gives no recoil. A jump by a whole step 2πħ/ℓ needs about 10¹⁹ eV, the zone edge where the Wilson term puts the doublers, so energy matching excludes it; ħk is a fraction kℓ/2π ≲ 10⁻²⁵ of that step. The cubic lattice breaks rotation, so angular-momentum selection rules hold up to corrections of order (pℓ/ħ)² ≈ 10⁻⁴¹ (the binding momentum, dominant) and (kℓ)² ≈ 10⁻⁴⁸. The Wilson term's O(mℓc/ħ) ≈ 10⁻¹⁹ shifts levels but is rotation-invariant at leading order.
- *What it bears on.* For a classical drive and a translation-invariant engager, the walk and the phase give C8's frequency and direction matching, within a width (Ω, or the linewidth); a sharp cutoff does not follow, and C8 reads a channel as a final state with its width, engagement following its line shape (chosen, candidate). What stays posited in C8: that a passage can be an engagement, which passages are, single-quantum exchange (number change, priced and held), and the Lüders update; Born's chance is identified, the pointer basis imported.


**Sketch, ungraded (2026-11-11): three kinds of pattern, and a three-fold one.** The picture: three kinds of pattern, the single one (the electron), the three-fold one (quarks) and the channel-less one (dark). The three-fold pattern renews only as a set of three tiles: one tile alone is a design, not real on its own; two have some realness but do not complete a pattern; three complete it; inside, the parts keep switching roles. The known facts it meets: quarks are never seen alone (confinement); every quark–antiquark particle decays; the lightest three-quark particle, the proton, is stable (the free neutron decays, stable only in nuclei); and nucleons trade two-quark pions, turning protons into neutrons and back (Yukawa's nuclear force). What would make it CO's own and not a relabelling of QCD is a reason for three. Physics gets three from colour; the standing lesson forbids putting a three-valued label on a tile and calling it colour. CO's version runs the other way: three as a completion count, colour as the bookkeeping of which tile is which.

*A first check of "three from the three axes".* The walk splits each step into one part per axis. Suppose a tile is a two-component walker that moves along one axis only. Moving in all three directions then needs three tiles. Their joint states have 2³ = 8 components, and with the tiles linked by sign strings (a Jordan–Wigner arrangement, a candidate meaning for "kinship"), they carry the full Dirac set: α_x = σ_x⊗1⊗1, α_y = σ_z⊗σ_x⊗1, α_z = σ_z⊗σ_z⊗σ_x and β = σ_z⊗σ_z⊗σ_z all anticommute (checked numerically). The operators commuting with all four form a 2 × 2 algebra, so the eight components are a spin-½ pattern (four) times an extra two-valued label. One tile moves on a line, two only in a plane, so three is the smallest number that moves in space. The extra two-valued label invites a reading as the proton–neutron pair (a doublet whose charge differs by one), and thirds of a pattern's charge shared over three tiles give quark-like charges. All of this is a shape: the tiles as one-axis walkers, the strings as kinship, and the doublet as the nucleon are identifications, and known mismatches remain (quarks are spin ½ each; the tiles here are not; mesons have spin 0 or 1).

*Second check: the two-way label.* The operators that commute with the whole Dirac set (α_x, α_y, α_z, β) are, in tile terms, τ₁ = 1⊗σ_x⊗σ_y, τ₂ = σ_x⊗σ_y⊗1 and τ₃ = σ_x⊗σ_z⊗σ_y (with the identity): a Pauli algebra, so the label is a true doublet with its own SU(2). Three things follow. (1) The walk built from α's and β (its hops, its mass and its Wilson term) commutes with τ, so the two versions are exactly degenerate on the lattice: an exact isospin symmetry. In nature proton and neutron differ by 0.14% in mass (938.27 and 939.57 MeV), a symmetry broken only by charge and the quark masses, so exact degeneracy at this level is the right first answer, and the splitting needs couplings CO has not built. (2) The operators that flip the label, τ₁ and τ₂, each act on a pair of tiles; the label itself, τ₃, acts on all three. In CO's terms, two-tile parts switch the three-tile pattern between its versions, which is the role the two-quark pion plays between proton and neutron. (3) Charge differing by one across the doublet, Q = (1 + τ₃)/2, is a choice here, not derived. Grade unchanged: shape.

*Third check, the one that breaks it: spin 3/2.* Three quarks, each of spin ½ and isospin ½, combine into spin ½ with isospin ½ (the nucleon doublet, 938–940 MeV) or spin 3/2 with isospin 3/2 (the Δ quartet, Δ⁺⁺, Δ⁺, Δ⁰, Δ⁻ near 1232 MeV, very well established). In the three-tile pattern as built, the rotation generators S_i = −(i/4)ε_ijk α_j α_k close correctly but give S² = 3/4 on all eight states, and the doublet's Casimir is 3/4 on all eight: spin ½ and isospin ½ only (checked numerically). There is no room for the Δ. So the identification, as built, reproduces the nucleon and misses its nearest relative. The break is located: here the tiles carry neither spin ½ nor isospin ½ of their own; only the whole does. Getting the Δ needs each tile to carry its own spin ½ and two-way label, which is the quark model's starting point and moves the sketch back toward relabelling QCD. What survives: three as the least number of one-axis tiles that moves in space, and the nucleon doublet with its pair-acting flips. Grade: shape, with a recorded mismatch.

*Fourth check, the repair: a spin ½ on each tile.* Give each one-axis tile its own spin ½ as well. The tile spins alone combine to ½ or 3/2, as the Δ needs, but the three-tile Dirac structure already carries a spin ½ of its own (any set of anticommuting α's does: their commutators generate rotations), so the whole is four spin-½ pieces and its total spin is 0, 1 or 2 (checked numerically): integer, the wrong kind for a baryon. The repair is excluded. Making the tiles full spin-½ walkers in three dimensions would work, but each tile then moves in space alone, and three is no longer the least number that moves: the reason for three from the axes is lost, and three would have to come from elsewhere (colour, as in QCD).

*Where the sketch closes.* Three one-axis tiles give the nucleon doublet, exactly degenerate and flipped by two-tile operators, with three as the least number that moves in space; they cannot give the Δ, and the one repair that would gives integer spin. So "three from the three axes" does not reach the baryons as they are. Kept as a shape that met the nucleon and failed at its first excited state, with the failure located. Reopened (2026-11-12): one failed repair does not close it.

*Poke 1: the Δ as three plus two, not as a completed pattern.* The Δ lives about 5 × 10⁻²⁴ s and always decays into a nucleon and a pion. In CO's terms that is a three-tile pattern with a two-tile one moving around it: "the 3 and 2 parts interact". Read that way, the Δ needs no spin on the tiles. The sketch already has the pieces: the nucleon doublet (spin ½, isospin ½, positive parity) and the two-tile flip operators τ₁, τ₂, which carry the doublet's label as a vector (isospin 1, like the pion). The combination with one unit of orbital motion (L = 1) gives
- spin: ½ ⊗ 1 = 3/2 ⊕ ½,
- isospin: ½ ⊗ 1 = 3/2 ⊕ ½,
- parity: (+)(−)(−1)^L = +,
so a state with J = 3/2, I = 3/2 and positive parity is available: the Δ's quantum numbers, four charge states included. This is the established picture of the Δ as a pion–nucleon resonance in a P wave (the static model of Chew and Low, 1956). What the sketch would need: the two-tile pattern to be a real, pseudoscalar (negative-parity, spin-0) pattern, and a reason the 3 + 2 configuration resonates near 1232 MeV.

*Poke 2: dimension locked to the label (Skyrme).* In Skyrme's picture of baryons, the pion field's three label directions are tied to the three directions of space (the "hedgehog"), and the nucleon and the Δ come out as two rotation states of one object, with spin equal to isospin: ½ and ½, then 3/2 and 3/2. Witten (1983) showed that this object is a fermion exactly when the number of quark colours is odd. Two things there match the instinct that quark behaviour is tied to dimension: the label locked to the three axes, and the count three mattering because it is odd, which makes the baryon a fermion. Not yet checked in CO's terms: whether the three-tile pattern, rotated as a whole with its label turned along with space, gives the J = I tower.

Grade: both pokes are shapes, with known physics behind them; neither is derived from CO's rules.

*The two-tile pattern, built.* Two one-axis tiles linked by the sign rule carry α_x = σ_x⊗1, α_y = σ_z⊗σ_x and β = σ_z⊗σ_z (checked): a pattern that moves only in a plane, with spin ±½ about the plane's normal and a two-valued (not three-valued) extra label. The pion moves in space, has spin 0 and a three-valued label. So the two-tile pattern on its own is not the pion: no.

*What survives of poke 1.* Inside the three-tile pattern, operators with the pion's quantum numbers exist: with G = iα_xα_yα_z (it commutes with the α's and anticommutes with β, the role of γ₅), the three operators Gτ_a are pseudoscalar and carry the label as a vector. That is the standard pion–nucleon coupling, ψ̄ iγ₅ τ_a ψ π_a, the field-theory form of the Chew–Low picture; so the Δ as nucleon plus pion in a P wave survives, but only with the pion as a field coupled to the nucleon, not as a two-tile pattern.

*A correction.* The earlier point that the label is flipped by two-tile operators is not robust: the tile count of an operator depends on the order in which the sign rule links the tiles. In the same arrangement τ₁ and τ₂ act on two tiles and τ₃ on three, while the pseudoscalar set Gτ_a acts on three, one and two (checked). That point is withdrawn.

So far: three as the least number of one-axis tiles moving in space; an exact doublet; the Δ only as nucleon plus a pion field, not from tiles; the two-tile pattern a planar spin-½ object, not a meson. Poke 2 (rotating the whole with its label tied to space) is untried.

*Poke 2: the label tied to space.* Within one three-tile pattern, tying the label to space means combining spin and label into one rotation, K = S + I. The three-tile states have K = 0 or 1 (checked): a K = 0 state is the pattern's "hedgehog", the label pointing along each direction of space. But one pattern of eight states cannot carry the tower spin = label = ½, 3/2, …: rotating it gives back only spin ½ and label ½, the same limit as the third check. Skyrme's tower needs a different kind of object: not a few tiles, but an extended twist in a three-valued field, with the field's label wound once around the three directions of space. Such twists exist because space has three dimensions and the label space (the unit 3-sphere of an SU(2) field) has three as well: the maps from one to the other fall into whole-number classes (π₃(S³) = ℤ), and the class is the baryon number. Turning the twist as a whole gives the nucleon (spin = label = ½) and the Δ (3/2, 3/2) as its first two rotation states (Adkins, Nappi and Witten 1983 fit their masses), and the twist is a fermion when quantised with the 2π rotation giving −1 (Finkelstein–Rubinstein; Witten 1983: when the number of colours is odd).

*Reading for CO.* This is the cleanest form of the instinct that quark behaviour is tied to dimension: a baryon as a twist that wraps three directions of space onto a three-dimensional label space, possible because both are three. The Δ comes for free, as rotation. What CO lacks: a three-valued field on the network (pion-like) and a reason its twists are stable (Skyrme's extra term), and the fermion condition, which in CO could come from P8b's frame transport (a 2π turn of the twist giving −1) rather than from a count of colours. Grade: shape, with established physics behind it; the tiles give way to a twist. The nucleus wall's "three" would then be the three dimensions twice over, space and label, not a count of parts.

*Piece 1: where a three-valued field could come from in CO.* The twist needs a field whose value at each place is a point on a 3-sphere (an SU(2) element). Four places to look, with verdicts:
1. *The spin frame at each place* (the frames P8b carries for the exchange sign). Exactly the right kind of value, and it would bring the fermion condition with it. But its three rotations are a gauge freedom in CO's gravity (the walk's spin connection; space and gravity record): a frame twist would be a relabelling, not a thing, and its windings would label vacua (as instantons do), not particles. Unusable unless frame rotations cost something, which would go against the gauge reading. No, as CO stands.
2. *The which-way field made three-valued* (an SU(2) phase in place of the U(1) phase). Its gauge transformations wind (the same π₃), but again as vacuum labels, not particles; and a three-valued which-way field is the weak interaction's form, which CO lacks. Not for baryons.
3. *The orientation of the walk field's vacuum in its label space.* With number change ruled (C6), the vacuum is a state of the walk field. If the field carries the two-valued label found in the three-tile check, and its vacuum picks a direction in the combined left- and right-handed label space, the slow turning of that direction from place to place is a field U(x) with values in SU(2): Goldstone's pions, and Skyrme's twists are its windings. This is the established route (spontaneous chiral symmetry breaking; pions as its Goldstone modes; baryons as Skyrmions). Needs, in CO: (a) the label as a property of the walk field everywhere, not only of a three-tile pattern; (b) a pairing between the walk field's left- and right-handed parts strong enough to make the vacuum pick a direction (in QCD, the strong interaction does it; Nambu–Jona-Lasinio models show four-fermion pairing suffices); (c) a lattice walk that keeps the left–right symmetry to break, against Nielsen–Ninomiya and the Wilson term, which breaks it by hand (Ginsparg–Wilson walks are the known escape).
4. *A new primitive field.* Excluded by method unless it cascades.

So the candidate is 3: the pion field as the vacuum's orientation, the baryon as its twist, three as dimension twice over, and the fermion sign from the 2π turn. Of its three needs, (c) is a known lattice problem with a known form of solution; (b) is the substantive one: what in CO pairs left and right strongly.

*Need 2: what in CO pairs left and right.* CO already has it. In the walk, mass is the flip F(θ_m) = exp(−iβθ_m), Feynman's checkerboard turn between the two movers on each step: a pairing of left- and right-handed parts, θ_m = c₀mcℓ/ħ per step, and the renewal rate is the mass (C1–C2). What it lacks is a direction. For a field carrying the two-valued label, the flip can turn the movers into each other along any direction in the label's space:

F(θ_m, U) = exp(−iθ_m β (P_R U + P_L U†)),  U = exp(iτ·π/f) ∈ SU(2),

with P_{R,L} the handedness projections and τ the label's generators. With U = 1 this is the present flip; the flip's strength, the renewal rate, fixes the mass, while its direction U is not fixed by any rule. Read as set place by place, U(x) is the three-valued field of piece 1, and the vacuum's choice of a uniform U is the spontaneous breaking: slow turnings of U are pions, and windings of U over the three directions of space are Skyrme's twists, the baryons. This is the chiral (sigma-model) form of a mass term, m ψ̄(σ + iγ₅τ·π)ψ with σ² + π² = 1, written as CO's flip.

So the candidate for needs 1 and 2 together: *the renewal's flip has a direction in the label's space, set place by place.* What it would give: pions as ripples of the flip's direction, nearly massless (their 140 MeV, small against the nucleon's 939 MeV, from a small fixed tilt, as quark masses do in QCD); nucleons getting their mass from the flip's strength; baryons as twists. What remains: (a) a cost for gradients of U, and Skyrme's extra term (or another reason) for twists to hold their size; (b) the Wilson term, which flips with a fixed direction and so breaks the symmetry by hand (need 3: a Ginsparg–Wilson walk); (c) why the label is two-valued (in nature: two light quark kinds). Grade: shape; a proposal that would need to be chosen before it is a posit.

*Need 3: can the walk keep the left–right symmetry and still have no doublers?* Three known routes, read against CO's walk, which is strictly local (each step reaches only neighbours; L1b):
1. *Exact lattice chiral symmetry (Ginsparg–Wilson, overlap).* Keeps a lattice-modified symmetry exactly, with no doublers, but no such operator can be strictly local: its reach falls off exponentially over a few spacings, never to zero (Horváth 1998; Hernández, Jansen and Lüscher 1999). For a walk, by analogy, that would mean leakage outside the light cone at the scale ℓ. Against L1b: no, as CO stands.
2. *Wilson's fixed-direction flip, then tuning.* Removes the doublers and breaks the symmetry by hand; the symmetry comes back only as kℓ → 0, and the mass must be tuned against an additive shift (lattice QCD's practice; discrete-time walks contain this term naturally, Arnault 2021, 2105.12314). Works, at the cost of a second tuning beside the frame problem's.
3. *Keep the doublers and count them as the label.* The doublers are not removed but carried as a flavour, and a symmetry with handedness survives exactly because nothing is cut away; Nielsen–Ninomiya is met, not evaded, since each flavour comes with both handednesses (Bakircioglu, Arnault and Arrighi 2505.07900, discrete time; Kogut and Susskind's staggered fermions, continuous time). In three space dimensions, staggered fermions in Hamiltonian form put one component on each corner of a 2×2×2 cell: eight components, which is exactly two four-component Dirac fields (Susskind 1977). (In four-dimensional Euclidean form the count is four; the two belongs to three space dimensions.)

*Reading for CO.* Route 3 meets the three-tile check from the other side. The three one-axis tiles linked by sign strings have eight states, and their α's carry the same sign pattern as the staggered phases (a sign for each axis already crossed); the exact two-valued label found there is then the lattice's own doubling, halved by the tiles, which is what staggered fermions call taste. That would answer need (c), why the label is two-valued: 2³ components per cell, four per Dirac field, leaves two. Nature has two light quark kinds; whether that is more than a coincidence of counts is not shown. Costs: the symmetry kept exactly is a remnant (the one-site shift; U(1)_ε in the Euclidean form) (in continuous time; for a strictly local discrete-time walk, Meyer's lemma removes it in the one-dimensional single-component case, Arnault et al. 1807.08303, so it is open for CO's walk), and the full left–right symmetry of the two kinds returns only as (kℓ)² → 0, as the walk's other lattice corrections do. Pions need it only approximately (they have mass), so that cost may be tolerable; with one exact remnant, one pion is an exact Goldstone mode and the other two are split by interactions. *Checked: term for term.* Kogut and Susskind's staggered Hamiltonian on a 2×2×2 cell (one component per corner, hopping (−i/2)η_j χ†_x χ_{x+j} + h.c. with sign η₁ = 1, η₂ = (−1)^{x₁}, η₃ = (−1)^{x₁+x₂}, mass sign (−1)^{x₁+x₂+x₃}, x₁ the first tile, p measured per site), has

dH/dp_j at p = 0 equal to α_x, α_y, α_z, and its mass matrix equal to mβ,

exactly the three-tile matrices, with the corner's three bits as the three tiles. The spectrum is exactly four-fold degenerate at every p. *Handedness, checked ([`handedness_check.py`](../RECORD_OF_METHOD/NUCLEUS_notes/handedness_check.py)).* Does a field acting on the kind label couple one-handedly, as Schmelzer found? Two findings.
- *Strictly local: no.* The operators acting inside one cell that commute with the step at every q are only the charge (the identity, both handednesses, both kinds alike) and, for the massless walk, complex conjugation. *The tail, found ([`staggered_label_twist.py`](../RECORD_OF_METHOD/NUCLEUS_notes/staggered_label_twist.py)).* The slow tail is not an artefact of the grid; it is a twist. ## The up–down charge difference (12 Nov, reading; shape)

*Where the difference lives.* In every pair nature has, the two members differ in charge by exactly one unit: up (+2/3) and down (−1/3), neutrino (0) and electron (−1). The standard account is

Q = T₃ + Y,

with T₃ = ±½ the kind label (weak isospin) and Y one shared number per pair (hypercharge). The unit difference is T₃ itself. So the difference is not a property of the two patterns separately: it is light coupling to the kind label, which is what the weak interaction gauges. Within CO, the up–down charge question and the weak interaction are one question. On the staggered walk it is blocked (the twist; C6 exact). On two separate walk fields with their own charges, the values are free to assign but not explained, and "exactly one unit in every pair" is a coincidence.

*What fixes the values: exact gauge invariance (poke).* A chiral gauge field, one that couples to the left-handed kind only as the weak interaction does, is consistent and exactly gauge-invariant only if its gauge anomalies cancel, in the continuum and on a lattice alike (on a lattice: Lüscher 1999 for U(1)). An anomalous one can be written down, but then gauge invariance fails and gauge modes become physical, unless something else cancels the anomaly (a Wess–Zumino term, or inflow from a fifth dimension). CO holds its gauge symmetries exact (C6 held exact today; no backdrop), so for CO anomaly cancellation is a condition for a consistent, exactly gauge-invariant chiral gauge field, not an extra demand. For one generation (left-handed Weyl fields Q, u^c, d^c, L, e^c, with N_c colours), the conditions are

SU(N_c)²·U(1): 2y_Q + y_u + y_d = 0;  SU(2)²·U(1): N_c y_Q + y_L = 0;  gravity²·U(1): 2y_L + y_e = 0 (given the first);  U(1)³: 2N_c y_Q³ + N_c(y_u³ + y_d³) + 2y_L³ + y_e³ = 0.

With the electron's charge as the unit (y_L = −½) they have one solution, up to swapping u and d (checked, [`charges_from_anomalies.py`](../RECORD_OF_METHOD/NUCLEUS_notes/charges_from_anomalies.py)):

Q_u = ½ + 1/(2N_c),  Q_d = −½ + 1/(2N_c),  Q_ν = 0,  Q_e = −1.

For N_c = 3: +2/3 and −1/3. For any N_c, the proton (with (N_c+1)/2 ups and (N_c−1)/2 downs) has charge exactly +1, equal and opposite to the electron: atoms are neutral for any colour count, which is tested to about 10⁻²¹ of the electron's charge (Bressi et al. 2011). The derivation: Geng and Marshak 1989; Babu and Mohapatra 1989; Bär and Wiese 2001 on the N_c dependence. By algebra: the SU(2) condition gives y_Q = 1/(2N_c); then y_u + y_d = −1/N_c, and the cubic leaves a quadratic whose two roots are y_u = −(½ + 1/(2N_c)), y_d = ½ − 1/(2N_c) and the swap. Witten's global anomaly needs N_c + 1 doublets to be even, so N_c is odd, the same condition under which Skyrme's twisted baryon is a fermion.

*Reading for CO.* The up–down charges are not a separate problem. They are fixed once CO has two things: the weak interaction as a chiral gauge field on its lattice, which then must be anomaly-free, and the colour count. The difference of one is the kind label; the offset 1/(2N_c) is colour's. Three walls fold into two: colour, and a chiral gauge field on the lattice. The second is a known hard problem of lattice physics. The best-established construction (Lüscher's: U(1) exactly, SU(2) × U(1) to all orders in perturbation theory, not yet beyond) uses Ginsparg–Wilson (overlap) fermions, which cannot be strictly local (Horváth 1998), only exponentially local. That is what the locality ruling (fast-fading tail) allows. Domain-wall fermions are also exponentially local in their effective four-dimensional operator. Strictly local alternatives (the Rome gauge-fixing approach; mirror fermions with symmetric mass generation, Eichten–Preskill, Wang–Wen) remain unproven. So the locality ruling has a use after all: it permits the best-established lattice route to a one-handed gauge field.

Costs and limits. With a right-handed neutrino the solution is unique only up to a B − L shift of the right-handed fields, which Yukawa couplings (or a Majorana mass for the right-handed neutrino) remove. The non-abelian lattice construction is established to all orders in perturbation theory, not beyond. Colour is still CO's wall: without it (N_c = 1) the rule gives up +1 and down 0, which is wrong. Grade: shape. Up and down as two kinds of the weak doublet, with charges forced by anomaly freedom on CO's lattice once colour and a chiral gauge field are in hand.

*Poke: colour from the corners of the cell (ungraded).* The naive walk (no Wilson term) in three dimensions has eight Dirac species, one at each corner of the momentum cube, and a corner is labelled by which axes are flipped to π. Eight Dirac fields is exactly one generation with a Dirac neutrino: u and d in three colours each, plus ν and e. Group the corners by how many axes are flipped. The chirality of each species flips sign with every flipped axis, (−1)^{flips}, since G = iα_xα_yα_z and each flipped axis reverses one α. The groups are then 1 + 3 + 3 + 1:

0 flips (one corner) | 1 flip (three) | 2 flips (three) | 3 flips (one),

with even counts of one chirality and odd counts of the other. Read with the even–odd sign as T₃, the triplets as colour (the triplet member named by an axis) and the single corners as leptons: ν (0 flips), d (1 flip), u (2 flips), e (3 flips). Then Y = Q − T₃ is 1/6 for both triplets and −½ for both single corners, which is (B − L)/2: the anomaly-forced charges above, with colour's three as the three axes of space. Precedents: Belyea (arXiv:1004.3009) identifies 16 doubled Weyl species with a generation, and Schmelzer's space-geometric reading of a generation as fields over ℝ³ (hep-lat/0311009). The obstacles are the ones found today. The cube's symmetry only permutes the three axis-corners, which is a discrete group, not SU(3). Gauging colour or T₃ across corners means gauging a label tied to momentum (the twist). A lattice model of the sixteen left-handed fields brings sixteen mirror fields with it unless they are gapped (Wang and Wen 2013, symmetric mass generation). The count fits exactly; whether the corners can carry gauge charges is the same open question as the kind label's. Standing lesson kept in view: a three-valued label is not colour until it carries an SU(3) gauge field.

*Can the corners carry gauge charges? (12 Nov, reading.)* The lattice literature's answer agrees with today's twist: no. A symmetry can be gauged on a lattice when it acts on-site, at each place by itself. Wang and Wen build gaugeable chiral models precisely by keeping every charge on-site (the 3-4-5-0 model; their SO(10) proposal), and DeMarco and Wen list on-site action as a condition for gauging. The doublers' labels (corners, tastes) are not on-site: they are tied to momentum, as the twist showed. So the corners can supply the *count* of a generation, but not its *charges*.

What does work is this:
1. Put the charges on internal labels at each place.
2. Build the theory together with its mirror (the opposite-handed copy that the lattice forces).
3. Gap the mirror by interactions that break no symmetry: symmetric mass generation (Eichten–Preskill's mirror idea, made precise by Wang–Wen and Catterall).

Two consequences for CO:
- *Colour and the weak label must be internal labels at each place*, not corners, tastes or directions of space. That is the nucleus wall restated: CO has no on-site three-valued label, and adding one is a new posit. It is worth making only if it pays elsewhere (the criterion for a third primitive). The standing lesson stands. A three-valued label on a place is not colour until it carries an SU(3) gauge field, but an on-site label is exactly the kind that *can* carry one; it is the label CO lacks, not the gauge field's possibility.
- *Sixteen per generation.* Symmetric mass generation is possible only when all anomalies cancel, including discrete ones. In 3+1 dimensions, with the discrete symmetry of SO(10)-type models kept, that needs a multiple of sixteen Weyl fields: Catterall's reduced staggered construction lands on exactly sixteen, Wang and Wen on the SO(10) 16. One generation of the Standard Model has fifteen without a right-handed neutrino. So if CO's one-handed fields come by this route, each generation carries a right-handed neutrino. This is not forced for the Standard Model group alone (Wang and Wen also propose a fifteen-field SU(5) route), so it is a consequence of the route, not of CO.

Grade: reading. The corner-colour poke stays ungraded: the count fits, but the charges cannot live on corners. Colour, the weak label and the charges all wait on one posit, an on-site internal label, and the mirror-gapping route (exponential locality not needed for it; on-site interactions instead).

## Baryons as twists, on the flow step (19 Nov, desk; shape)

*The twist, re-read.* Take two light flavours (u, d; as C9 takes them). With one handedness on each wall, the flavour symmetry of the light quarks is SU(2) on one wall times SU(2) on the other: the chiral symmetry of two-flavour QCD, in the form lattice QCD gives it with domain-wall quarks. CO's colour sector by identification (NUCLEUS) breaks it to one SU(2), isospin, the diagonal one (numerical, not proved, as for confinement): the quarks' wall-to-wall coupling condenses with a direction in flavour space,

$$\langle \bar q_{R}^{\,b}\, q_{L}^{\,a}\rangle \;\propto\; U^{ab}(x),\qquad U(x)\in SU(2)\cong S^3,$$

with q_L and q_R the two walls' quarks.

So the "flip's direction, set place by place" of 12 Nov is the direction of the wall-to-wall coupling in flavour space: its slow ripples are pions (light, 140 MeV against the nucleon's 939, from the small quark masses), and a winding of U over the three directions of space,

$$B=\frac{1}{24\pi^2}\int d^3x\;\varepsilon^{ijk}\,\mathrm{tr}\big(U^\dagger\partial_iU\,U^\dagger\partial_jU\,U^\dagger\partial_kU\big)=1,$$

is a baryon (Skyrme 1961). In the twist with B = 1, the hedgehog, the flavour direction points along the direction in space at each place: turning the pattern in space and turning it in flavour are the same turn. Its rotation states form one tower with spin equal to isospin, the nucleon (½, ½) then the Δ (3/2, 3/2), with energies M + J(J + 1)/(2𝓘); it is a fermion exactly when the number of colours is odd (Witten 1983), so C9's three makes it one.

*Where the two threes sit.* Colour's three is internal (C9; not space). The twist's three is space's three directions locked to the three directions of the flavour turn. A rotation of a three-dimensional pattern belongs here, in how three quarks bind into a baryon, not in colour.

*What it gives and does not.* With the twist's two numbers fitted to the nucleon and Δ masses, the Skyrme model gives the nucleon's other properties to about 30% (Adkins, Nappi and Witten 1983: the magnetic-moment ratio μ_p/μ_n ≈ −1.43 against −1.46 measured; the axial coupling g_A ≈ 0.61 against 1.27; and the fit itself needs the pion decay constant at about 65 MeV against 93). The pion field and its leading cost are QCD's long-distance description; the extra term that holds the twist's size is a model's, not derived, so the 12 Nov *takes* stay: a cost for twisting and a reason twists hold their size, supplied by QCD only in principle. Conditional on everything the nucleus already takes (C9, the colour field's cost and coupling), on the walls' handedness extended to interactions (B4–B6 are free or static-field runs), and on reading quark mass as a coupling between the walls, which the weak interaction's one-wall shape would re-read; at most one of the two readings survives (`weak_interaction.md`). ## A nucleon as confined commitment (21 Nov, reading)

Mass is renewal rate (C2, C4), and a proton renews at 938 MeV/ħ while its quarks' own masses supply about 1%: nearly all of its rate is quarks and gluons in transit, confined. The rules already count that as mass. On the 1D step a massless pattern held between two walls (run C10) has a level E₀ with no mass of its own, and in a uniform field (N, ψ²) a box of fixed proper width has E = N·E₀ to 0.4% up to Φ = 1, the factor a rest mass has; raised in a weak field its energy changes as a mass's does. So trapped transit ticks, redshifts and weighs as mass does, which gravity's source needs (a proton gravitates with its whole mass). Size sets the scale: three massless quarks in a ball of radius R have E = 3 × 2.04 ħc/R, 1440 MeV at the charge radius 0.84 fm against 938 (a bag estimate, a factor 1.5). Limits: the walls are held, not made by the pattern (in a nucleon the colour field confines, by identification); in the continuum the scaling follows from P7f's form, so the run checks only that the lattice keeps it. Held walls give the weight but not the inertia: carried by a slow heavy carrier the box adds 1.99 E₀ of inertia, not E₀ (the level under a drift λ is E₀(1 − λ²), run C10). That is the old box-of-light problem (4/3 in three dimensions, 2 in one): a rigid wall carries no stress, and the stress of real walls supplies the missing −E₀. Weight and inertia agree only when what confines is itself dynamical, as the colour field is. Grade: reading.

## Mass with none put in: the Schwinger model on the step (run C16)

The nucleon reading above needs what confines to be dynamical. The simplest case where it is: one space dimension, charged patterns with no bare mass on the flow step, H_W = σ_x sin k + σ_z(m + r(1 − cos k)) with r = ½, coupled to the which-way field with charge g (units: hop, ħ and c are 1). In one dimension the field has no waves of its own; Gauss's law fixes it from the charges, with energy (g²/2)ΣL_n². Schwinger's exact answer in the continuum: massless charges vanish from the spectrum, leaving one free neutral pattern of mass g/√π = 0.564g, with E² = M² + p². Exact diagonalisation on chains of 8 and 10 places, open ends, bare m = 0:

| g | M/g (N = 8, 10) | speed of the levels | third level, predicted from the first two (N = 8, 10) | dM/dm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0.69, 0.68 | 1.04 | within 11%, 4% | 1.95 |
| 0.75 | 0.69, 0.68 | 1.01 | within 3.5%, 2.2% | 1.86 |
| 1.0 | 0.68, 0.68 | 0.97 | within 3.1%, 1.8% | 1.85 |

- The lowest neutral pattern has a mass in proportion to the charge, 0.68g, with no bare mass. It is 21% above Schwinger's 0.564g.
- Its levels fit one free pattern with E² = M² + v²p² and fixed ends: the speed from the two chains is within 4% of light's, and the third level falls where the first two put it to 2–3% for g ≥ 0.75. It moves as a pattern of that rest energy does. What the held-wall box lacked (inertia 1.99 E₀ for weight E₀) is not seen; the inertia was not measured apart from this.
- Its mass answers to the bare mass at 1.85–1.95 per unit, against the continuum's e^γ = 1.78.
- The 21%: the lifting term breaks the handedness symmetry, so with the field on, a charge with no bare mass has an induced one (the mass's additive shift, tuning 2). For this model with Wilson's term the shift is known to go as g² (Angelides, Funcke, Jansen and Kühn 2023, at r = 1); carried to r = ½ it would be about 0.1g², which accounts for part or all of the 21%. These chains are too short to find the massless point themselves (the standard way, full screening of a background field, needs chains several times longer), so the split between that shift and the hop's own size is open.

Grade: reading. The step with the which-way field confines and gives mass as the identification with lattice QED says it should; Schwinger's number is met to 21% with no bare mass, the excess of the size the lifting term's induced mass would give.

## The string breaks (run C16d)

Two held charges, +1 and −1, set d links apart in the middle of the 10-place chain (g = 1). With nothing else there the field between them is a string of energy (g²/2)d. The step's own charges answer:

| d | heavy charges (m = 0.8): energy, field at the middle | no bare mass: energy, field at the middle |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.48, 0.97 | 0.39, 0.73 |
| 2 | 0.96, 0.96 | 0.66, 0.55 |
| 3 | 1.44, 0.95 | 0.81, 0.33 |
| 4 | 1.92, 0.94 | 0.88, 0.21 |
| 5 | 2.37, 0.61 | 0.91, 0.12 |
| 6 | 2.43, 0.01 | 0.94, 0.09 |
| 7 | 2.44, 0.00 | 0.97, 0.06 |

With heavy charges the string holds, its energy within about 4% of (g²/2)d out to four links, then breaks between five and six: the energy stops at 2.44, the field at the middle goes to zero, and a charge of 0.96 from the step has gathered at each end, opposite to the held one. The line has become two neutral things. The break comes where the string's energy passes the cost of the pair, 2.44, near d = 5. With no bare mass there is no stretch to speak of: the field is cut down from the first link and the energy levels off near 0.9–1.0 (the chain's ends are not far enough away to fix it; the continuum's (g√π/2)(1 − e^{−Md}) levels at 0.89 and gives 0.38 at one link, against 0.39 here), with 0.87 of a charge gathered at each end by d = 7.

Reading. A whole charge is not left alone on the step: pulling one out makes the field pay for a partner, and what is left is neutral. One chain, one coupling, whole held charges only: a held half charge cannot be matched by whole ones, and its string is expected to hold (not run here).

## A half charge keeps its string (run C16e)

The same chain with held charges of ±½. The step has only whole charges to answer with.

| d | heavy charges (m = 0.8): energy, field at the middle | no bare mass: energy, field at the middle |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.121, 0.49 | 0.101, 0.40 |
| 3 | 0.362, 0.48 | 0.257, 0.31 |
| 5 | 0.603, 0.48 | 0.381, 0.23 |
| 7 | 0.844, 0.48 | 0.496, 0.17 |

With heavy charges the string holds at every length tried: 0.1205 per link against the bare string's 0.125, the field staying at 0.48, nothing gathered at the ends. A whole charge at each end would leave a string of −½, the same energy, so no pair can end it. With no bare mass the field is cut down but a pull remains, still 0.06 per link at seven links; in the continuum, charges truly without mass screen any held charge fully, and a small mass leaves a pull in proportion to it, so this is the lifting term's induced mass showing again (its size is not fixed by a chain this short).

Reading. On the step a held charge that the moving charges cannot match stays tied, so long as they have mass. This is the U(1) likeness of a colour charge that the free charges cannot cancel; it says nothing about quarks' fractional electric charge. One dimension, one chain, one coupling.

## Which combinations leave no string (run C17, desk)

With three colour states and the colour field in place of the which-way field, Gauss's law in one dimension gives each link the energy (g²/2)·C₂, with C₂ fixed by the colour state of everything to one side of the link. Computed from the eight colour generators acting on the quarks themselves:

| what is on one side | string energy per link, in units of g²/2 (number of states) |
|---|---|
| one quark | 4/3 (3) |
| two quarks | 4/3 (3), 10/3 (6) |
| a quark and an antiquark | 0 (1), 3 (8) |
| three quarks | 0 (1), 3 (16), 6 (10) |
| two quarks and an antiquark | 4/3 (6), 10/3 (6), 16/3 (15) |

One quark always trails a string. Two quarks at best trail the same string as one (their best joint state acts as an antiquark). A quark with an antiquark, or three quarks, have exactly one joint state with no string at all, and that is the state the field's energy picks. So the combinations seen, in twos (quark and antiquark) and in threes, all have a string-free state; a pair of quarks has none. This is the group's arithmetic (3 ⊗ 3̄ ∋ 1, 3 ⊗ 3 ⊗ 3 ∋ 1, 3 ⊗ 3 = 6 ⊕ 3̄) read as field energy, not a new result.

What a run would add is the dynamics: masses, and strings breaking. An exact run of the three-colour model on the step has 3.4 × 10⁵ states at 4 places, 1.6 × 10⁷ at 5 and 8 × 10⁸ at 6; 4 and 5 places are in reach, 6 is not; at C16d's mass and coupling a string needed 5 to 6 links to break (a heavier coupling or lighter charge shortens that). Two colours reach 6 or 7 places. A first exact run at 3 and 4 places is below; strings breaking would need 5 places with the break brought inside the chain, or another method.

## Three colours on the step, moving (run C18)

Quarks as the flow step's pattern in three colour states, the colour field fixed by Gauss's law, chains of 3 and 4 places, bare mass 0, exact. A tail of K links beyond the chain carries its total colour away, so a set of quarks that is not neutral pays for the tail. Quarks added to the filled lower states, at g = 1.5 on 4 places:

| quarks added | energy above the vacuum, no tail | with a tail of 3 links | rise per link of tail |
|---|---|---|---|
| none | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| one | 0.55 | 5.05 | (4/3)(g²/2) |
| two | 1.08 | 5.58 | (4/3)(g²/2) |
| three | 1.50 | 1.50 | 0 |

With the quarks free to move and the field answering, one quark trails a string of 4/3, two quarks the same (they take the joint state that acts as an antiquark, not the one at 10/3), and three quarks none. The vacuum's first excitation (read as a quark with an antiquark; shown only to be neutral) costs 0.95 and trails none. The same holds at 3 places and at g = 1, 1.5 and 2. The rises per link are exact, as the colour symmetry and the tail require of a correct code; the antiquark-like state for two quarks is forced by the field's energy once there is a tail.

The energies of the neutral sets (three quarks 1.3 to 1.6, quark with antiquark 0.8 to 1.0, over g = 1 to 2) are not yet masses: with no tail an added quark sits on the last place, where its field leaves the chain at no cost, and its energy (0.55, the same at 3 and 4 places) is set by the step's lifting term there and the open end; three cost about three times that.

Grade: reading. The three-state colour kind (C9) on the step, with its field, gives a neutral three and a neutral first excitation, and strings on one quark and on two, with the quarks moving. The exact rises check the code more than the step. One dimension; chains of 3 and 4 places; a tail added by hand.

## One pattern, three seats, a pool on loan (run C16f)

A picture of the nucleon in CO's two primitives. A nucleon is one whole with one clock. Its commitment is one event with three seats in it, the quarks: they share the commitment, and so the clock; a quark has none of its own (chosen, candidate). Between them is a pool of trapped possibility, in transit: the colour field, shaped by where the three are. Between commitments the quarks hand off to the pool and take back from it. The commitment is on loan: almost none of the nucleon's rate is the quarks' own mass.

Where the line is drawn. At confinement, which is imported: a part that cannot stand alone has no commitment of its own, and shares that of the smallest colour-neutral whole it is in. (That colour is no fact is the motive, not a derivation: the which-way phase is no fact either, and electrons commit.) Parts that can stand alone (an electron, a nucleon) keep their own commitments; in an atom or a nucleus their rates add to nearly the whole's, short by the binding (under 1% in nuclei), where the quarks' own masses would give a nucleon about 1% of its rate, which is why the nucleon forces the question. A probe's engagement can still land at one quark's place (C8), as deep inelastic scattering shows; what commit afterwards are the hadrons made. Consistent with only neutral things being seen; that itself stays imported.

The one case that can be solved exactly bears out only the last sentence: little of the rate is the charges' own mass. It says nothing of one clock, three seats or taking turns, and its neutral pattern is made of two charges, not three. On the chain of run C16 (one dimension, one kind of charge, 10 places, no bare mass), the lowest neutral pattern's energy above the vacuum can be divided into three parts that add up to it (one way of dividing it: the parts do not commute, so only the sum is a fact; the field's part is (g/2)∂E/∂g):

| g | in the field | in the charges' motion | in the lifting term |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 31% | 56% | 13% |
| 0.75 | 39% | 45% | 15% |
| 1.0 | 39% | 42% | 19% |

A third to two-fifths of what makes the pattern tick is the field's; most of the rest is the charges moving; the lifting term's share is the induced mass of run C16, the only part that is mass of the charges' own. On 8 places the shares move by up to 5 points. The level is the chain's lowest standing wave, not a pattern at rest, so part of the motion's share is the pattern's own; for a free pattern at rest the field's share would be one half, and for a moving one M²/2E² (32%, 40%, 45% here). For the proton itself, lattice QCD gives 36% in the gluon field, 32% in the quarks' energy, 9% from the mass term of the u, d and s quarks (the three valence quarks' own masses are about 1%) and 23% from the trace anomaly, in one scheme at one scale (Yang et al., Phys. Rev. Lett. 121, 212001 (2018)): similar rough proportions in a far richer theory, so the likeness is not a test.

Grade: the shared commitment chosen, candidate (C9 reworded: a quark no longer renews as an electron does; the nucleus record's "the tiles of one pattern commit together" is kept); the rest reading. Open: how the separate rates of free-standing parts add in a bound body, binding included; whether a neutral whole of field alone counts as a pattern that commits; what a quark's mass is, now a term in the step and not a rate of its own; what commits where there is no neutral whole (the quark–gluon plasma, the beginning, a top quark that decays before it binds, a coloured pair just made); wholes of four or five quarks and hadron molecules; the counts made per quark (dark matter's mass by count, the first ball's first places), to be redone per whole.
