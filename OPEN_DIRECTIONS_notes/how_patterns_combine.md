# How patterns combine: the gap under the ladder tops

**The gap.** CO's rules are written for one pattern walking and one commitment at a time. Almost every wall past 1930 needs a rule for several patterns sharing routes, with their number changing: the sea and pair creation; quantised light; the exchange sign (Pauli exclusion); gluons and the nucleon's source; how fields draw inside matter (pressure); loops. It is one gap seen from six sides.

**Three parts of it.**
1. *Binding (kinships).* How patterns form a composite that acts as one: atoms, nuclei, a crystal. The author's picture: a bound composite is a weave of kinships, and a stray quantum that matches the weave's channel rings the whole weave at once, like a bell, or like jumping on a made bed. Size sets how many stray quanta reach a weave; stiffness keeps each chain short. This touches measurement (the stopping number N) as much as binding.
2. *Identity (the exchange sign).* Two patterns of the same kind: why two electrons will not share a state. The smallest test is two electrons. Helium's ground state (−79.0 eV, reached with the two-pattern ruling) is symmetric in space and does not test the sign; helium's excited states do: the triplet (orthohelium) and singlet (parahelium) differ by the exchange energy, and the triplet exists only because the sign is negative. Reproducing that splitting would test whatever CO supplies for the sign.
3. *Number change.* Patterns made and unmade: pairs, photons emitted and absorbed. Light's number changes once the which-way field is quantised (capped link, shape); patterns' number: candidate (C6: the walk as a field with filled negative states, pairs made from light).

**What 1930 did.** The same wall, met by physics in its own order: Jordan–Klein and Jordan–Wigner (1927–28) quantised fields so particles became their excitations, with fermions by anticommutators; Heisenberg and Pauli (1929) built quantum electrodynamics; Dirac's hole theory (1930–31) and the positron (1932) made number change real; Fermi (1933–34) created and destroyed particles in beta decay; renormalisation (1947–49) tamed the loops. The move was to make the field primary and particles its excitations. CO's counterpart would be a rule for possibility in transit as something whose excitations are patterns, with number free and the exchange sign fixed. CO has a field-like thing already, the stock of possibility, but rules it classical.

**The direction of the move.** 1930 made the field primary and particles its excitations. CO's instinct runs the other way: a relation carries what is set by what passes (the branched-field ruling), so fields are made by patterns. The capped light field takes the 1930 direction for light (quanta as excitations of what relations carry). A rule for combining patterns has to choose a direction for patterns too, or reconcile the two. Number change (C6) settles it for matter, read both ways (matter record, 2026-11-11): a sea of actual patterns (Dirac's hole theory) is excluded, since newborn places, born with no commitments, would arrive either with empty negative states (antimatter everywhere as space grows) or with filled ones (patterns started other than in pairs, against C7); read as a field, the walk's vacuum is a state newborn places join, and patterns are its excitations, compatible with CO's definition of a pattern (possibility in transit, a wave). So CO is field-first for possibility in transit, matter and light, and pattern-set only for the Coulomb part of the which-way field, the standard division in Coulomb gauge.

**Permissive or selective.** A state space with number free (Fock space) allows; it does not pick. What picks, in standard physics, is a consistency condition: locality, Lorentz invariance and positive energy together force the exchange sign (the spin–statistics theorem). On CO's lattice locality alone selects nothing, and no lattice spin–statistics theorem exists. So the combining rule, posited, would be permissive; it becomes selective only if CO finds a consistency condition that forces the sign.

**What pivots together.**
- *Combining → loops → the frame.* As soon as patterns combine with number change, loops exist, and the lattice's frame gives species-dependent speeds at O(α/π). Climbing this ladder triggers the frame problem.
- *Amounts classical →* the falsifier, semiclassical gravity, harmless dents, Way B blocked.
- *No infinities →* a bounded degree, the frame, the capped light field, a finite self-energy.

**Clues, and the absence of clues.**
- In CO's favour: the hop is physical and data fix it (step order, ℓ ≲ 1.3 × 10⁻³¹ m); the Aharonov–Bohm effect shows the phase on a path is physical where the field is zero, which the phase ruling (the which-way quantity a U(1) phase) requires and a real ratio could not give; the capped field's lineage (Born–Infeld, for a finite self-energy); kinship with Smolin's real-time programme and energetic causal sets.
- The absence: CO has produced integers and fixed values (three dimensions as a consistency condition; spin ½ and g = 2 through the Dirac walk; two polarisations), but no continuous dimensionless number from its primitives (no mass ratio, no 1/137, no reason for three colours). Each step up has needed standard physics' structure (Wald and Boulanger–Damour–Gualtieri–Henneaux, the Dirac walk, Kogut–Susskind). The primitives host structure more than they pick it.

**Can the primitives produce numbers?** The test is whether CO has anything that selects among possibilities, not only things that allow them. Numbers come from selection. Elsewhere: symmetry and consistency give integers (g = 2, fractional quark charges from anomaly cancellation, CO's three dimensions); dynamics with one scale gives ratios (lattice QCD's hadron mass ratios from one input scale); topology gives integers (windings, charges); no one derives 1/137. CO's best number-maker so far is a consistency condition (three dimensions from annealing). The way to strengthen the primitives is more constraints of that kind, not more posits that assert. The place to try first: a consistency condition fixing P_max/ħ, P∞/ħ or δ. One candidate is already in the records: the annealed degree in three dimensions (about 4–6, R7b) with degree ≈ P∞/ħ + 1 (R5) would put P∞ at a few ħ; whether that holds as a ruling, and what it then fixes, is unread. A continuous dimensionless number from the primitives would be the clearest sign they pick structure rather than host it.

**Where to start.** Two electrons: the smallest place the exchange sign shows (orthohelium against parahelium), with no number change and no loops, so no frame problem; read, the sign is now a candidate (P8b); orthohelium against parahelium would test it. For binding without the sign: positronium, two elementary patterns of different kinds. Its gross structure (−6.80 eV/n²) follows from the two-pattern ruling by identification; of its hyperfine split, the magnetic 4/7 is in reach and the annihilation 3/7 needs number change, as do its lifetimes. The line between gross binding and number change runs through one atom.

**Related.** Condensed matter is the nearest laboratory for relativity from a substrate: graphene's electrons are a Dirac walk on a lattice with their own limiting speed; Volovik, *The Universe in a Helium Droplet* (2003), develops emergent relativity and gravity-like effects from a quantum liquid and meets the same frame problem.

**Interaction as falling into step (sketch).** The author's picture: a star's commitments pulse in step, and patterns interact through what they can fall into step with. Two parts, graded separately.
- *Same rate (candidate, with the free-share rule).* Every commitment at a place runs at its own ω₀ times the same 1 − h, so all slow by the same fraction; a level difference (E_f − E_i)/ħ slows with them, so a resonance between patterns at one place survives. Same rate is not same phase.
- *Same beat (candidate, by identification; classical drive).* A bound pattern's states beat at E_k/ħ. With the walk (Dirac) and the which-way phase (minimal coupling), a classical drive at ω (the large-occupancy limit of the branched which-way field) moves amplitude from state i to state f with probability

$$P_{i\to f}(t)=\frac{\Omega^{2}}{\Omega^{2}+\Delta^{2}}\sin^{2}\!\left(\tfrac12\sqrt{\Omega^{2}+\Delta^{2}}\,t\right),\qquad \Delta=\omega-\frac{E_f-E_i}{\hbar},$$

with Ω = |⟨f|d·E₀|i⟩|/ħ, starting in i, two levels only (others far off step), rotating-wave (Ω, |Δ| ≪ ω), no decay. Off step (|Δ| ≫ Ω) the transfer stays small (peak Ω²/(Ω² + Δ²)); in step it is complete. Transfer also needs a nonzero matrix element (selection rules). So "interact through what you can fall into step with" is resonance: a drive's beat must match the difference between two of the pattern's beats, within a width.
- *Between two bound patterns.* Closer than about a wavelength, the bound part of the which-way field (Coulomb plus Breit) couples |e g⟩ to |g e⟩, and the transfer is resonant in the mismatch of their level differences (candidate, by identification; it rests on the two-pattern ruling carried to more patterns). At larger separations the transfer goes through the emitted part, which is number change.
- *Direction (candidate, by identification; classical drive).* A drive travelling along k carries the spatial phase e^{i(k·x − ωt)} and couples to each constituent at its own place, e^{ik·x_j}. With the two-pattern ruling the bound pattern is the same at every place, so the pattern's total lattice momentum obeys

$$\mathbf P_f=\mathbf P_i+\hbar\mathbf k\pmod{2\pi\hbar/\ell},$$

the recoil of absorption; a fixed external source gives no recoil. A jump by a whole step 2πħ/ℓ needs about 10¹⁹ eV, the zone edge where the Wilson term puts the doublers, so energy matching excludes it; ħk is a fraction kℓ/2π ≲ 10⁻²⁵ of that step. The cubic lattice breaks rotation, so angular-momentum selection rules hold up to corrections of order (pℓ/ħ)² ≈ 10⁻⁴¹ (the binding momentum, dominant) and (kℓ)² ≈ 10⁻⁴⁸. The Wilson term's O(mℓc/ħ) ≈ 10⁻¹⁹ shifts levels but is rotation-invariant at leading order.
- *What it bears on.* For a classical drive and a translation-invariant engager, the walk and the phase give C8's frequency and direction matching, within a width (Ω, or the linewidth); a sharp cutoff does not follow, and C8 reads a channel as a final state with its width, engagement following its line shape (the author's ruling, candidate). What stays posited in C8: that a passage can be an engagement, which passages are, single-quantum exchange (number change, priced and held), and the Lüders update; Born's chance is identified, the pointer basis imported.


**Sketch, ungraded (the author, 2026-11-11): three kinds of pattern, and a three-fold one.** The author's picture: three kinds of pattern, the single one (the electron), the three-fold one (quarks) and the channel-less one (dark). The three-fold pattern renews only as a set of three tiles: one tile alone is a design, not real on its own; two have some realness but do not complete a pattern; three complete it; inside, the parts keep switching roles. The known facts it meets: quarks are never seen alone (confinement); every quark–antiquark particle decays; the lightest three-quark particle, the proton, is stable (the free neutron decays, stable only in nuclei); and nucleons trade two-quark pions, turning protons into neutrons and back (Yukawa's nuclear force). What would make it CO's own and not a relabelling of QCD is a reason for three. Physics gets three from colour; the standing lesson forbids putting a three-valued label on a tile and calling it colour. The author's version runs the other way: three as a completion count, colour as the bookkeeping of which tile is which.

*A first check of "three from the three axes".* The walk splits each step into one part per axis. Suppose a tile is a two-component walker that moves along one axis only. Moving in all three directions then needs three tiles. Their joint states have 2³ = 8 components, and with the tiles linked by sign strings (a Jordan–Wigner arrangement, a candidate meaning for "kinship"), they carry the full Dirac set: α_x = σ_x⊗1⊗1, α_y = σ_z⊗σ_x⊗1, α_z = σ_z⊗σ_z⊗σ_x and β = σ_z⊗σ_z⊗σ_z all anticommute (checked numerically). The operators commuting with all four form a 2 × 2 algebra, so the eight components are a spin-½ pattern (four) times an extra two-valued label. One tile moves on a line, two only in a plane, so three is the smallest number that moves in space. The extra two-valued label invites a reading as the proton–neutron pair (a doublet whose charge differs by one), and thirds of a pattern's charge shared over three tiles give quark-like charges. All of this is a shape: the tiles as one-axis walkers, the strings as kinship, and the doublet as the nucleon are identifications, and known mismatches remain (quarks are spin ½ each; the tiles here are not; mesons have spin 0 or 1).

*Second check: the two-way label.* The operators that commute with the whole Dirac set (α_x, α_y, α_z, β) are, in tile terms, τ₁ = 1⊗σ_x⊗σ_y, τ₂ = σ_x⊗σ_y⊗1 and τ₃ = σ_x⊗σ_z⊗σ_y (with the identity): a Pauli algebra, so the label is a true doublet with its own SU(2). Three things follow. (1) The walk built from α's and β (its hops, its mass and its Wilson term) commutes with τ, so the two versions are exactly degenerate on the lattice: an exact isospin symmetry. In nature proton and neutron differ by 0.14% in mass (938.27 and 939.57 MeV), a symmetry broken only by charge and the quark masses, so exact degeneracy at this level is the right first answer, and the splitting needs couplings CO has not built. (2) The operators that flip the label, τ₁ and τ₂, each act on a pair of tiles; the label itself, τ₃, acts on all three. In the author's words, two-tile parts switch the three-tile pattern between its versions, which is the role the two-quark pion plays between proton and neutron. (3) Charge differing by one across the doublet, Q = (1 + τ₃)/2, is a choice here, not derived. Grade unchanged: shape.

*Third check, the one that breaks it: spin 3/2.* Three quarks, each of spin ½ and isospin ½, combine into spin ½ with isospin ½ (the nucleon doublet, 938–940 MeV) or spin 3/2 with isospin 3/2 (the Δ quartet, Δ⁺⁺, Δ⁺, Δ⁰, Δ⁻ near 1232 MeV, very well established). In the three-tile pattern as built, the rotation generators S_i = −(i/4)ε_ijk α_j α_k close correctly but give S² = 3/4 on all eight states, and the doublet's Casimir is 3/4 on all eight: spin ½ and isospin ½ only (checked numerically). There is no room for the Δ. So the identification, as built, reproduces the nucleon and misses its nearest relative. The break is located: here the tiles carry neither spin ½ nor isospin ½ of their own; only the whole does. Getting the Δ needs each tile to carry its own spin ½ and two-way label, which is the quark model's starting point and moves the sketch back toward relabelling QCD. What survives: three as the least number of one-axis tiles that moves in space, and the nucleon doublet with its pair-acting flips. Grade: shape, with a recorded mismatch.

*Fourth check, the repair: a spin ½ on each tile.* Give each one-axis tile its own spin ½ as well. The tile spins alone combine to ½ or 3/2, as the Δ needs, but the three-tile Dirac structure already carries a spin ½ of its own (any set of anticommuting α's does: their commutators generate rotations), so the whole is four spin-½ pieces and its total spin is 0, 1 or 2 (checked numerically): integer, the wrong kind for a baryon. The repair is excluded. Making the tiles full spin-½ walkers in three dimensions would work, but each tile then moves in space alone, and three is no longer the least number that moves: the reason for three from the axes is lost, and three would have to come from elsewhere (colour, as in QCD).

*Where the sketch closes.* Three one-axis tiles give the nucleon doublet, exactly degenerate and flipped by two-tile operators, with three as the least number that moves in space; they cannot give the Δ, and the one repair that would gives integer spin. So "three from the three axes" does not reach the baryons as they are. Kept as a shape that met the nucleon and failed at its first excited state, with the failure located. Reopened by the author (2026-11-12): one failed repair does not close it.

*Poke 1: the Δ as three plus two, not as a completed pattern.* The Δ lives about 5 × 10⁻²⁴ s and always decays into a nucleon and a pion. In the author's terms that is a three-tile pattern with a two-tile one moving around it: "the 3 and 2 parts interact". Read that way, the Δ needs no spin on the tiles. The sketch already has the pieces: the nucleon doublet (spin ½, isospin ½, positive parity) and the two-tile flip operators τ₁, τ₂, which carry the doublet's label as a vector (isospin 1, like the pion). The combination with one unit of orbital motion (L = 1) gives
- spin: ½ ⊗ 1 = 3/2 ⊕ ½,
- isospin: ½ ⊗ 1 = 3/2 ⊕ ½,
- parity: (+)(−)(−1)^L = +,
so a state with J = 3/2, I = 3/2 and positive parity is available: the Δ's quantum numbers, four charge states included. This is the established picture of the Δ as a pion–nucleon resonance in a P wave (the static model of Chew and Low, 1956). What the sketch would need: the two-tile pattern to be a real, pseudoscalar (negative-parity, spin-0) pattern, and a reason the 3 + 2 configuration resonates near 1232 MeV.

*Poke 2: dimension locked to the label (Skyrme).* In Skyrme's picture of baryons, the pion field's three label directions are tied to the three directions of space (the "hedgehog"), and the nucleon and the Δ come out as two rotation states of one object, with spin equal to isospin: ½ and ½, then 3/2 and 3/2. Witten (1983) showed that this object is a fermion exactly when the number of quark colours is odd. Two things there match the author's instinct that quark behaviour is tied to dimension: the label locked to the three axes, and the count three mattering because it is odd, which makes the baryon a fermion. Not yet checked in CO's terms: whether the three-tile pattern, rotated as a whole with its label turned along with space, gives the J = I tower.

Grade: both pokes are shapes, with known physics behind them; neither is derived from CO's rules.

*The two-tile pattern, built.* Two one-axis tiles linked by the sign rule carry α_x = σ_x⊗1, α_y = σ_z⊗σ_x and β = σ_z⊗σ_z (checked): a pattern that moves only in a plane, with spin ±½ about the plane's normal and a two-valued (not three-valued) extra label. The pion moves in space, has spin 0 and a three-valued label. So the two-tile pattern on its own is not the pion: no.

*What survives of poke 1.* Inside the three-tile pattern, operators with the pion's quantum numbers exist: with G = iα_xα_yα_z (it commutes with the α's and anticommutes with β, the role of γ₅), the three operators Gτ_a are pseudoscalar and carry the label as a vector. That is the standard pion–nucleon coupling, ψ̄ iγ₅ τ_a ψ π_a, the field-theory form of the Chew–Low picture; so the Δ as nucleon plus pion in a P wave survives, but only with the pion as a field coupled to the nucleon, not as a two-tile pattern.

*A correction.* The earlier point that the label is flipped by two-tile operators is not robust: the tile count of an operator depends on the order in which the sign rule links the tiles. In the same arrangement τ₁ and τ₂ act on two tiles and τ₃ on three, while the pseudoscalar set Gτ_a acts on three, one and two (checked). That point is withdrawn.

So far: three as the least number of one-axis tiles moving in space; an exact doublet; the Δ only as nucleon plus a pion field, not from tiles; the two-tile pattern a planar spin-½ object, not a meson. Poke 2 (rotating the whole with its label tied to space) is untried.

*Poke 2: the label tied to space.* Within one three-tile pattern, tying the label to space means combining spin and label into one rotation, K = S + I. The three-tile states have K = 0 or 1 (checked): a K = 0 state is the pattern's "hedgehog", the label pointing along each direction of space. But one pattern of eight states cannot carry the tower spin = label = ½, 3/2, …: rotating it gives back only spin ½ and label ½, the same limit as the third check. Skyrme's tower needs a different kind of object: not a few tiles, but an extended twist in a three-valued field, with the field's label wound once around the three directions of space. Such twists exist because space has three dimensions and the label space (the unit 3-sphere of an SU(2) field) has three as well: the maps from one to the other fall into whole-number classes (π₃(S³) = ℤ), and the class is the baryon number. Turning the twist as a whole gives the nucleon (spin = label = ½) and the Δ (3/2, 3/2) as its first two rotation states (Adkins, Nappi and Witten 1983 fit their masses), and the twist is a fermion when quantised with the 2π rotation giving −1 (Finkelstein–Rubinstein; Witten 1983: when the number of colours is odd).

*Reading for CO.* This is the cleanest form of the author's instinct that quark behaviour is tied to dimension: a baryon as a twist that wraps three directions of space onto a three-dimensional label space, possible because both are three. The Δ comes for free, as rotation. What CO lacks: a three-valued field on the network (pion-like) and a reason its twists are stable (Skyrme's extra term), and the fermion condition, which in CO could come from P8b's frame transport (a 2π turn of the twist giving −1) rather than from a count of colours. Grade: shape, with established physics behind it; the tiles give way to a twist. The nucleus wall's "three" would then be the three dimensions twice over, space and label, not a count of parts.

*Piece 1: where a three-valued field could come from in CO.* The twist needs a field whose value at each place is a point on a 3-sphere (an SU(2) element). Four places to look, with verdicts:
1. *The spin frame at each place* (the frames P8b carries for the exchange sign). Exactly the right kind of value, and it would bring the fermion condition with it. But its three rotations are a gauge freedom in CO's gravity (the walk's spin connection; space and gravity record): a frame twist would be a relabelling, not a thing, and its windings would label vacua (as instantons do), not particles. Unusable unless frame rotations cost something, which would go against the gauge reading. No, as CO stands.
2. *The which-way field made three-valued* (an SU(2) phase in place of the U(1) phase). Its gauge transformations wind (the same π₃), but again as vacuum labels, not particles; and a three-valued which-way field is the weak interaction's form, which CO lacks. Not for baryons.
3. *The orientation of the walk field's vacuum in its label space.* With number change ruled (C6), the vacuum is a state of the walk field. If the field carries the two-valued label found in the three-tile check, and its vacuum picks a direction in the combined left- and right-handed label space, the slow turning of that direction from place to place is a field U(x) with values in SU(2): Goldstone's pions, and Skyrme's twists are its windings. This is the established route (spontaneous chiral symmetry breaking; pions as its Goldstone modes; baryons as Skyrmions). Needs, in CO: (a) the label as a property of the walk field everywhere, not only of a three-tile pattern; (b) a pairing between the walk field's left- and right-handed parts strong enough to make the vacuum pick a direction (in QCD, the strong interaction does it; Nambu–Jona-Lasinio models show four-fermion pairing suffices); (c) a lattice walk that keeps the left–right symmetry to break, against Nielsen–Ninomiya and the Wilson term, which breaks it by hand (Ginsparg–Wilson walks are the known escape).
4. *A new primitive field.* Excluded by method unless it cascades.

So the candidate is 3: the pion field as the vacuum's orientation, the baryon as its twist, three as dimension twice over, and the fermion sign from the 2π turn. Of its three needs, (c) is a known lattice problem with a known form of solution; (b) is the substantive one: what in CO pairs left and right strongly.

*Need 2: what in CO pairs left and right.* CO already has it. In the walk, mass is the flip F(θ_m) = exp(−iβθ_m), Feynman's checkerboard turn between the two movers on each step: a pairing of left- and right-handed parts, θ_m = c₀mcℓ/ħ per step, and the renewal rate is the mass (C1–C2). What it lacks is a direction. For a field carrying the two-valued label, the flip can turn the movers into each other along any direction in the label's space:

F(θ_m, U) = exp(−iθ_m β (P_R U + P_L U†)),  U = exp(iτ·π/f) ∈ SU(2),

with P_{R,L} the handedness projections and τ the label's generators. With U = 1 this is the present flip; the flip's strength, the renewal rate, fixes the mass, while its direction U is not fixed by any rule. Read as set place by place, U(x) is the three-valued field of piece 1, and the vacuum's choice of a uniform U is the spontaneous breaking: slow turnings of U are pions, and windings of U over the three directions of space are Skyrme's twists, the baryons. This is the chiral (sigma-model) form of a mass term, m ψ̄(σ + iγ₅τ·π)ψ with σ² + π² = 1, written as CO's flip.

So the candidate for needs 1 and 2 together: *the renewal's flip has a direction in the label's space, set place by place.* What it would give: pions as ripples of the flip's direction, nearly massless (their 140 MeV, small against the nucleon's 939 MeV, from a small fixed tilt, as quark masses do in QCD); nucleons getting their mass from the flip's strength; baryons as twists. What remains: (a) a cost for gradients of U, and Skyrme's extra term (or another reason) for twists to hold their size; (b) the Wilson term, which flips with a fixed direction and so breaks the symmetry by hand (need 3: a Ginsparg–Wilson walk); (c) why the label is two-valued (in nature: two light quark kinds). Grade: shape; a proposal that would need the author's ruling before it is a posit.

*Need 3: can the walk keep the left–right symmetry and still have no doublers?* Three known routes, read against CO's walk, which is strictly local (each step reaches only neighbours; L1b):
1. *Exact lattice chiral symmetry (Ginsparg–Wilson, overlap).* Keeps a lattice-modified symmetry exactly, with no doublers, but no such operator can be strictly local: its reach falls off exponentially over a few spacings, never to zero (Horváth 1998; Hernández, Jansen and Lüscher 1999). For a walk, by analogy, that would mean leakage outside the light cone at the scale ℓ. Against L1b: no, as CO stands.
2. *Wilson's fixed-direction flip, then tuning.* Removes the doublers and breaks the symmetry by hand; the symmetry comes back only as kℓ → 0, and the mass must be tuned against an additive shift (lattice QCD's practice; discrete-time walks contain this term naturally, Arnault 2021, 2105.12314). Works, at the cost of a second tuning beside the frame problem's.
3. *Keep the doublers and count them as the label.* The doublers are not removed but carried as a flavour, and a symmetry with handedness survives exactly because nothing is cut away; Nielsen–Ninomiya is met, not evaded, since each flavour comes with both handednesses (Bakircioglu, Arnault and Arrighi 2505.07900, discrete time; Kogut and Susskind's staggered fermions, continuous time). In three space dimensions, staggered fermions in Hamiltonian form put one component on each corner of a 2×2×2 cell: eight components, which is exactly two four-component Dirac fields (Susskind 1977). (In four-dimensional Euclidean form the count is four; the two belongs to three space dimensions.)

*Reading for CO.* Route 3 meets the three-tile check from the other side. The three one-axis tiles linked by sign strings have eight states, and their α's carry the same sign pattern as the staggered phases (a sign for each axis already crossed); the exact two-valued label found there is then the lattice's own doubling, halved by the tiles, which is what staggered fermions call taste. That would answer need (c), why the label is two-valued: 2³ components per cell, four per Dirac field, leaves two. Nature has two light quark kinds; whether that is more than a coincidence of counts is not shown. Costs: the symmetry kept exactly is a remnant (the one-site shift; U(1)_ε in the Euclidean form) (in continuous time; for a strictly local discrete-time walk, Meyer's lemma removes it in the one-dimensional single-component case, Arnault et al. 1807.08303, so it is open for CO's walk), and the full left–right symmetry of the two kinds returns only as (kℓ)² → 0, as the walk's other lattice corrections do. Pions need it only approximately (they have mass), so that cost may be tolerable; with one exact remnant, one pion is an exact Goldstone mode and the other two are split by interactions. *Checked: term for term.* Kogut and Susskind's staggered Hamiltonian on a 2×2×2 cell (one component per corner, hopping (−i/2)η_j χ†_x χ_{x+j} + h.c. with sign η₁ = 1, η₂ = (−1)^{x₁}, η₃ = (−1)^{x₁+x₂}, mass sign (−1)^{x₁+x₂+x₃}, x₁ the first tile, p measured per site), has

dH/dp_j at p = 0 equal to α_x, α_y, α_z, and its mass matrix equal to mβ,

exactly the three-tile matrices, with the corner's three bits as the three tiles. The spectrum is exactly four-fold degenerate at every p. With the Bloch phase taken per site, H(p) = Σ_j sin p_j α_j + mβ exactly, and the τ_a commute with it at every p; with the phase taken per cell they commute only up to order p² (a convention, checked both ways). So the three-tile pattern is the staggered fermion's cell, and its two-valued label is the staggered taste: exact for the free walk; real taste splitting comes only from interactions (exchanges that carry momentum near π/ℓ), at order (kℓ)² in lattice QCD.

So need 3 is not blocked. Route 1 is closed by strict locality, route 2 works with a tuning, and route 3 keeps the symmetry by making the doublers the label. With the check, need (c) has a candidate answer in CO's terms: the label is two-valued because the three tiles are the 2³ corners of a cell, which hold two Dirac fields. Grade: shape; route 3 is the recommended reading, held. Still open: whether CO's discrete-time walk keeps the exact remnant (Meyer's lemma), and why nature's two light kinds would be these two.

*Discrete time: the staggered walk in CO's form (checked, [`staggered_walk_check.py`](../RECORD_OF_METHOD/NUCLEUS_notes/staggered_walk_check.py)).* One component per place, the 2×2×2 cell as the three tiles. Each axis step is A_j = e^{−iθP_j^in/2} e^{−iθP_j^out} e^{−iθP_j^in/2}, with P_j^in swapping the two corners inside a cell along j and P_j^out swapping across the cell wall (each with the factor i and the sign η_j, so each is its own inverse), and the step is CO's palindrome, U = A_x A_y A_z e^{−iθ_m β} A_z A_y A_x. Strictly local and unitary. Found:
- *Dirac motion:* at long wavelength the step's generator is c₀ Σ_j q_j α_j + θ_m β, with α_j exactly the three-tile matrices (c₀ = 0.59, 0.96 hops per step at θ = 0.3, 0.5). The half-swaps on either side are needed; without them the three generators do not anticommute.
- *No doublers beyond the two kinds* for θ < π/4: the massless quasi-energy closes only at q = 0. At θ = π/4 a time doubler appears at q = (π, π, π), quasi-energy π.
- *Chirality kept exactly:* with no mass, βUβ = U† at every q (more generally βU(m)β = U(−m)†); the mass breaks it, as it should. This is a discrete sublattice symmetry that forbids a mass, not the continuous left–right rotation; the Wilson half-shifts break it by hand. Meyer's lemma (no nontrivial one-component walk repeating every place) does not apply, since this walk repeats every two places.
- *Two kinds, exactly matched:* the spectrum is four-fold degenerate at every q. The local label operators τ_a commute with U only up to order q² (site phase); what keeps the exact match is not identified.
- *Speed (corrected, see the speed check below):* the mass enters the dispersion only as θ_m², so there is no limiting-speed shift linear in the mass, unlike the Wilson walk. An earlier reading here, a top speed below light's by (0.46–0.81)θ_m, was the peak of the curve, not a limiting speed; withdrawn.

So route 3 survives in discrete time: a strictly local, palindromic walk with no doublers, an exact chiral symmetry, and the two-valued label. It would replace C6's four-component walk with Wilson half-shifts.

*The cost: every walk field gets two kinds.* Up and down quarks fit. The electron would get an exactly matched twin of the same charge, which does not exist. The one reading that fits is the twin as the electron's neutrino, as nature pairs every fermion (u with d, ν with e). That needs three things. (a) Different masses for the two kinds: masses are already a silence, and staggered lattices allow kind-splitting mass terms over a few places. (b) The weak interaction's one-handedness: already a named silence. (c) A charge that tells the two kinds apart, and that one is new and hard. A per-cell SU(2) does commute with every in-cell swap and with the mass (IYX, YXI, YZX in tile terms, checked), but each member is broken by the swaps across two of the three cell walls. The label the walk does keep (τ_a) is kept only to order (kℓ)², because it is tied to shifts of one place. So a charge that differs across the two kinds would not be exactly conserved by the walk. With electron stability tested to lifetimes beyond 10²⁸ years, that breaking needs a number before the reading can be judged either way.

No principled reason was found for staggering quarks and not leptons; two walk forms would be an added posit. Grade: route 3 is a shape, held. The Wilson walk (C6) stands.

*The leak, checked ([`staggered_kind_label.py`](../RECORD_OF_METHOD/NUCLEUS_notes/staggered_kind_label.py)).* The free walk keeps an exact two-valued kind label: in each set of matched states, the local label τ₃ projected onto that set gives an operator Q that commutes with the step (automatically, by construction), with eigenvalues ±(1 − O(q²)) (0.99996 at q = 0.4); the label proper is the sign of Q. It differs from the local label by order (qℓ)² (‖Q − τ₃‖ = 2.6 × 10⁻³, 6.9 × 10⁻⁴ at qℓ = 0.1, 0.05). So the free walk has no leak: a pattern of one kind stays that kind forever. Whether couplings leak is open: in staggered lattice QCD, exchanges carrying momentum near π/ℓ change the kind at order (kℓ)². The local label only misnames it, by an amplitude of order (qℓ)²; that is no decay. For an electron in an atom that is about 10⁻⁴¹. What the exact label is not is strictly local: its reach falls by about 0.3 per cell over the first few cells (1.5 × 10⁻², 5 × 10⁻³, 1.5 × 10⁻³, 4.5 × 10⁻⁴), then flattens to a slow tail near 5 × 10⁻⁵ that shrinks only slowly as the grid is refined, and the near-range decay barely changes with the mass, so the reach is not shown to be exponential (possibly a zone-edge discontinuity in the construction). So for the free walk the charge problem is not stability; with couplings, stability is open. The free-level problem is locality: light coupling to a charge that differs across the two kinds would need that charge's density spread over a few places, which is route 1's cost in a smaller form.

*Poke (the author): electrons take part.* In nature the kinds do change: a free neutron becomes a proton in about 15 minutes (n → p e ν̄), and a proton becomes a neutron by taking in an electron (p e → n ν). It never happens alone. A quark's kind flip (d → u, charge +1) always comes with a lepton's (ν → e, charge −1), so total charge is kept exactly. That is the weak interaction, and in Fermi's first form (1934) it is exactly a contact coupling of the two flips. Read in CO: if quarks and leptons are both staggered doublets, the electron's twin is its neutrino. The pairing of the two labels' flips is then the natural form of the weak interaction, and charge has to be kept only for the pair, not for each label alone. That argues for staggering leptons too, not against. Quarks and leptons share the doublet shape; what tells them apart is colour, which this reading does not supply. Precedent: Schmelzer (hep-lat/0111057, 0208040) proposed a staggered lattice whose doubling gives exactly one Dirac doublet, read as a physical lepton or quark doublet, and found that coupling it to gauge fields produces a one-handed (1 + γ₅) interaction naturally, which is the weak interaction's handedness, a named silence in CO. A caution in the other direction: doublers carry the same quantum numbers as the physical states (see the survey in 2604.13146), which is the charge problem again.

So far: the free walk keeps the kinds exactly, so there is no stability bound; the open costs are the locality of a kind-dependent charge, and the weak interaction's handedness, which Schmelzer's construction suggests the doubling may itself supply. Grade: shape, held.

*Handedness, checked ([`handedness_check.py`](../RECORD_OF_METHOD/NUCLEUS_notes/handedness_check.py)).* Does a field acting on the kind label couple one-handedly, as Schmelzer found? Two findings.
- *Strictly local: no.* The operators acting inside one cell that commute with the step at every q are only the charge (the identity, both handednesses, both kinds alike) and, for the massless walk, complex conjugation. There is no strictly local commuting kind symmetry, continuous chiral symmetry or second complex structure (β is strictly local but anticommutes: the sublattice symmetry above). So a field that couples place by place can only be light's kind of coupling: kind-blind and two-handed. Schmelzer's one-handedness comes from putting real and imaginary parts on different places, so that the complex structure becomes a choice. CO's amplitude rule (one complex amplitude per transit) does not allow that split. His mechanism does not carry over as CO stands.
- *Quasi-local: yes, permitted.* The chirality G = iα_xα_yα_z (it commutes with the α's and τ's and anticommutes with β) and the one-handed kind flips P_L τ_a, P_L = (1 − G)/2, are kept by the massless step up to order (qℓ)² ([G, U] = 1.2 × 10⁻³, 3.1 × 10⁻⁴ at qℓ = 0.1, 0.05). Projected onto each set of matched states, they commute with the step and with the kind label (eigenvalues ±(1 − O(q²))). So the free walk has separate left and right kind rotations, SU(2)_L × SU(2)_R. Limits: the kind-blind chirality G cannot be both exact and local with no extra doublers (Nielsen–Ninomiya), and once light couples it is broken by the axial anomaly; the projected G's reach falls more slowly than the kind label's (1.2 × 10⁻⁴ at 10 cells). The kind-dependent rotations (τ_a, Gτ_a) are not excluded by that argument, but their exact local form is not shown. So a field coupling to the left rotation only, the weak interaction's form, is permitted at the free level. Not predicted: nothing in the walk chooses left over right. Witten's global anomaly needs an even number of left-handed doublers: with colour, N_c quark doublets plus one lepton doublet, four for three colours; the perturbative anomalies also cancel only with N_c = 3, and this reading does not supply colour.

So the weak interaction's handedness moves from silence to permitted at the free level, at the same cost as the kind-dependent charge: a coupling spread over a few places, not strictly local, and with the reach of that spread not yet shown to fade exponentially. Mass breaks the left–right split, as it should. Grade: shape, held.

*Locality relaxed (the author's ruling, candidate; MATTER record, 2026-11-12).* Couplings may reach beyond neighbours with a tail fading exponentially at the hop scale; the free walk may stay strictly local. With that, the staggered walk's exact kind label can carry a kind-dependent charge, and its left and right kind rotations can carry a one-handed coupling: the weak interaction's form, permitted, not predicted.

*Speed check ([`staggered_speed_check.py`](../RECORD_OF_METHOD/NUCLEUS_notes/staggered_speed_check.py)).* E(q, m)² − E(q, 0)² = θ_m²(1 − O(q²)) to eight digits: no term linear in the mass (exact by the symmetry βU(m)β = U(−m)†), so no limiting-speed shift of the Wilson kind (s ≈ 0.24 r mcℓ/ħ). But the massless dispersion bends below light's and depends on direction (E/c₀q − 1 ≈ −0.032 q² along an axis, −0.062 q² along the diagonal, θ = 0.5, q per cell), and the two kinds of term are the same size at k ≈ √(m/ℓ), about 1 PeV for the electron: the Crab and LHAASO range. Unless light bends the same way, subluminal electrons are bounded there (photon decay, synchrotron cutoff), at a scale comparable to the Wilson bound (roughly ℓ ≲ 3 × 10⁻³¹ m). So the staggered walk does not lift the bound on ℓ; it changes its form, and the bound depends on light's own dispersion. Not yet computed.

*The tail, found ([`staggered_label_twist.py`](../RECORD_OF_METHOD/NUCLEUS_notes/staggered_label_twist.py)).* The slow tail is not an artefact of the grid; it is a twist. The natural kind label (τ_a in the per-site phase, the one that is exact at long wavelength) is not periodic over the momentum zone. Going once around the zone along axis j conjugates it by Z on tile j, and that flips each τ_a along two of the three axes:

τ₁: kept along x, flips along y and z; τ₂: flips along x and y, kept along z; τ₃: flips along x and z, kept along y (checked).

So the two kinds swap when a pattern's momentum is carried once around the zone along x or z: over the zone, the kind label is a Möbius strip, not a plain strip. A label with a jump of this kind has a reach that falls only as a power of the distance (about 1/d), which is the tail seen (site-phase seed: 8.5 × 10⁻², 6.2 × 10⁻², 5.0 × 10⁻², … 3.3 × 10⁻² at 4 to 10 cells, the same on 12- and 20-cell grids). The other way round confirms it: the strictly local label τ₃, periodic by construction, cannot keep the two kinds apart everywhere. Projected onto each set of matched states it weakens to θ_m (0.199 at θ_m = 0.2; 0.050 at 0.05) exactly at the zone points q = (±π, 0, ±π), where the twist bites. Not proved: that no other smooth split of the matched states exists. The natural one is twisted, and the local one fails where the twist says it must.

*Reading.* Kind is partly a momentum label: the two kinds are the lattice's doublers, which sit at different corners of the zone, so at the hop scale the walk cannot tell them apart exactly. At low momentum the strictly local label is good to (kℓ)²; it fails only near kℓ ~ π. So a charge that differs between the kinds can be strictly local and nearly exact, but it is not exactly conserved at the hop scale, and the fading-tail ruling does not rescue it, because the exact label's reach is a power law, not an exponential. The cost lands on light: a coupling to a charge the walk does not keep exactly at the hop scale breaks gauge invariance there, and in loops (momenta up to 1/ℓ) that would give light a mass unless tuned away, a third tuning beside the frame problem's. On the other side of the ledger, the staggered walk needs no mass tuning, since its exact sublattice symmetry protects the mass. The kind-changing processes it allows at low energy are suppressed by powers of ℓ: an electron turning into a neutrino by giving off light comes out at lifetimes far beyond the 10²⁸-year limit (rough estimate, Γ ~ α m_e⁵ ℓ⁴, about 10⁴⁹ years at ℓ = 10⁻³¹ m).

So the openings from the locality ruling come down to one named cost: a kind-dependent charge needs light's mass tuned to zero. Handedness (the weak form) carries the same cost, since it rests on the same label. Grade: shape, held; the cost is named, not excluded.
