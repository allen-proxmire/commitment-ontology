# The weak interaction: where it could enter (desk, 16 Nov 2026)

**Standing.** Silence. CO has no weak interaction: no kind change (n → p e ν̄), no parity violation, no W or Z, no account of neutrinos beyond kinematics. This note asks where in CO's present inventory it could enter. Grade throughout: shape.

**What it needs.** An SU(2) gauge field acting on one handedness only (left-handed doublets), a hypercharge U(1) with different values on the two handednesses, and a field that gives the W and Z their masses and links the two handednesses into fermion masses (in the Standard Model, the Higgs doublet). On any lattice the obstacle is the first item: lattice fermions come in left–right pairs (Nielsen–Ninomiya), so a gauge field that sees only one handedness needs the other's partners removed without breaking the gauge symmetry. No method is established for non-abelian chiral gauge theories in four dimensions.

**What CO already has.** The candidate removal of the mass counter-term (tuning 2) puts matter in a short inner direction: a ring of 2L places carrying two walls in the mass, with one one-way mode bound to each wall (MATTER.md; runs B2–B3 in 1+1 dimensions). In 3+1 dimensions the two walls carry modes of opposite handedness: one handedness on one, the opposite on the other (run B4, free fermions). That is Kaplan's periodic domain-wall construction, the starting point of a recent proposal for a chiral gauge theory on a lattice (others: Lüscher's Ginsparg–Wilson construction, mirror-fermion and symmetric-mass approaches): Grabowska and Kaplan (Phys. Rev. Lett. 116, 211602 (2016)) simulate domain-wall fermions in one more dimension with gauge fields that live on one wall and are extended into the bulk by gradient flow. The partners on the far wall (mirror fermions) then couple to the gauge fields only through form factors that become exponentially soft with the distance between the walls; the construction has a local interpretation only if the chiral representation is free of anomalies.

**The other entry point.** With locality relaxed, the staggered walk's one-handed kind rotation is permitted, not predicted, at the cost of light's mass tuned to zero; held, since C6 keeps charge exactly conserved ([`how_patterns_combine.md`](how_patterns_combine.md)).

**The shape.** The weak interaction enters as the Grabowska–Kaplan form on CO's inner ring: every handedness-carrying field written as left-handed on one wall, the gauge fields living on that wall and carried across the inner direction by a flow, the far wall's mirror partners proposed to decouple as the ring lengthens (the proposal's claim; later analyses question whether they fully do). Its anomaly condition is the one already recorded for the quark charges (OPEN_DIRECTIONS, Up and down charges): with one generation it forces Q_u = 2/3, Q_d = −1/3 for three colours.

**Costs and tensions.**
- *The walls change job.* Tuning 2's removal reads a fermion's mass as a coupling between the two walls (left on one, right on the other). In the chiral form both handednesses of a physical fermion sit on one wall, each written as a left-handed field (the right-handed one as its conjugate), and its mass becomes a coupling between two fields there, through a doublet field. The two readings assign the walls differently; at most one survives.
- *A doublet field.* The W and Z masses and the fermion masses need a field CO does not have. Whether a commitment-rate reading of mass (C4) can stand in for it is open; a size set by the free share is excluded (below).
- *The flow along the inner direction* is a posit of its own, as is the inner direction.
- *Mirrors.* The far wall carries a mirror copy of every fermion, coupled to the gauge fields only softly; with gravity they would be a dark sector; with no mass source there they would be light, radiation and not cold dark matter ([`dark_matter.md`](dark_matter.md)).
- *Three generations* stay a silence.

**Reading.** CO's inner ring is the structure a proposed lattice construction for chiral gauge theories starts from, so the weak interaction's handedness has a named place to enter, at the cost of re-reading what the walls do. No rule; nothing derived. Grade: shape.

## What the direction belongs to (28 Nov 2026, desk; no ruling)

The mass flip joins a pattern's two hands, and only the left hand carries the kind label, so the flip must carry a direction in kind space at each place ([`how_patterns_combine.md`](how_patterns_combine.md), The flip with a direction). Nothing in CO has that direction to carry. Two ways it could enter:

- *A quality of possibility.* Possibility at a place carries, besides its amount, a direction in kind space with a size: in the standard theory's terms a complex doublet (a size, three angles, hypercharge one half, a phase of its own), its resting direction electrically neutral. The left hand's label passes to and from it at each flip; the kind field on the relations meets it everywhere, so three carriers (two of the kind field, one mixed with the hypercharge phase) are heavy, and the one mixture it does not carry, electric charge, keeps a massless carrier, light.
- *A third thing at each place*, beside possibility and record, carrying the same.

Either is the standard mechanism in CO's words. It gives no number: the size (246 GeV), the carriers' masses, the coupling and each kind's mass stay inputs.

**Does the clock survive the first?** The clock rule is derived from possibility carrying "no mark but free or engaged" (appendix, The clock from the cycle). It survives on three conditions.
1. *The direction is a convention at each place.* Only a difference of direction across a relation means anything, as for colour (C9). No place can then read the direction alone. A left hand still reads it against its own label, and the size is readable anywhere (the flip reads both).
2. *The size does not follow the free share.* If it did, the masses that come from the flip would fall with the free share while the clock rule already slows every rate by it (P7f): an electron's rate would go as N², a nucleon's nearly as N, and clocks of different make would drift apart in a gravitational field, which comparisons exclude to under a part in 10⁶ of the potential's change (Lange et al. 2021; C2, P7f). So the size has a stiffness and a resting value of its own, an input.
3. *A commitment engages possibility blind to both.* Not a consequence of the first two: posited. With it the free share 1 − h counts amounts as before.

A size held rigid would meet all three, and is excluded by data: the size's own ripple is the scalar seen at 125 GeV. So the form left is a direction that is a convention place by place, with a size that can ripple and does not track the free share, and commitments blind to both.

**What is not answered.** Hops move amounts only, so a direction that does not follow the amount cannot ride the quanta: it sits at the place, which leans to the second way. Whether the size's resting energy and its ripple count as amounts (the vacuum-energy cost) is open. A field on one hand only still does not stand on the lattice (above). The flip is part of the walk and leaves no record, so it is not a commitment (C1, C3); the passing of the label at a flip is the standard coupling, not C1's engagement. The lifting term would have to carry the direction too (how_patterns_combine). The hop scale against 246 GeV. The walls' two readings (above). Whether "a quality of possibility" differs from "a third thing" in anything that could be checked is not shown: as it stands the difference is a name. The gravity carriers at places stay excluded as the doublet (they are kind-blind); this is a further quality, not the held share.

Grade: shape; reading.
