# Dark matter, and matter over antimatter

Shapes, silences and ungraded sketches, graded as such. Nothing here is a ruling.

**The shape: a pattern that renews but has no channel.** CO separates two things standard physics bundles together:
- *What has mass gravitates through renewal.* Each repeat keeps δħ (C3), and that draw is the sink; charge plays no part.
- *What can be engaged* is anything with a channel: a final state through which another commitment can take a quantum from it (C8, candidate). Charge is a channel for light; colour is a channel for gluons.

A pattern that renews but carries no channel would gravitate fully and be engaged by nothing. As first put: *a pattern that commits but never measures.* Standard physics' dark matter (heavy, cold, felt only through gravity) is then permitted in CO with nothing added, though not predicted.

**What it has to do.**
- *Collisionless:* no channel, no scattering. Halos stay extended; the dark mass in the Bullet Cluster (Clowe et al. 2006) passes through with the galaxies, while the gas, which has channels, is slowed.
- *Cold:* a channel-less pattern never comes into heat balance with anything, so it keeps whatever speed the beginning gives it; a heavy pattern made slow stays slow. How slow the beginning makes it is not ruled.
- *Never detected directly:* every underground search looks for a channel, so CO expects them to keep finding nothing. (Gravity-only dark matter models expect the same, so this does not single CO out.)
- *Made only at the start:* with no channel, nothing later can make it. It would come out of the beginning, when every place was over capacity and committed at once. C7 says matter forms immediately but not which patterns, or how many; that the beginning makes channel-less ones is assumed. Made before the spreading, it faces the same question as ordinary matter made there: how it survives the spreading.

**Two that do not work.**
- *The record as dark matter:* no. The record is not extra mass; the retained share is how ordinary matter gravitates. Counting it again would double count, and it sits where matter has been, not in halos.
- *Neutrinos:* no. They are light and fast, and would wash out small structure; they are excluded as the bulk of dark matter. Whatever the dark pattern is, it differs from them.

**How much: the "about five".** Dark matter is about 5.4 times ordinary matter by mass (Planck 2018: Ω_c h² ≈ 0.120 against Ω_b h² ≈ 0.0224). CO cannot derive that ratio without masses, which are inputs. One CO-shaped relation: if the beginning made dark patterns and ordinary matter in equal numbers, by the same "no room for a second pattern" proposed for the matter–antimatter asymmetry, then the dark pattern's mass would be about 5.4 proton masses, around 5 GeV. That matches, in its simplest form, the asymmetric-dark-matter relation of the standard literature (Kaplan, Luty and Zurek 2009, who give 5–15 GeV). The standard version uses a channel to destroy the dark antiparticles; a channel-less pair cannot annihilate, so this needs the beginning to make no dark antipatterns.

**No anti-dark, from the same hunch.** Read tightly, the hunch says a saturated place at the start makes one pattern, not a pair, because there is no room for the second. That rule does not ask which kind of pattern it makes, so it leaves ordinary matter without primordial antimatter and dark patterns without antipatterns at once: the condition above follows from the hunch rather than being added. It carries one bill. In the standard story, matter and antimatter were made almost equally and annihilated, leaving about one proton per 1.6 × 10⁹ photons of the cosmic background light (η ≈ 6 × 10⁻¹⁰); a beginning that never made antimatter still owes a source for that light and that ratio. Equal numbers of dark and ordinary patterns is not given; it would need a rule for how the beginning picks a kind. Since a channel-less pattern cannot annihilate, whatever the beginning made of it is all there is. Grade: shape. It would be a number with a mass as input, not a continuous number from the primitives; and a channel-less 5 GeV pattern would show only through gravity, where 5 GeV is hard to tell from heavier.

**The photon ratio, read.** Three things follow when the hunch is held against the standard history.
- *What survives is a net excess, not "no antimatter".* Once the early universe is hot enough (in the quark era and down to a few tens of MeV for nucleons), light makes matter–antimatter pairs and pairs make light, in balance, whatever the beginning left. Only the net count of matter over antimatter is kept through that era. So the hunch's lasting content is the excess: one matter pattern per saturated place, with any antimatter later made in pairs from light. Channel-less dark patterns are not remade this way, since light cannot reach them.
- *The ratio becomes a question about the beginning.* With the excess fixed at the start, η = n_B/n_γ ≈ 6 × 10⁻¹⁰ is the number of matter patterns per quantum of light today, fixed since electron–positron annihilation a few seconds in: about 1.6 × 10⁹ quanta of light per matter pattern. The hunch owes a reason the beginning's possibility went almost entirely to light. It gives none yet; η is a continuous number, like 1/137, and stays a silence.
- *One sign everywhere.* If each saturated place chose matter or antimatter on its own, the universe would be patches of each, and their borders would glow in gamma rays; such patches are excluded out to about a gigaparsec, near the size of the visible universe (Cohen, De Rújula and Glashow 1998). Standard physics answers with a slight bias between matter and antimatter (Sakharov's conditions: baryon-number violation, C and CP violation, departure from thermal equilibrium). CO's beginning offers another answer: if it was one event, a single timeless ball turning definite at many places at once, the sign could be chosen once for all places. That would give one sign everywhere without a bias, but says nothing about η.

**Read against the rules as they now stand (2026-11-11).** Four consequences for the channel-less pattern, with nothing added; each is a reading of the shape and no stronger than it.
- *It gravitates with the same strength and falls the same way, at linear order.* Matter's response to the field is fixed by no backdrop applied to the walk (P7e, chosen, candidate) to the single coupling −½ h_μν T^μν, with T the pattern's own stress tensor, in the continuum at linear order. So, to that order, there is no separate dark gravity and no dark fifth force from the gravity side: the equivalence principle holds for dark matter as for ordinary matter. Beyond linear order, as for all matter, it rests on the nonlinear rule.
- *New places make none.* Places born after the beginning come on the possibility side, with no commitments (chosen for void births), so expansion does not make dark patterns that way. Other routes (gravitational pair production, or production from ordinary matter through gravity) are not excluded: CO has no rule for number change yet (priced, held). If the count of dark patterns is conserved and they are slow, their density falls as a⁻³, as cold dark matter's must.
- *It is free of the frame problem's loop split.* The frame problem's danger is interaction loops shifting each species' speed at order α/π. A channel-less pattern has no such loops. Two shifts remain: the lattice's own tree-level, mass-dependent excess (s = ½ r·mcℓ/ħ, appendix §3; about 3 × 10⁻¹⁵ for a 5 GeV pattern at the photon bound on ℓ, 2 × 10⁻¹⁶ at the candidate synchrotron bound), and gravitational loops, whose size depends on the cutoff and is not computed. Present bounds on Lorentz violation in the dark sector are loose.
- *Its elementary pattern is a fermion, so it cannot be ultralight, given CO's present inventory.* Every elementary pattern CO builds is a walk pattern, spin ½ (appendix §3); bosons arise as the light on the links and as composites of an even number of patterns (helium-4, P8b). In the strict shape, with no channel at all, dark patterns bind with nothing except through gravity, so they form no light bosonic composites, and CO has no channel-less field on the links. Then CO's dark matter is fermionic, and the Pauli principle bounds its mass from below through how densely dwarf galaxies pack it: m ≥ 0.13 keV at 95% confidence, model-independent (Alvey et al., MNRAS 501, 1188 (2021)). Ultralight bosonic ("fuzzy") dark matter, m ~ 10⁻²² eV, is excluded given this inventory; a clear detection of wave-like dark matter at such masses would rule the strict shape out, or call for a channel-less field CO does not have. A dark–dark channel (as the superfluid doodle below would need) would be an addition, and would allow bosonic composites, though their constituents would still obey the bound. The equal-numbers relation's ~5 GeV sits far above it.

**The photon ratio against the beginning's epoch (2026-11-11, a sketch with stated assumptions).** The beginning puts every place over capacity; renewals spread possibility over new places until every place is under capacity, after N_exit = N₀ + E₀/(P_max − P_b) places (space and gravity record).

*Assumptions, none of them rulings:* (a) the excess possibility ends as light; (b) a quantum of possibility per tick carries the hop's energy ħc/ℓ; (c) the light comes to heat balance on the network and is then carried down adiabatically, with no large later entropy production; (d) the matter count made at the beginning survives a bath at or above the cutoff (CO has no number-change rule, so wash-out is unruled).

- *Light per place.* A place holds about P_max quanta. With two modes per place (two polarisations) in classical equipartition, kT ≈ (P_max/ħ)(ħc/ℓ)/2, at or above the cutoff. The entropy per place is then S ≈ 2[ln(P_max/ħ) + 1]; carried down adiabatically, it becomes about S/3.6 photons per place after cooling below the cutoff: of order 1–10 for P_max/ħ between about 5 and 10⁴, about 50 for 10⁴⁰.
- *One pattern per saturated place: not excluded (corrected).* If every place that is over capacity during the epoch makes one pattern (the hunch read literally), there are of order 0.02 to 1 patterns per photon. That was first read as η and excluded; but η counts baryons per photon, not all patterns. Relic neutrinos and baryons together number about 0.8 per photon today (336 against 411 cm⁻³), inside that range. So the literal hunch fits if almost all first patterns are neutrinos and about one in 10⁹ is a baryon; it then needs a rule for which kind a place makes, and it gives the relic neutrinos one sign (not testable through N_eff; Majorana neutrinos have none). **Corrected again (13 Nov): excluded unless almost all its patterns are later erased.** Above about 1 MeV, weak interactions keep neutrinos in balance with the hot bath, so the 336 cm⁻³ relic neutrinos and antineutrinos are made thermally whatever the beginning left; only a net one-sign excess would survive, as a lepton asymmetry. An excess of 0.2–1 per photon means neutrino chemical potentials of order one, while big-bang nucleosynthesis allows only |ξ| ≲ 0.03–0.1 with the flavours alike (0.02–0.07 per photon), and such an excess would also raise N_eff (so "not testable through N_eff" was wrong). Dark patterns cannot carry the count either: 0.1–1 per photon needs a mass near 3–30 eV, below the 0.13 keV Pauli bound. **Narrowed (24 Nov):** with the flavours unequal the data allow a net excess up to about 0.19 per photon (−0.12 < L < 0.13 per T_cm³, 95%; arXiv:2510.02438). So the hunch is excluded above about 0.2 one-sign neutrinos per photon (unless both signs are made and later erased in pairs), and not excluded at its low end: 0.02–0.07 per photon with the flavours alike, up to 0.19 only with a particular pattern of flavours, which the hunch would have to supply, and near the edge of what N_eff allows. It is then the large-lepton-number case of the first ball's count below: first places about as many as the places at the epoch's end, and no growth. The net lepton number tells the two apart. (In the standard theory a lepton number that large, present above the electroweak scale, would be turned into baryons; CO has no such process.)
- *What survives: matter from the first places, light from all.* If only the first N₀ places, those of the first commitments, make matter, and the light ends spread over all N_exit places, then

η ≈ (N₀ / N_exit) × (0.1 to 1) ≈ N₀(P_max − P_b)/E₀ × (0.1 to 1),

and η = 6 × 10⁻¹⁰ asks N_exit/N₀ ≈ 10⁸–10⁹: about 18–21 e-folds in the number of places, about 6–7 in length. The photon ratio would then be the first excess E₀ measured in units of N₀(P_max − P_b): how many-fold the places multiplied during the beginning's epoch. Two cautions: why only the first places make matter is now a ruling (C7, below), not a derivation; and 6–7 e-folds in length is far short of the ~60 that standard inflation uses for the horizon and flatness problems (a comparison of size only: in standard physics η is set after reheating, not by inflation's dilution); whether CO needs those 60 is open (its beginning is one event).

*Which places make matter (chosen, candidate, C7).* Only the first commitments, out of the beginning, start renewing patterns; a commitment forced later in the epoch by over-capacity is a single event, leaving record and releasing the rest in transit. With C7's pairs clause, the net count the first commitments set is kept, which supplies assumption (d).

Grade: the literal hunch (one pattern per saturated place, all places) is excluded by the lepton-asymmetry bound above about 0.2 per photon unless almost all its patterns are later erased, and not excluded below that, given unequal flavours (24 Nov); it needs a rule for kinds (and kinds change only through the weak interaction, a silence in CO). With the C7 ruling (the other reading), the first-places version is a shape: η ≈ N₀(P_max − P_b)/E₀ × (0.1–1), relating η to the beginning's first excess, given (a)–(c), with (d) supplied by C7's pairs clause (candidate); the value of η is not derived (N₀, E₀ and P_max are open).

*Read further (2026-11-13, space and gravity record).* With the gap P_max − P_b above ħ (light must pass), the relation bounds the first excess per first commitment: E₀/N₀ ≳ 1.7 × 10⁸–1.7 × 10⁹ ħ, about 3 × 10⁷–3 × 10⁸ times capacity for P_max ≈ 6ħ (conditional; flags in the record). So the matter-to-light ratio is really a measurement of how far over capacity the very first commitments were. N₀ and P_max are not pinned.

**The story of threes.** Ordinary matter: patterns that renew with channels, in three families. Dark matter: patterns that renew without. Whether the dark kind also comes in three is open.

**Doodles, flagged as doodles.**
- *MOND's scale (excluded, conditional on the nonlinear rule's premises).* MOND's acceleration a₀ ≈ 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m s⁻² is close to cH₀/2π. Restated in CO's terms (a = c²∇h, h ≈ GM/(c²r)), a ≈ cH is where a body's held share falls to h ≈ Hr/c. The doodle hoped a falling floor of possibility would act there; three readings close it. (1) The floor does not fall: newborn places bring their own share (the birth ruling), so P∞ is held; and a uniform floor would make no force anyway. (2) Bound regions add no places, and void births come in place and make no field, so births do not disturb a galaxy's field. (3) Beyond linear order the relations' cost is Einstein–Hilbert plus Λ (consistent deformation, given no backdrop at every order, locality and two derivatives, and Lorentz-invariant interactions); expansion then acts on a galaxy only as in GR with Λ, at accelerations of order H²r, about 10⁻⁵ of a₀ at galactic radii (H²r/a₀ = 2πHr/c ≈ 4 × 10⁻⁵ at 30 kpc). A MOND-like change at a ≈ a₀ would need a nonlocal term, which locality excludes, or an extra field (as in AQUAL, TeVeS or AeST), which CO's present inventory lacks. So, within CO as it stands, galaxies' extra pull must be matter (the channel-less pattern above), and the a₀ ≈ cH₀ coincidence stays unexplained, as in ΛCDM.
- *The network's edge.* An older picture: at the network's edge (the visible horizon) something overcomes weak gravity, and in the Bullet Cluster the collision jammed the network into a knot that carries the extra pull. The horizon half has a well-known relative: Verlinde's emergent gravity (SciPost Phys. 2, 016 (2017)) derives an apparent dark matter from the de Sitter horizon, with a₀ tied to cH₀. It fits galaxy weak lensing in one test (Brouwer et al. 2017) and has trouble with clusters and with the timing of structure. The knot half would have to travel with the galaxies and not the gas, as the Bullet Cluster's lensing mass does, and would also have to supply the missing mass of clusters that have not merged in billions of years, which show it as well (lensing, X-ray gas held up by its own pressure, galaxy speeds). A knot made only in mergers cannot. CO's which-way field is a U(1) phase, which allows vortex lines but no point defects; CO has no field of directions on a sphere.
- *Superfluid dark matter.* Berezhiani and Khoury (2015) propose dark matter that condenses in galaxies and acts like MOND there, while acting as ordinary dark matter in clusters and in the early universe. Binding among dark patterns would need a dark–dark channel, an addition to the strict shape (see above), but the model's MOND-like force needs a phonon coupling to ordinary matter, which is a channel, and its condensate needs heat balance among dark patterns; how it fits the shape above is open.

*What the shape must be, as far as the rules reach.* A fermion with no channel, made at the beginning, collisionless, never detected except through gravity; cold, which observation requires and the rules do not yet give.
- *Mass:* at least 0.13 keV whatever made it (Pauli's principle in dwarf galaxies; 0.41 keV for a thermal relic). The few-keV bounds from small-scale structure (1.8 keV from galaxy counts at high redshift, several keV from the Lyman-α forest and lensing, for thermal warm dark matter) bound its speed, not its mass: a pattern that never met a heat bath has whatever speed the beginning gave it. No upper bound from the rules below the hop scale. The one CO-shaped number comes from equal numbers at the beginning with no dark anti-partners (the hunch above, a shape), and depends on what is counted: 5.0 GeV for one dark pattern per baryon, 1.7 GeV per quark (the patterns, by C9), 1.3 GeV per quark and electron together.
- *What would rule the shape out:* any confirmed non-gravitational signal of dark matter (a direct-detection event, a decay or annihilation line, production at a collider); wave-like dark matter; a measured self-interaction; dark matter falling differently from ordinary matter beyond the lattice's small excess.
- *What could still narrow it, through gravity alone:* cores set by Pauli pressure in the smallest galaxies (if the mass is near the keV bound); the cut-off in small-scale structure (its speed at the beginning).

**Why it would be cold (21 Nov 2026, desk; run C11).** A free pattern's momentum falls as space stretches, exactly as the light's does. So a dark pattern made at the beginning with a typical momentum f times the light's then (f ≤ 1) has today

$$v \approx 3.15\,f\,\frac{k T_x}{m c},\qquad T_x = T_\gamma\Big(\frac{3.91}{g_{\text{then}}}\Big)^{1/3} \approx 0.9\ \text{K}$$

(g_then the number of kinds of light then, 106.75 for the known particles; the dark pattern shares none of the light's later heating). Structure's bounds on warm dark matter, read as speeds today (exact for a thermal spread of momenta, rough otherwise), are v < 5 × 10⁻⁸ c (galaxy counts at high redshift) and 1–2 × 10⁻⁸ c (the Lyman-α forest, depending on the gas's history). Then:
- cold enough needs m ≳ 5 keV (galaxy counts) to 11–19 keV (Lyman-α) at f = 1, less for smaller f or more kinds of light at the start; a 5 GeV pattern moves at 5 × 10⁻¹⁴ c today;
- at the Pauli bound, 0.13 keV, it would move at 2 × 10⁻⁶ c: too fast unless f ≲ 0.01;
- even born at one place with the hop's momentum ħ/ℓ, a 5 GeV pattern reaches 3 × 10⁻⁹ c after a stretch of 10²⁴–10²⁶ in length, less than the light's own stretch from hop-scale heat to today (10²⁹–10³¹).

So, given a start no faster than the light's, no reason beyond the stretching is needed: a pattern heavier than about 5–19 keV is cold today because the stretching that cooled the light cooled it too. It was never in balance with the light (a thermal count would be about 90 times too many at 10 keV). The question moves to number: a 5 GeV pattern must be as rare as baryons, 6 × 10⁻¹⁰ per photon; a 10 keV one, 3 × 10⁻⁴ per photon. Conditional on the dark pattern's starting momentum not exceeding the light's, and on the light having been at or near hop-scale heat (assumptions (b), (c) above). Grade: reading of the shape.
