# The frame with loops

**The problem.** CO's matter walks on a lattice with a bounded number of neighbours, and any such lattice picks a frame (Bombelli–Henson–Sorkin 2009; the finite-group argument in the matter record). Gravity at long distances does not see it: Einstein's equations come back through Lovelock's theorem, which assumes a Lorentzian metric as the only field, and with it local Lorentz symmetry. Matter's loops do see it: on a frame-picking lattice they give each species its own top speed at order α/π ≈ 2 × 10⁻³ (Collins et al. 2004, by analogy for QED), against bounds near 5 × 10⁻²⁰. With number change held, CO has no loops yet; nature has them, so the problem is deferred, not avoided.


## Technical statement

**Setup.** Each species i propagates on the network with low-energy dispersion

E² = p²c_i² + m_i²c_i⁴ + O(p³c²ℓ/ħ),

where the c_i are Coleman and Glashow's dimension-four parametrisation and the p³ term is dimension five (Myers–Pospelov 2003). On CO's palindromic Wilson walk all species hop on the same network with the same hop rule, so at tree level c_i = c for every species, and the leading Lorentz-violating term is dimension five, a massive pattern outrunning a massless one by s ≈ 0.14·mcℓ/ħ (run L1b), which the Crab's PeV electrons bound to ℓ ≲ 1.3 × 10⁻³¹ m.

**The loop problem.** With interactions, self-energies computed with the network as regulator shift each species' limiting speed at dimension four:

δc_i/c ~ (α_i/π) ξ_i,

with ξ_i of order one and set by the regulator's shape, not suppressed by ℓ (Collins, Pérez, Sudarsky, Urrutia and Vucetich, PRL 93, 191301 (2004), shown for Yukawa theory). The Crab's PeV electrons bound c_e − c_γ < ~3 × 10⁻²⁰ c (Li and Ma, PLB 829, 137034 (2022)); the opposite sign, from photon decay, is weaker, near 10⁻¹⁹. Then |ξ_e − ξ_γ| ≲ 10⁻¹⁷: a tuning, not an approximation.

**CO's own fork.** CO's G formula, G = δc³ℓ²/(4πħ) with δ ≤ 1 (a kept fraction), gives ℓ = ℓ_P√(4π/δ) ≥ √(4π) ℓ_P ≈ 3.5 ℓ_P: the network's scale sits at or below the Planck energy, never above it. The suppressions open to matter need the Lorentz-violating scale far above the scale where light and gravity take their low-energy form (below); the one that works the other way (frame only in gravity) is not open to CO.

**Exits** (record, 2026-11-10):

**What a CO-shaped protecting symmetry must do.** Act exactly on a finite-degree network in real time; relate the walk (matter) and the link (light), since the dangerous difference is between them; survive the Wilson term; and force equal dimension-four speeds as a consequence, as hypercubic symmetry does on a Euclidean lattice. None is known. Failing one, the next route is Way B (discreteness in values, not places), which reverses the classical-amounts ruling and with it the falsifier.

## In plain words

**The exits, priced** (matter record, 2026-11-10).
- A substrate that picks no frame: not available (sprinkling excluded; Way B parked).
- Flow to Lorentz invariance at low energy (Chadha–Nielsen 1983; Anber–Donoghue 2011): too slow for QED.
- The frame only in gravity (Pospelov–Shang 2012): not available; matter walks on the substrate.
- A symmetry pairing fermions and bosons (Groot Nibbelink–Pospelov 2005): works for breaking below about 10⁷ GeV, but needs partners CO does not have. Shape, outside CO.
- Tuning the bare speeds to about 1 part in 10¹⁷: permitted.

CO stands where any real-time lattice field theory stands: permitted with a tuning.

**The CO-shaped exit, if there is one.** A real-time discrete structure with a symmetry that ties species' speeds together, without a frame-free substrate. None is known. What it would have to do:
- act in real time, step by step, not only in a Euclidean (imaginary-time) lattice, where hypercubic symmetry already rules out frame-picking terms up to dimension four;
- relate the walk (matter) and the link (light), since the dangerous speed difference is between them;
- survive the walk's Wilson term and its doublers.

**Directions, ungraded.**
- *Exact lattice supersymmetry.* Some lattice theories keep part of a fermion–boson symmetry exactly at finite spacing (twisted constructions; Catterall, Kaplan and Ünsal, Phys. Rep. 484, 71 (2009)). They are Euclidean, and they need partners, but they show such a symmetry can live on a lattice.
- *A common origin for walk and link.* If light were made from the same walk as matter rather than carried separately on links, both would inherit one light cone at tree level. Whether loops would keep them together is the question; in condensed matter, emergent gauge fields inherit the light cone of the fermions that make them, but several species share one only if their Fermi points share a metric, which needs extra symmetry (Volovik 2003).
- *A discrete real-time analogue of hypercubic symmetry.* Discrete spacetime point sets can carry integral Lorentz transformations (Schild 1948), but no finite-degree graph keeps them. A structure in between, finite-degree but with a symmetry that mixes the tick with the hops, is the unexplored case.
