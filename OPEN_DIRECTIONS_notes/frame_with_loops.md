# The frame with loops

**The problem.** CO's matter walks on a lattice with a bounded number of neighbours, and any such lattice picks a frame (Bombelli–Henson–Sorkin 2009; the finite-group argument in the matter record). Gravity's nonlinear rule rests on the same question: Einstein's equations come back by consistent deformation (Wald; Boulanger–Damour–Gualtieri–Henneaux), which assumes Lorentz-invariant interactions. Matter's loops do see it: on a frame-picking lattice they give each species its own top speed at order α/π ≈ 2 × 10⁻³ (Collins et al. 2004, by analogy for QED), against bounds near 5 × 10⁻²⁰. With number change held, CO has no loops yet; nature has them, so the problem is deferred, not avoided.


## Technical statement

**Setup.** Each species i propagates on the network with low-energy dispersion

E² = p²c_i² + m_i²c_i⁴ + O(p³c²ℓ/ħ),

where the c_i are Coleman and Glashow's dimension-four parametrisation and the p³ term is dimension five (Myers–Pospelov 2003). On CO's palindromic Wilson walk all species hop on the same network with the same hop rule, so at tree level c_i = c for every species, and the leading Lorentz-violating term is dimension five, a massive pattern outrunning a massless one by s ≈ 0.14·mcℓ/ħ (run L1b), which the Crab's PeV electrons bound to ℓ ≲ 1.3 × 10⁻³¹ m.

**The loop problem.** With interactions, self-energies computed with the network as regulator shift each species' limiting speed at dimension four:

δc_i/c ~ (α_i/π) ξ_i,

with ξ_i of order one and set by the regulator's shape, not suppressed by ℓ (Collins, Pérez, Sudarsky, Urrutia and Vucetich, PRL 93, 191301 (2004), shown for Yukawa theory). The Crab's PeV electrons bound c_e − c_γ < ~3 × 10⁻²⁰ c (Li and Ma, PLB 829, 137034 (2022)); the opposite sign, from photon decay, is weaker, near 10⁻¹⁹. Then |ξ_e − ξ_γ| ≲ 10⁻¹⁷: a tuning, not an approximation.

**CO's own fork.** CO's G formula, G = δc³ℓ²/(4πħ) with δ ≤ 1 (a kept fraction), gives ℓ = ℓ_P√(4π/δ) ≥ √(4π) ℓ_P ≈ 3.5 ℓ_P: the network's scale sits at or below the Planck energy, never above it. The suppressions open to matter need the Lorentz-violating scale far above the scale where light and gravity take their low-energy form (below); the one that works the other way (frame only in gravity) is not open to CO.

**What a CO-shaped protecting symmetry must do.** Act exactly on a finite-degree network in real time; relate the walk (matter) and the link (light), since the dangerous difference is between them; survive the Wilson term; and force equal dimension-four speeds as a consequence, as hypercubic symmetry does on a Euclidean lattice. None is known. Failing one, the next route is Way B (discreteness in values, not places), which reverses the classical-amounts ruling and with it the falsifier.

## In plain words

**The exits, priced** (matter record, 2026-11-10).
- A substrate that picks no frame: not available (sprinkling excluded; Way B with quantum values does not reach the loop problem; with a quantum graph, unsettled and costs the substrate as ruled).
- Flow to Lorentz invariance at low energy (Chadha–Nielsen 1983; Anber–Donoghue 2011): too slow for QED.
- The frame only in gravity (Pospelov–Shang 2012): not available; matter walks on the substrate.
- A symmetry pairing fermions and bosons (Groot Nibbelink–Pospelov, PRL 94, 081601 (2005)): works for breaking below about 10⁷ GeV, but needs partners CO does not have. Shape, outside CO.
- Tuning the bare speeds to about 1 part in 10¹⁷: permitted.

CO stands where any real-time lattice field theory stands: permitted with a tuning.

**The CO-shaped exit, if there is one.** A real-time discrete structure with a symmetry that ties species' speeds together, without a frame-free substrate. None is known. What it would have to do:
- act in real time, step by step, not only in a Euclidean (imaginary-time) lattice, where hypercubic symmetry already rules out frame-picking terms up to dimension four;
- relate the walk (matter) and the link (light), since the dangerous speed difference is between them;
- survive the walk's Wilson term and its doublers.

**Directions, ungraded.**
- *Exact lattice supersymmetry.* Some lattice theories keep part of a fermion–boson symmetry exactly at finite spacing (twisted constructions; Catterall, Kaplan and Ünsal, Phys. Rep. 484, 71 (2009)). They are Euclidean, and they need partners, but they show such a symmetry can live on a lattice.
- *A common origin for walk and link.* If light were made from the same walk as matter rather than carried separately on links, both would inherit one light cone at tree level. Whether loops would keep them together is the question; in condensed matter, emergent gauge fields inherit the light cone of the fermions that make them, but several species share one only if their Fermi points share a metric, which needs extra symmetry (Volovik 2003).
- *A discrete real-time analogue of hypercubic symmetry.* Discrete spacetime point sets can carry integral Lorentz transformations (Schild 1948), but no finite-degree graph keeps them. A structure in between, finite-degree but with a symmetry that mixes the tick with the hops, is the unexplored case.

## Symmetry search, step one: supersymmetry on a lattice (after cold reading)

**What exists.** Exact supersymmetry does live on lattices, in three forms:
- *Hamiltonian, real time, any graph:* H = {Q⁺, Q⁻} with nilpotent fermionic Q (Nicolai 1976; Fendley, Schoutens and de Boer, PRL 90, 120402 (2003)). Time is continuous; space is any graph, in any dimension.
- *Euclidean:* twisted constructions keep a nilpotent scalar part of the algebra exactly (Catterall, Kaplan and Ünsal 2009).
- *Discrete time:* supersymmetric quantum cellular automata have been written down (McGuigan, quant-ph/0307176), with doubling as a stated barrier; and every chirally symmetric one-particle walk has a supersymmetric structure (Suzuki 2019), which is a property of one walk, not a fermion–boson pairing.

**What a lattice can keep, and what that protects.** A lattice cannot keep the part of the algebra that closes on momentum, {Q, Q̄} ~ P_μ, because lattice translations are discrete. What it can keep is a charge that commutes with the step (or with H) and with lattice translations, carrying no crystal momentum. That forces partners in one multiplet to have the same energy at every lattice momentum, so their dispersions, and their top speeds, agree exactly, loops included, while the symmetry is unbroken.

**Why that does not reach the dangerous pair.** The speed difference that the data constrain hardest is between the electron and the photon. A supercharge that commutes with electric charge maps the electron (charge −1, spin ½) to a boson of charge −1, never to the photon (charge 0). So, for a supercharge carrying no electric charge, the electron and the photon cannot be partners in one multiplet (in the continuum this is forced for global supersymmetry, Haag–Łopuszański–Sohnius; on a lattice it is an assumption). In the continuum, supersymmetry protects them anyway, through the full algebra that closes on momentum (Groot Nibbelink–Pospelov 2005); that is exactly the part a lattice cannot keep exactly. A lattice-exact supersymmetry would protect electron against its scalar partner, and photon against its fermion partner, but not electron against photon.

**Grade.** Excluded, for a charge-neutral supercharge, as the CO-shaped protection in its direct form: the electron and the photon cannot share a multiplet. The indirect form is a silence on a lattice (whether a partial lattice supersymmetry with the point group forbids an electron–photon speed difference has no known result) and needs partners CO does not have. Step one of the search finds nothing for CO.

## The fork: can CO's lattice be finer than Planck?

From the sink's strength with a fixed amount q per hop, G = δc³ℓ²/(4πq) in general, so ℓ = ℓ_P√(4πq/(δħ)). A lattice finer than Planck (ℓ < ℓ_P) needs q < δħ/(4π) ≤ ħ/(4π): hops carrying less than 1/(4π) ≈ 0.08 of a quantum. The ruling q = ħ (hops move one quantum; it settles the degree of the network against R5) and δ ≤ 1 (a kept fraction) both forbid it. So ℓ ≥ √(4π) ℓ_P is structural, not a choice made along the way: in CO the lattice scale is tied to gravity's strength, and the two-scale protection (Lorentz-violating scale far above Planck) is closed unless gravity's strength comes from somewhere other than the sink's conductance. Induced gravity (Sakharov 1967) is one such source, but there 1/G ~ NΛ², so more species push the cutoff below Planck, not above.

**So.** The two direct routes found so far (lattice supersymmetry; a lattice finer than Planck) are closed for CO as it stands. By the author's plan, the next route was Way B: read 2026-11-10 (space and gravity record), it does not reach the loop problem with quantum values on a fixed graph, and with a quantum graph its status is unsettled and it costs the substrate as ruled. As ruled, CO's network picks a frame that matter's loops would see; no exit read so far removes it without tuning or a change of ruling.


**Sketch, ungraded (the author, 2026-11-11): the frame is where space is being made.** New places are born at the same rate everywhere in one frame, the one in which the expansion looks the same in every direction: the frame the microwave background picks out (Earth moves through it at about 370 km/s). So the lattice's rest frame has a physical home, and any speed differences between species would line up with motion through the microwave background. Dark energy alone picks no frame (empty space with only Λ looks the same to every observer); matter, radiation and the making of places do. This names the frame; it does not shrink the loop-induced speed differences, which remain the problem.

## The common-origin exit, tested (12 Nov; reading)

**The question** (from the negative-space map). If the gauge fields are made from the walk's parts (the parton poke for colour; induced fields generally), do they inherit the walk's light cone, so that walk and link speeds cannot split?

**At tree level, yes; past that, no.** A gauge field with no cost of its own gets one from the loops of the fermions that make it:

1/g²_ind ≈ (Σ_i q_i² / 12π²) ln(1/(m_i ℓ)).

The coefficients of the electric and magnetic parts (E² and B²), whose ratio is the field's speed, each carry this logarithm with the same weight. The logarithm is fed equally by every scale up to the hop scale, though, and near the hop scale the walk's dispersion is not Lorentz invariant, so the two coefficients pick up different finite parts of order one. The induced field's speed then differs from the walk's by

|c_γ − c_f|/c ~ O(1)/ln(1/(mℓ)) ≈ 1/40 (electron, ℓ ~ 10⁻³¹ m),

far worse than the α/π split it was meant to cure, against bounds near 10⁻²⁰. This is the frame table's "common origin" row seen from the gauge side: Volovik's protection needs the Lorentz-violating scale far above the scale at which the field's cost is made, and CO has one scale.

**A second, lower scale does not rescue it.** If the fields formed only below some Λ ≪ ħc/ℓ, loops would be cut at Λ and the split would fall to α(Λℓ)². But the fermions that induce the field exist, with their coupling, all the way to the hop scale; the redundancy is exact at every scale, so the induced cost is still fed from there.

**And the parton poke already gave the field its own cost.** Its cold read found that a weak coupling at the hop scale (needed for asymptotic freedom) requires the gauge field's own quadratic cost, posited, not induced. A field mostly carried by its own cost is a carrier beside the walk, and its speed is free again.

**Result.** The common-origin exit closes for CO: induced fields split at O(1/ln), and the fields CO needs carry their own cost. The exits table now has one unexplored entry, a real-time symmetry mixing the tick with the hops, plus tuning (permitted, not accepted by the author). Grade: reading.
