# Triangles and gears

A picture of the quarks and the lightest nuclei.

Everything this picture is matched against is known or observed. Physics has the facts and the theory that computes them; it has no agreed picture of what is there underneath. CO tests itself as that connecting story. The picture is posited: a way the facts could make sense in CO's terms. Its rules are chosen, and possibility does not say why it behaves this way. Nothing here is derived, and nothing here is new physics.

## The picture

- A quark is a triangle that carries its kind, up or down.
- A nucleon is three triangles: one point down, set between two that point up. The one in the middle is the odd quark, the two beside it the alike pair. A down between two ups is the proton; an up between two downs is the neutron. The two are one shape with the kinds swapped.
- The group shows a different face each way: one side on top (the odd quark's), two sides underneath (the alike pair's), and a side with a corner to the left and to the right.

## The rules

1. **One fits two.** A single side bonds to a double side of the same kind. Together they make three.
2. **Triangles that touch along a side turn opposite ways.** Each triangle turns one of two ways; the turning is its spin. A touching that breaks this rule is weak: a join that breaks it does not hold alone.
3. **Alike ones are opposite.** Two protons in one bound body turn opposite ways, and two neutrons likewise. This is exclusion, put in. With two ways of turning, one body holds at most two of each.

A nucleon's own turning is its alike pair's.

## What follows, against what is observed

| In the picture | Observed |
|---|---|
| Inside a nucleon the two alike quarks each touch the odd one and not each other. So the alike pair turns together and the odd one against: a net of one turn | The proton and neutron have spin ½, and in the quark model the alike pair is spin-aligned. The fixed arrangement gives the ratio of the two magnetic moments as −5/4; the quark model's full mixture gives −3/2; measured −1.46 |
| Three quarks all turning one way break rule 2 | The Δ, three quarks with spins aligned, is 293 MeV heavier than the nucleon and short-lived |
| Stacked, a proton's two ups fit a neutron's one up, and a neutron's two downs fit a proton's one down. Two protons do not fit, nor two neutrons | The deuteron is the only bound pair |
| The two stackings are the same drawing with the kinds swapped | Nuclear forces are nearly the same with protons and neutrons swapped (helium-3 and tritium are mirror partners), and part of the force exchanges the two |
| At a join the single touches both of the pair, so it turns against them. That makes the two nucleons turn the same way. With the nucleons opposed the three at the join all turn alike: a weak join | The deuteron has spin 1. The pair with spins opposed is not bound, though it nearly is |
| Three: the two alike nucleons are opposite (rule 3), so one join is full and one is weak, and the spin left over is the unpaired nucleon's | Helium-3: spin ½, magnetic moment −2.13 against the neutron's −1.91. Tritium: spin ½, 2.98 against the proton's 2.79 |
| Four, the row bent round into a ring (not flat; see below), joins full and weak by turns: every bonding face is used and the spins cancel. Rule 3 allows no larger body | Helium-4: spin 0, bound far more tightly than its neighbours (7.1 MeV a nucleon against 1.1 for the deuteron and 2.6 to 2.8 for the threes) |
| A closed ring has no single or double side left: a fifth cannot bond, and two rings do not bond by rule 1 | No stable nucleus of five; beryllium-8 falls apart into two helium-4 |

## Where it misses

- **Beyond four there is nothing.** By rule 3 a body holds two of each, and a closed ring bonds to nothing. Lithium, beryllium, carbon and the rest need another state for the extra particles and another kind of join. In the standard account the extra particles take the next state, which carries one unit of orbital motion; the rules have no orbital motion (a trial of it is below).
- **The ring does not close flat.** Each stacked nucleon sits above the last, so four in a row end with a single side free at the top and its matching double side free at the bottom. Joining those two means bending the row round out of the page into a band, made so far only in paper.
- **A second join is a trial** (below).
- **The three at a join are not a whole.** Three alike quarks, two one way and one the other, is the arrangement quark exclusion forbids for a real three-quark particle.
- **Nothing can make one nucleon heavier.** Swapping the kinds turns the proton into the neutron exactly, joins and all, so no rule here can make one heavier. The neutron is heavier by 1.29 MeV, 0.14%; in the standard account that is the down quark's larger mass, partly offset by the electric energy of the proton's charge (arXiv:1406.4088). The picture needs two kinds that are not quite alike.
- **No charge, no parity, no shape.** Tritium and helium-3 differ in binding, heavy nuclei hold more neutrons than protons, and the deuteron is slightly stretched; none of these is in the picture.
- **No masses and no energies** come out, only yes or no and the spins.

## A trial: rolling

Not a rule yet. A closed ring's turnings cancel, so as a wheel it does not turn. A turning wheel pressed against a still one has to roll round it, and rolling round the outside it circles the same way it turns. So a nucleon outside a ring circles it, and the circling adds to its turning:

spin = 1 + ½ = 3/2

| Given by rolling | Observed |
|---|---|
| One outside a ring, or the one left over of three: spin 3/2 | Lithium-7, beryllium-7, beryllium-9, boron-11, carbon-11: 3/2. Helium-5 and lithium-5 do not hold; their lowest state is 3/2 |
| Two alike outside, turning opposite: 0 | Helium-6, beryllium-10: 0 |
| A proton and neutron outside. Joined, turning alike: 3 if they circle the same way round; 1 if they circle opposite ways and the circling cancels. Turning opposite, each circling with its turning: 0 (circling the same way: 2) | The three lowest states of lithium-6 are 1, 3, 0 and of boron-10 3, 1, 0. Lithium-6's magnetic moment is 0.82 against the deuteron's 0.86 |
| Circling and turning in the same sense | In nuclei the state with the two aligned lies lower, the reverse of the atom's order. The standard account took this sign from the data (1949) |

Where it misses. The order: nothing says why the pair cancels its circling beside one ring and not between two, and in the spin 1 state one of the two circles against its turning, so rolling is a preference and not a must. Everything odd beyond carbon-12: carbon-13, nitrogen-13, nitrogen-15 and oxygen-15 have spin ½ and nitrogen-14 has 1, where rolling gives 3/2 and 3; the standard account fills the aligned state at twelve, and rolling never fills. Magnetic moments are not given (no charge); borrowing the textbook single-particle values, lithium-7 is 3.79 against 3.26, beryllium-9 −1.91 against −1.18, boron-10 1.88 against 1.80. Nuclei with more than two alike outside the rings (helium-8, lithium-8, lithium-9) cannot be built.

What is put in. The one unit of circling. That the nucleon rolls outside the ring: rolling inside would circle the other way, so the sign comes with that choice. That with two rings the circling is still one unit: a wheel held between two still wheels would not roll. What holds a nucleon to a ring is the next trial. Desk check: [`joins_and_rolling.py`](../RECORD_OF_METHOD/NUCLEUS_notes/joins_and_rolling.py).

## A trial: the flank join

Not a rule yet. A nucleon's two slanted flanks are each one side of an alike quark. Flank on flank is one side on one side: a second join, not rule 1's one fits two, and unlike kinds can meet. By rule 2 the two nucleons turn opposite ways. One flank join does not hold two parts together; two do. It obeys rule 2 and still does not hold alone: that is put in, not taken from rule 2. A piece is a ring or a single nucleon.

| Given | Observed |
|---|---|
| One outside a ring, or two alike nucleons alone: one flank join, not held | Helium-5 and lithium-5 do not hold; two protons and two neutrons do not |
| A ring and two alike nucleons, each meeting the other two: held, though no two of the three hold alone | Helium-6 holds, and no two of its three pieces do |
| Ring with ring: one band turned over, the two rims mesh all the way round, four flank joins. In one setting of the four every join is between nucleons turning opposite ways, a proton on a neutron each time when the two rings are of the same sort (like on like when one is the other with the kinds swapped). Held, and more bands stack on without limit | Carbon-12 and oxygen-16 hold. Beryllium-8 falls apart, by 0.09 MeV |
| A joined pair or three with a flank each on a ring: held | Lithium-6, lithium-7 |

Where it misses. It holds beryllium-8, beryllium-6 and boron-9, which fall apart; the picture has no charge, and beryllium-8 is unbound through the repulsion between its two helium-4. So with this join the gap at eight is no longer given, and nothing in the picture ends a stack of rings. Beryllium-9 is not placed: where a neutron seats on two meshed bands is not tried. Counting joins does not rank arrangements: helium-6's three flank joins are worth 0.97 MeV, beryllium-8's four −0.09, carbon-12's eight 7.27.

What is put in. That one does not hold and two do. Two flat nucleons can share only one. How many flanks meet on a ring is read off a paper model, not put in: on a paper band of four the flanks lie on the two rims, each rim a sawtooth of four flanks all slanting the same way round; a flat nucleon seats one flank on it, not two; a second band turned over meshes with all four. With the nucleons labelled by kind and turning, a ring reads p↑ n↑ p↓ n↓ in stacking order, or p↑ n↓ p↓ n↑: the full joins are either the proton-on-neutron ones or the neutron-on-proton ones. The two are one ring with the kinds swapped, not mirror images; turned over, a ring reads as the other. Desk check of the settings: [`labelled_bands.py`](../RECORD_OF_METHOD/NUCLEUS_notes/labelled_bands.py). At each stacked join the single side covers the middle of the double side, leaving a bare half side at either end; no rule uses them. Desk check of the holding count, run on an earlier side-in-notch join with ring to ring allowed: [`join_count.py`](../RECORD_OF_METHOD/NUCLEUS_notes/join_count.py).

## The hidden gear

A picture of the turning itself, posited like the rest. A pattern of spin ½ turned once round comes back with its sign reversed, and turned twice comes back as it was. Draw the turning as a small gear meshed with a hidden gear twice its size: two turns of the small one are one turn of the hidden one. The hidden gear's position is the sign, and the sign is what routes agree or disagree on (routes meeting at a place add with their phases, P8; the sign under a swap is P8b). In the same drawing the part of colour that counts in threes would be a hidden gear of three positions, each quark moving it one step and three bringing it back; the rest of colour is not in the drawing. Nothing here follows from it.

## What is put in

Like kinds bond, with no reason given. Exclusion. Two ways of turning. That a join breaking rule 2 is weak and holds only beside a full one. The triangle and the count of three. The standard reasons are different: a force that depends on spin, and exclusion.

## Standing

Posited: a picture, not a derivation. Rules 1 and 2 were chosen to match the proton and the deuteron; exclusion and the weak join were added to reach three and four. With those four choices it gives the nucleon's spin, the one bound pair and its spin, the spins of the threes, helium-4's spin and, if the row bends shut, its closure, and nothing beyond four, which matches the gaps at five and eight (the flank trial gives up the gap at eight) and misses the rest. It matches known answers and says nothing new. A nucleus of four or fewer whose binding or spin the rules get wrong would count against it.

In CO's terms a bound whole is a now that keeps being shared (reading; [`how_patterns_combine.md`](how_patterns_combine.md)). The picture is one way to draw that.
