# Gravity from Counting Events

Oct 18, 2026 · Allen Proxmire

In the Commitment Ontology, time at a place is the count of events completing there, and matter slows that count around it. On CO's own event-layer simulations, the counting rules now give the exponential metric, with β = γ = 1, to the precision the runs reach. The event layer runs one way, and a clock's record averaged over time loses most of that order: time-symmetric laws as averages of one-way events. What fails is the field's dynamics: the well is carried by diffusion, so it cannot follow moving matter, and at the adopted scales it cannot form beyond about 0.3 μm. The static results in the table below are steady states that only a faster field rule (such as a deficit carried as a wave) would reach at physical distances.

## How it works

&#91;embedded content: from matter's draw to the metric · 6 steps\]

Matter's commitments draw possibility from their places. Conservation makes the outflow an exactly linear field of that draw, with no screening. A clock counts the possibility present, which grows exponentially with the outflow, so its rate is N = e^(−U) and two wells multiply. Light hops on ticks and needs free capacity, which sets the spatial part of the metric.

## The rules

Space is a lattice of places. Each holds an integer amount of possibility P, and nothing in the vacuum commits. Each tick, every unit hops to a random neighbour with a chance chosen so that a place sends out

```latex
f(P) = c \ln\left(1 + \frac{P}{p_s}\right), \qquad c = \frac{0.99}{\ln(1 + 1/p_s)}
```

The outflow is linear in P below a crossover p\_s and compares ratios above it. Matter is reserved pairs of neighbouring places. Each commitment takes one unit from each of its places, holds, and renews at once. Released units return uniformly. A commitment is also a clock: its hold ends when the possibility present at its places, summed over ticks, reaches a fixed amount.

```latex
\sum_{\text{ticks}} (P_a + P_b) \ \ge\ H = 2\hbar P_0
```

So a clock runs at a rate proportional to the possibility around it. Two results follow. First, conservation makes the mean outflow z = ⟨f(P)⟩ an exactly linear, unscreened field of matter's draw s:

```latex
\tfrac16 \sum_{y \sim x} z_y - z_x = s_x - \bar s
```

Second, the clock reads P, not z. In local equilibrium P = R(z), so the clock rate is N = R(z)/R(z₀) and

```latex
\beta = 1 + \frac{y''}{2\,y'^2}, \qquad y = \ln R(z)
```

When P ≫ p\_s, ln P is linear in z, so N = e^(−U) and β → 1, with β − 1 ≈ −0.45 p\_s/P₀. Light and other patterns of possibility hop on their place's ticks and need free capacity there, which fixes the spatial part:

```latex
ds^2 = -N^2\,dt^2 + N^{-2}\,d\mathbf{x}^2, \qquad \frac{1}{N^4}\,\partial_t^2 \phi = \nabla^2 \phi - \frac{m^2}{N^2}\,\phi
```

## What is put in, and what is open

Some of the agreement is built in, not derived:

- **γ = 1** comes from the pattern rule (light needs ticks and free capacity), chosen to give it.
- **Twist charged on tick–relation loops at equal price** makes knots fall universally. It is Lorentz invariance put in by hand.
- **The ratio medium and the presence clock** were designed to give β = 1. Their achievement is passing the independent checks, not producing β = 1 unasked.
- **The scales are bounds, not predictions:** p\_s ≳ 4×10⁵ so that clocks agree to 10⁻⁶, and P₀ ≳ 2.3×10⁴ p\_s ≈ 10¹⁰ units per place for the binary-pulsar bound on ζ₂ (2000 p\_s for Nordtvedt's β part). Both are extrapolated from the engine's reach. They say matter is light next to the medium, which is also what weak gravity needs.

Open:

- the field's dynamics (Run 31): the engine's well forms and moves by diffusion. At the adopted scales a well would spread about 0.3 μm in the age of the universe, and a body at 370 km/s would keep its well within 0.01 of a place. The fix needs a rule: a deficit carried as a wave (same static wells, changes at c), or places that move with matter
- the strong field: the metric has no horizon (a throat at areal radius e M). Its shadow is 4.6% larger than GR's, which EHT allows at 2σ. Its static ringdown frequency is about 4% below Kerr's, roughly 5σ from GW250114 if that carries over to spinning remnants and CO's own waves. CO's Hawking-radiation papers assume a horizon this metric lacks; a static body can never make one, because its draw throttles itself, and even the idealised surface where possibility runs out is at zero temperature (Run 32) (Run 28)
- a preferred frame (the lattice has a universal tick), and how moving matter and stress source gravity. Rest mass in a potential already pulls with GR's weight (Run 30); moving matter must draw 1 + 2v² times its ticking rate, and the engine has no rule for moving matter yet
- knots built from the engine's own commitments
- patterns falling close in to deep wells, where the measuring method itself fails (Run 27)
