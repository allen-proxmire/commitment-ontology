# Nucleus

What the two primitives do not reach. The working record is in [`RECORD_OF_METHOD/`](RECORD_OF_METHOD/).

## The three open problems

The particle masses, the strength of the electromagnetic coupling (1/137), and colour. Each needs something not in the inventory.

## Colour

Colour is a three-component internal arrow whose orientation is a convention at each point; the gluon field compares conventions between neighbouring points. The only three-valued structure the primitives supply is spatial (the three lines through a place), and an SU(3) acting on the triad rotates with space: SO(3) is a subgroup of SU(3), not a factor, so no internal remainder survives. **The three lines of space are not colour.** An internal three-dimensional space is outside the inventory; the three conditions of possibility (free, in transit, engaged) are not interchangeable and cannot be one either.

## The bar for a third primitive

A third primitive is admissible on a named contradiction with the world: the rules make a proton an elementary pattern, while experiment makes it composite, bound by a force the rules lack, with most of its mass in the binding. It is worth admitting only if it cascades, bringing a second open problem with it. In the standard theory, colour with one coupling does cascade to the whole hadron spectrum; it does not reach the lepton masses or 1/137. The one geometric route to an internal three, Kaluza–Klein's places with an inside, gives a real triad and so SO(3), not SU(3); it is closed below ("The wall, stated").

What a third primitive must deliver: separable carriers, not three labels on one carrier. The evidence is deep-inelastic scattering (a single quark absorbing a photon incoherently, Bjorken scaling) and the gluon jets of three-jet events, which also show a gluonic degree of freedom carrying about half the proton's momentum; constituent-count scaling of flow is weaker and approximate (coalescence-interpreted; violated at the 20% level in lead collisions). A colour that is an orientation of a single pattern cannot give separable carriers. This excludes possibility in sorts as the candidate and asks for a binding whose release depends on the surroundings, and for whatever carries the other half of the momentum.

### Sorts of possibility, already costed

The obvious candidate is possibility in sorts: N kinds of possibility, indistinguishable under the rules, conserved together. That candidate has been run, in an earlier program by the same author, and its cost is known.

- **What it buys.** N indistinguishable, jointly conserved sorts force the gauge group U(N) = (SU(N) × U(1))/Z_N by uniqueness. Posit three sorts and SU(3) follows. This is a textbook result (the symmetry group of N identical conserved channels), restated; it is not a derivation of colour.
- **What it does not buy.** The count N is the input. No rule selects {1, 2, 3}; a symmetric N-sort multiplet is stable for every N, so nothing picks three. And the counts as posited give U(1) × U(2) × U(3), with three U(1) factors where the standard theory has one. The route does not land on the standard group without a further rule.
- **The one structural check.** The three multiplicities reduce to one number: the most mutually orthogonal sorts an internal state of dimension d can carry is d, so d = 3 gives groups up to SU(3) and nothing stable above it. The question "why three sorts" becomes "why is the internal state three-dimensional." The matter sector already fixes an internal state of dimension four (Clifford), so the check is whether that four can carry the three. It cannot. The four is an irreducible representation of the Clifford algebra; by Schur's lemma the only operators commuting with the walk's dynamics (α₁, α₂, α₃, β) are scalars, so the only internal symmetry the four supports is U(1), charge. (Massless, the commutant is two-dimensional, 1 and γ₅: two chiral U(1)s. The mass term reduces it to one; so does the Wilson term adopted in the matter sector (half-shift form), which breaks chiral symmetry explicitly as Wilson terms do.) Under rotations the four splits as 2 ⊕ 2, spin ½ twice, with no triplet. So the Clifford four is exhausted by charge; colour would need a new tensor factor, an internal three added to the inventory, not found in it. This sharpens the stop: two primitives give exactly one internal symmetry, U(1), and no more. (A stable SU(N ≥ 4) gauge symmetry would contradict the reduction, but none is seen, so this is consistency, not a test.) Check script: [`RECORD_OF_METHOD/NUCLEUS_notes/clifford_commutant.py`](RECORD_OF_METHOD/NUCLEUS_notes/clifford_commutant.py).
- **Independent confirmation of one closure.** That run also found the internal three distinct in kind from the spatial three, not reducible to it. Same conclusion as above, reached by a different route.

Net: sorts cascade exactly as far as SU(N) and stop. By the bar set here, a third primitive of this form is not yet earned.

## Quarks and gluons

*Shape, the author's working picture.* A quark is a pattern that renews, as an electron does, but of a second kind: ordinary matter is two kinds of commitment, distinguished by colour: leptons and quarks (each in three generations, unselected; the weak interaction that ties them together, and the neutrinos, are not in CO). The second kind carries a three-component colour state, and the relations between quarks carry a 3 × 3 matching between those states. Gluons are patterns of that colour field on relations, as light is a pattern of the which-way field; their colour content is a quark–antiquark pair's minus the singlet (the octet). A proton is three quarks bound by that field. That quarks commit is read from the table (mass is renewal rate; quarks carry a mass parameter, scheme-dependent, with no pole mass), but their standing as patterns cannot rest on that mass: with massless quarks the strong force still confines and the proton keeps most of its mass.

- **What stands.** Colour as bookkeeping whose relabelling is a slide that costs nothing: gauge redundancy, correctly. The comparison of bookkeeping between places is a gauge connection on relations; if such a matching exists, its form is fixed: a 3 × 3 unitary of unit determinant (SU(3)); its existence as a dynamical object is not.
- **What the picture lacks.** The three itself (measured: R-ratio, π⁰ → γγ, the Z's hadronic width; the falsifier of any route). The matching as a dynamical object on relations (posit (ii), "The triplet"); given it, the octet and gluon self-coupling (three- and four-gluon vertices) follow, since an SU(3) connection is in the adjoint by its transformation law. Without it there is no colour field in empty space, and so no string tension. Pure-glue confinement (lattice pure gauge, √σ ≈ 440 MeV, scale set from the real world) is therefore not contradicted by the picture, nor reproduced by it. The running of the coupling.
- **Confinement, what the rules give.** Under the ordinary transit rule a pattern of size L renews once per round trip, mc² ≈ πħc/L, a cost that falls with separation: a bound state, not confinement; excluded. A linear cost needs the relations between quarks to be engaged possibility along their length, one held quantum per grain a: E = (ħc/a²) L, the strong-coupling flux string (Kogut–Susskind 1975), a lattice-regime result, with the lattice spacing renamed. The grain for the measured tension is a = ħc/√σ ≈ 0.45 fm, the QCD scale in disguise (ħc/a = √σ; Λ_MS-bar is ~200–340 MeV, the same order); a new constant for CO, as Λ_QCD is for the standard theory. The force between quarks does not fall off: beyond a few tenths of a fermi it stays constant (about 1 GeV/fm) while the energy grows linearly, until a quark–antiquark pair forms and the string breaks (~1.2 fm on the lattice). The force between nucleons is a leftover, colour-neutral force; the range of its long-range tail is Yukawa's, ħ/(m_π c) ≈ 1.4 fm, read through a route with a pattern added (a silence in the rules).
- **"Never found alone."** Quarks commit, interact singly in deep-inelastic scattering at high momentum transfer and are deconfined in the quark–gluon plasma; what confinement says is that no quark is found alone at the end.
- **What it costs.** The unselected three, the dynamical matching on relations, the tension. What it is: the standard account's ledger in CO's words, the gluons now in it as patterns, the dynamical connection that would give their self-coupling still missing.

## The wall, stated

*Excluded (six routes).* On relations CO's inventory carries one real scalar (occupancy and ratio: the symmetric and antisymmetric parts of one number, which is all a scalar on a link can be) and one non-commuting matching, the frame (which line is which; the α's; gravity's self-coupling). On patterns it carries one complex phase (charge) and the four, which carries only charge. Every three-valued thing in it is a spatial vector. The nucleus needs, on each quark, a three-component *complex spatial scalar*, and on each relation a unitary 3 × 3 matching between them: colour must leave space alone, so it cannot be the frame, and it must be unitary, so it cannot be a real internal triad (places with a real inside give SO(3), not SU(3)). There is no complex three-component scalar anywhere in two primitives. Six routes from the inventory to that three were tried and closed, each by the same finding, that the three had been put in by hand and then found there: possibility in sorts (the count unselected; three U(1)s); the Clifford four (carries only charge, by Schur); the three lines of space (SO(3) is a subgroup of SU(3), not a factor); tiles (quarks as non-committing pieces) with a tile mass (falsified by flavour-universality of the tension and by pure-glue confinement); one pattern three times on a shared cycle (the three arises but splits sectors by mass: flavour, not colour, and no confinement from it; R11); channels, parallel relations between two places (removed at formation, where the pairing rule drives the channel count to one; R12). Nothing in two primitives selects a three, and nothing in two primitives self-couples.

What a third primitive would have to be, and what it costs: a three-component label carried by quarks, with a three-way matching carried on the relations between them. Given that label, and a connection posited on those relations, the rest follows by uniqueness at the standard theory's price, one scale and one coupling (two, for U(3), unless unit determinant is imposed; see "The triplet" below): SU(3) from the matching; self-coupling once the connection is posited as a dynamical SU(3)-valued object (the adjoint then follows from its transformation law; "carriers are patterns" does not supply the connection, since the photon is a pattern and does not self-couple); confinement's shape from a held, non-sliding relation between quarks. It cascades to the hadron spectrum and no further: not to the masses, not to 1/137, not to the now. By the bar set above, it is not earned. **CO's only non-abelian matching is space's; the nucleus needs one that is not space's; CO stops at the nucleus, and this is why.** The earliest point at which the rules say "one" where the nucleus needs "three" is the amplitude rule: possibility in transit carries one complex amplitude. A transit carrying a complex triplet, with its own addition rule, would be the object; nothing supplies it, and nothing forbids it. Any future route starts there, not at the nucleus. Read against the rules: the amplitude rule fixes the algebra of one amplitude and says nothing about how many a transit carries; the internal-state ruling sets a lower bound (the four) and admits further factors. So the slot for a third primitive is located: a factor of what some transit carries. It is not a single posit; what it would take is set out below.

The count is measured, not free: the e⁺e⁻ → hadrons ratio (proportional to the number of colours times the sum of squared quark charges, checked across energy ranges) and the π⁰ → γγ rate (proportional to the number squared) both give three, with charges ⅔ and −⅓. Any route to the nucleus must give three distinct carriers with those charges; a two or a four would be a falsification, not a near miss.

*On the gluon column.* In CO a comparison between places is carried by possibility in transit. Whether the carrier acts on itself is decided by its internal state: a carrier with no sign (the photon) does not; a carrier whose internal state is a pair of matching-labels (the adjoint) does. That connection is a posit, not a consequence of carriers being patterns. The standard fact is that a Lie-algebra-valued connection is in the adjoint by its own transformation law, and the commutator term in its field strength is the self-coupling.

## The triplet (shape)

*Shape.* The one admissible slot for a three-component complex scalar is a factor of what transit carries (the internal-state ruling sets a lower bound, the four, and admits more; the amplitude rule is silent on the count). The elimination to that slot stands. The claim that a single posit there gives the nucleus "by uniqueness" at the electromagnetic sector's standing does not.

**What a candidate at the electromagnetic sector's standing would have to posit, stated as posits.** (i) *Some* transit carries three indistinguishable complex amplitudes: which patterns, is a posit (as written, "transit carries a triplet" colours the electron, contradicting the very measurements that count three); this is possibility in sorts returning, with "which" unselected beside "three." (ii) Relations carry a 3 × 3 unitary matching with a quadratic cost: the connection as a dynamical object on links, posited as the ratio is posited in the electromagnetic sector; free relabelling at each place alone gives only a global symmetry, and "carriers are patterns" does not supply the link object (the photon is also a pattern carrying stock and does not self-couple; what decides self-coupling is the carrier's internal state, a second representation posit). (iii) Unit determinant on that matching: three indistinguishable amplitudes give U(3), and its extra phase couples to quark number, a massless colour-singlet vector, a long-range force on baryon number excluded at 10⁻⁴⁷ (Lee and Yang 1955); it cannot be electric charge (⅔, −⅓ are not colour-uniform and leptons carry charge with no triplet). (iv) A second measured input, the scale Λ. With those, Yang–Mills is the unique quadratic cost (Utiyama 1956), with *two* couplings for U(3) unless (iii) is imposed.

**What even that leaves unbuilt.** The proton. One triplet species with no flavour gives a spin-3/2 ground state, not a spin-½ proton; Fermi statistics for quarks is open in the matter sector (the exchange sign); the singlet rule that picks qqq and qq̄ is a consequence of confinement, which is named, not obtained; the gluon field is second-quantised and carries half the momentum, and no field in CO is second-quantised; the mass is a lattice computation; and the fractional charges require the electromagnetic sector's "charge is a sign" to become a rational multiple. The proton contradiction is not resolved by the triplet; it is relocated to the list above.

**Standing.** Worse than the electromagnetic sector: there the connection is posited, the group is forced by the amplitude's phase, and the one measured input is a continuous coupling; here the group is the input, the connection would have to be posited separately, a further rule removes a ninth massless vector, and the bound state is not built. "The wall, stated" is the sector's last word.

## Three kinds of wall

The walls around the nucleus are not all the same kind, and only one kind can be got round.

- **Silences.** Nothing selects N = 3. Not a theorem; the primitives do not speak. Passable by one thing only, a new rule, and the cascade bar decides whether it is earned.
- **Representation theorems.** The Clifford four carries only U(1) (above). Schur's lemma has no lattice premise. A relational lattice does not touch it; a time-dependent dynamics (hold-and-release) can only shrink a commutant, never enlarge it.
- **Continuum no-gos.** Nielsen–Ninomiya assumes a fixed translation-invariant lattice; Coleman–Mandula assumes Poincaré invariance and an S-matrix. Neither premise holds at the hop scale, so both have loopholes on a relational lattice. The Coleman–Mandula loophole is where "the three lines of space are colour" could have lived; at the hop scale the triad is three link labels, and mixing them with phases is the walk's own coin, which is the Clifford structure, which is the second wall. The loophole is real; using it lands on Schur.

One loophole with content was checked and is empty. If hold-and-release treated the two chiralities differently, the held phase would carry structure the transit phase lacks. It does not: hold scales hop and flip alike, the flip is parity-even, and the hop rule (symmetric occupancy, antisymmetric ratio 1-form) has no handedness. A chiral asymmetry would need an axial 1-form, which the inventory lacks. Checked numerically on the three-dimensional walk with a hold bump: L→R and R→L transfer equal to 10⁻¹⁴ ([`hold_chirality_check.py`](RECORD_OF_METHOD/NUCLEUS_notes/hold_chirality_check.py)). Ruled: hold-and-release is chirality-blind. (The check used a normalised explicit scheme, not the automaton; the equality is a symmetry of the scheme, and the number is round-off on an identity.)

## Two walls, faced

- **Bell.** A commitment is a local, irreversible, definite event, and Born is read as an identification. Two entangled patterns committing at spacelike separation correlate beyond anything a local rule gives; no lattice property evades this, since Bell has no lattice premise. Candidate: an entangled pair is one pattern sampled twice, one stock with one cut; each end's commitment is its own event, and each finds the stock as it stands in its own past cone. Cost, open: the two places are spacelike. "One event with two locations whose past cone is the union" does not fit the now ruling (what has committed, for a commitment, is its past cone): a later commitment with one end in its past would have the spacelike other end definite for it, and the union is the past of no event. The relativistic collapse literature pays this either as two point-events with a nonlocal covariant law (Tumulka 2006; interacting distinguishable case 2020/21; indistinguishable particles and creation open) or as a foliation fixed by the wavefunction (Dürr et al. 2014; Bedingham et al. 2014). CO's candidate position is the first: two point commitments, one stock, one joint law; for each commitment, the stock it engages is the pair's possibility in transit given the commitments in its past cone (Tumulka's conditioning, in structure; the law is Born on the pair, not GRW's). The now ruling, read as ordering nothing spacelike, state-fixed or not, selects this over a foliation; a foliation fixed by the state is not excluded by relativity, so the selection is CO's and is priced here; the substrate as identified has a frame, so a frame-fixed foliation is also available, and setting it aside is a posit. Inherited, not supplied: the joint law (Born on the pair), that two spacelike engagements commute (they act on the stock at different places), and what the pair's possibility is at one place (the marginal); covariance holds in the continuum limit at tree level, the walk having a hop (with loops, see `MATTER.md`, Lorentz invariance). Open: the coupled two-pattern walk (`MATTER.md`, the two-pattern problem); the interacting chain is that gap, not a further one: at the cut the engagement is the commitment and nothing is shared afterwards. Candidate: a commitment resolves exactly the possibility it engages, so a renewal, which engages free possibility, resolves nothing about the pattern, and the retained record is set by free stock, the same in every branch (`MATTER.md`, candidate ruling), so free flight dims no fringe and a pattern left alone is never resolved; δ is a constant of gravity only. That a commitment resolves only what it engages, free possibility in a renewal and a pattern's transit in a measurement, is a reading of the rules (candidate; working record), not a derivation. Candidate.
- **Doublers.** Faced, not closed. The Dirac automaton as published carries three zone-edge doublers and, in its sequential step ordering, eight further massless modes. A relational lattice alone does not remove them (R8); a one-way non-unitary hop removes them and the electron with them (R9, excluded); symmetrising the step removes the eight and is needed (the sequential step gives a handedness-dependent speed that Crab electrons exclude; L1); the Wilson term in strictly local half-shift form gives the rest mass at the hop scale (candidate; L1b). That is the standard Wilson statement: one light species plus heavy copies at the cutoff, predicted particles since the hop is physical. The binding bound on the hop is the electron's speed, ℓ ≲ 1.3 × 10⁻³¹ m (needed). Recorded in the matter sector. Open.

## Status

**CO is a reconstruction, from two primitives, of gravity in empty space and for dust, and of one-particle quantum mechanics up to 1930.** It covers gravity, electromagnetism and the electron, and stops at the nucleus.
