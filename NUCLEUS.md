# Nucleus

What the two primitives do not reach. The working record is in [`RECORD_OF_METHOD/`](RECORD_OF_METHOD/); the blind reviews of 2026-11-04 and 2026-11-05 are there, and this file was corrected after the second.

## The three open problems

The particle masses, the strength of the electromagnetic coupling (1/137), and colour. Each needs something not in the inventory.

## Colour

Colour is a three-component internal arrow whose orientation is a convention at each point; the gluon field compares conventions between neighbouring points. The only three-valued structure the primitives supply is spatial (the three lines through a place), and an SU(3) acting on the triad rotates with space: SO(3) is a subgroup of SU(3), not a factor, so no internal remainder survives. **The three lines of space are not colour.** An internal three-dimensional space is outside the inventory; the three conditions of possibility (free, in transit, engaged) are not interchangeable and cannot be one either.

## The bar for a third primitive

A third primitive is admissible on a named contradiction with the world: the rules make a proton an elementary pattern, while experiment makes it composite, bound by a force the rules lack, with most of its mass in the binding. It is worth admitting only if it cascades, bringing a second open problem with it. In the standard theory, colour with one coupling does cascade to the whole hadron spectrum; it does not reach the lepton masses or 1/137. The one geometric route to an internal three is Kaluza–Klein's: places with an inside.

What a third primitive must deliver is fixed by experiment: three separable carriers, not three labels on one carrier. In heavy-ion collisions the anisotropic flow of a hadron scales with its number of quarks (constituent-quark scaling, RHIC; seen in oxygen–oxygen by ALICE, preliminary, 2026), which needs three pieces that carry momentum on their own inside the plasma and recombine outside it. A colour that is an orientation of a single pattern cannot give that. This excludes possibility in sorts as the candidate and asks instead for a binding whose release depends on the surroundings.

### Sorts of possibility, already costed

The obvious candidate is possibility in sorts: N kinds of possibility, indistinguishable under the rules, conserved together. That candidate has been run, in an earlier program by the same author, and its cost is known.

- **What it buys.** N indistinguishable, jointly conserved sorts force the gauge group U(N) = (SU(N) × U(1))/Z_N by uniqueness. Posit three sorts and SU(3) follows. This is a textbook result (the symmetry group of N identical conserved channels), restated; it is not a derivation of colour.
- **What it does not buy.** The count N is the input. No rule selects {1, 2, 3}; a symmetric N-sort multiplet is stable for every N, so nothing picks three. And the counts as posited give U(1) × U(2) × U(3), with three U(1) factors where the standard theory has one. The route does not land on the standard group without a further rule.
- **The one structural lead.** The three multiplicities reduce to one number: the most mutually orthogonal sorts an internal state of dimension d can carry is d, so d = 3 gives groups up to SU(3) and nothing stable above it. The question "why three sorts" becomes "why is the internal state three-dimensional." The matter sector already fixes an internal state of dimension four (Clifford), so the check is whether that four can carry the three. It cannot. The four is an irreducible representation of the Clifford algebra; by Schur's lemma the only operators commuting with the walk's dynamics (α₁, α₂, α₃, β) are scalars, so the only internal symmetry the four supports is U(1), charge. (Massless, the commutant is two-dimensional, 1 and γ₅: two chiral U(1)s. The mass term reduces it to one; so does the alternation term adopted in the matter sector, which breaks chiral symmetry explicitly as Wilson terms do.) Under rotations the four splits as 2 ⊕ 2, spin ½ twice, with no triplet. So the Clifford four is exhausted by charge; colour would need a new tensor factor, an internal three added to the inventory, not found in it. This sharpens the stop: two primitives give exactly one internal symmetry, U(1), and no more. (A stable SU(N ≥ 4) gauge symmetry would contradict the reduction, but none is seen, so this is consistency, not a test.) Check script: [`RECORD_OF_METHOD/NUCLEUS_notes/clifford_commutant.py`](RECORD_OF_METHOD/NUCLEUS_notes/clifford_commutant.py).
- **Independent confirmation of one closure.** That run also found the internal three distinct in kind from the spatial three, not reducible to it. Same conclusion as above, reached by a different route.

Net: sorts cascade exactly as far as SU(N) and stop. By the bar set here, a third primitive of this form is not yet earned.

## Three-tile patterns

*Candidate.* A quark is not a pattern. A proton is: a pattern of three **tiles**, a kind of pattern different from the electron's one tile. The relations inside such a pattern are its **kinships**, distinct from the relations between places. This is a ruling on what possibility in transit can be, three tiles in transit together, not a third primitive. Its inside is marked by the ruling already made for entanglement: a commitment belongs to the pattern, so the tiles of one pattern commit together. A tile never commits alone; in a plasma it completes with whatever kin are near. (That no free quark is seen is consistent with this, not a test of it; confinement is already known.)

- **Colour** is the bookkeeping of kinship, which tile completes with which. That is a convention, and changing it is a slide, which costs nothing: colour change is not an event. The gluon field is the comparison of that bookkeeping between places. It is not a ratio: a ratio is one phase per link and gives an abelian field, as it did for charge; a three-way matching needs a 3 × 3 unitary per kinship, which is what SU(3) is. Three-way matching gives SU(3) by uniqueness; the three is not selected.
- **Confinement.** Under the ordinary transit rule a pattern of size L renews once per round trip, mc² ≈ πħc/L, a cost that falls with separation: a bound state, not confinement; excluded. A linear cost needs the kinship to be engaged possibility along its length, one held quantum per grain a: E = (ħc/a²) L, a string with tension σ = ħc/a². If the held quanta are the tiles' own renewals, the grain is the tile's renewal length a = ħ/(m_tile c) and σ = (m_tile c²)²/ħc. With a tile at a third of the proton, σ ≈ 0.5 GeV/fm against the measured ≈ 0.9 GeV/fm: consistent to a factor two, not a prediction. The tension is then not a new constant; it is the tile mass, which already sits in the mass problem.
- **What it costs.** The unselected three, the tile masses, which the matter sector already owes, and new objects: a tile (not a pattern), a kinship (a relation that does not slide free, against the rule for relations between places), a completion rule and a cost per length. Whether calling these "a ruling on transit" rather than a third primitive is more than a name is open; the cascade bar above has not been met, only restated. What it is: the standard account's ledger in CO's words (tile = constituent quark, kinship = colour string, completion with near kin = deconfinement, σ = m² = one-scale dimensional analysis); the shape, not a derivation. The flow-scaling argument above is the weaker evidence for three carriers; deep-inelastic scattering and three-jet events are the stronger, and constituent-count scaling is violated at the 20% level in lead collisions.

Checks (including the m_p/3 figure) in [`RECORD_OF_METHOD/NUCLEUS_notes/kinship_stretch_cost.py`](RECORD_OF_METHOD/NUCLEUS_notes/kinship_stretch_cost.py); working record in `NUCLEUS_record.md`.

## Three kinds of wall

The walls around the nucleus are not all the same kind, and only one kind can be got round.

- **Silences.** Nothing selects N = 3. Not a theorem; the primitives do not speak. Passable by one thing only, a new rule, and the cascade bar decides whether it is earned.
- **Representation theorems.** The Clifford four carries only U(1) (above). Schur's lemma has no lattice premise. A relational lattice does not touch it; a time-dependent dynamics (hold-and-release) can only shrink a commutant, never enlarge it.
- **Continuum no-gos.** Nielsen–Ninomiya assumes a fixed translation-invariant lattice; Coleman–Mandula assumes Poincaré invariance and an S-matrix. Neither premise holds at the hop scale, so both have loopholes on a relational lattice. The Coleman–Mandula loophole is where "the three lines of space are colour" could have lived; at the hop scale the triad is three link labels, and mixing them with phases is the walk's own coin, which is the Clifford structure, which is the second wall. The loophole is real; using it lands on Schur.

One loophole with content was checked and is empty. If hold-and-release treated the two chiralities differently, the held phase would carry structure the transit phase lacks. It does not: hold scales hop and flip alike, the flip is parity-even, and the hop rule (symmetric occupancy, antisymmetric ratio 1-form) has no handedness. A chiral asymmetry would need an axial 1-form, which the inventory lacks. Checked numerically on the three-dimensional walk with a hold bump: L→R and R→L transfer equal to 10⁻¹⁴ ([`hold_chirality_check.py`](RECORD_OF_METHOD/NUCLEUS_notes/hold_chirality_check.py)). Ruled: hold-and-release is chirality-blind. (The check used a normalised explicit scheme, not the automaton; the equality is a symmetry of the scheme, and the number is round-off on an identity.)

## Two walls, faced

- **Bell.** A commitment is a local, irreversible, definite event, and Born is read as an identification. Two entangled patterns committing at spacelike separation correlate beyond anything a local rule gives; no lattice property evades this, since Bell has no lattice premise. Candidate: an entangled pair is one pattern sampled twice; a commitment belongs to the pattern, not the place, and one event increments the count at both ends. Cost: not yet paid. A single event at two spacelike places needs either a preferred ordering (a foliation, which the gravity sector does not have and which would generically give nonzero preferred-frame parameters against its no-deviation-from-GR result) or a flash-type ontology in which the event is Lorentz-invariantly defined without one (Tumulka 2006). Which, is open. Checked: a commitment resolves exactly the possibility it engages, so a renewal, which engages free possibility, resolves nothing about the pattern, and the only free-flight trace is the retained record δħ per renewal, giving fringe visibility V = (1 − δ)^N after N renewals; molecule interferometry (C₇₀, 25 kDa) bounds δ ≲ 10⁻²⁷ and δ ≈ 10⁻³² sits inside it ([`record_visibility_bound.py`](RECORD_OF_METHOD/NUCLEUS_notes/record_visibility_bound.py)). A bound passed, not a prediction. That a commitment resolves only what it engages, free possibility in a renewal and a pattern's transit in a measurement, is a reading of the rules (candidate; working record), not a derivation. Candidate.
- **Doublers.** Faced, not closed. The Dirac automaton as published carries three zone-edge doublers and, in its sequential step ordering, eight further massless modes. A relational lattice alone does not remove them (R8); a one-way non-unitary hop removes them and the electron with them (R9, excluded); symmetrising the step removes the eight (R10) and a flip rate rising with alternation, the Wilson term in unitary form, lifts the rest to the hop scale for r in a window (R10, corrected in R10d after review). That is the standard Wilson statement: one light species plus heavy copies at the cutoff. Since the hop is physical, the copies are predicted particles, and their absence bounds the hop length from above. Two candidate rulings and one bound, recorded in the matter sector. Open.

## Status

**CO is a reconstruction of physics up to 1930 from two primitives.** It covers gravity, electromagnetism and the electron, and stops at the nucleus.
