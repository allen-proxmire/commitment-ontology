## Reading I14: simulating the bottom — CO with no grid

*29 October 2026. After Reading I13 ruling 3. A reading of the record and of the causal-set and related literature, plus two small exploratory scripts (not registered; §6). It encodes no new rule. Revised after an independent check (`checkI14/REPORT.md`, with its script `check_i14.py` and log), whose corrections are adopted; the pre-check version is `reading_I14_v1_precheck.md`. Statements marked **new statement** are proposals that would need a ruling; **inference** marks a step past what the record or a source says.*

**In plain terms.** Reading I13 ruled that places are finite in number in any region but have no fixed spacing, so the engine's cubic lattice is only a stand-in. This reading asks what a simulation of CO with no grid would look like, and whether it could turn one of CO's borrowed inputs (Lorentz invariance, three dimensions) into an output. The nearest existing theory is causal set theory, which already shows that randomly scattered events keep Lorentz invariance exactly and already reads a dimension off a bare order of events; what it has not managed in nearly forty years is a growth rule that produces orders looking like a smooth four-dimensional spacetime. If CO's history is read as an order generated by possibility's transfers, each unit going to one later element (a reading the record does not yet contain), then every commitment has finitely many immediate successors, while a sprinkling of Minkowski space has infinitely many, so such an order is not a Lorentz-invariant sprinkling (a known causal-set result, Bombelli–Henson–Sorkin 2009, applied to a new reading). Counted within a chosen frame, the successors needed grow as about 12γ² with the boost γ, so a cap at the vacuum's lower bound of 6.4×10¹⁰ units would be reached at γ ≈ 7×10⁴, and hiding a frame up to the boosts of observed particles would need about 10²⁰–10²³ units. Whether CO's order is generated by possibility's transfers is therefore the first thing to rule on. A small first test of one candidate growth rule is proposed with a kill rule; it tests only that rule, is expected to fail, and passing would make C7 a candidate, not a derivation of 3+1 dimensions.

### 1. Why

- Reading I13 ruling 3 (29 October): "Position takes values only at places; places are finite in number in any finite region but have no fixed spacing … The engine's lattice is a stand-in." The hop "is read as a typical spacing set by the local count, not a fixed length".
- The 23 October statement: "The engine's medium is itself an average … A scaleless substrate has no direct model, so the engine uses an averaged medium as its stand-in" (`record/ONTOLOGY_full.md` §5).
- So much of the gravity line (Runs 1–91: the cubic lattice, Run 13's random complexes, Runs 83–91's Kuhn-triangulated carrier and its constraint algebra) tested the stand-in. Run 90 and Run 101 found that relativity on the lattice follows from the borrowed boost relation {H[1], H[xᵃ]} = D[e_a], not from the primitives.
- CO borrows, among other things: Lorentz invariance (R7's twist across ticks; R8's boosted rest field and inertia = total energy; the boost relation in Runs 90 and 101), GR's form of curvature (R9, option 3), three dimensions ("Three dimensions of space are assumed", `ONTOLOGY.md`), and the Born rule (Q5).
- The question here: does a grid-free statement of CO's primitives have a minimal simulation whose outcome could turn one of these into an output, by a test that could fail?

### 3. What is already known (the literature)

**Causal sets: kinematics.**
- *Order plus number equals geometry.* A causal set is a locally finite partial order; a Lorentzian manifold is approximated by a Poisson sprinkling at density ρ, with the number of elements in a region a Poisson variable of mean ρV (Bombelli, Lee, Meyer & Sorkin, *PRL* 59, 521 (1987); review: Surya, *Living Rev. Rel.* 22, 5 (2019), [arXiv:1903.11544](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.11544)). The *Hauptvermutung* (a causal set faithfully embeds in two spacetimes only if they are approximately isometric) is conjectured, not proved (Surya §3.1).
- *Lorentz invariance from randomness.* "There exists no equivariant measurable map from sprinklings to spacetime directions (even locally)", so a sprinkled causal set picks out no frame and gives no modified dispersion; and "there is no way to associate a finite-valency graph to a sprinkling consistently with Lorentz invariance" (Bombelli, Henson & Sorkin, *Mod. Phys. Lett. A* 24, 2579 (2009), [arXiv:gr-qc/0605006](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0605006)). Its hypotheses are a Poisson process in infinite Minkowski space and a measurable, Lorentz-equivariant map. In a sprinkling of Minkowski space each element has almost surely infinitely many links (Surya §3); the order's own link diagram is such an equivariant construction, which is why its valency must be infinite. Phenomenology of the residual Lorentz-invariant effects ("swerves"): Dowker, Henson & Sorkin, [arXiv:gr-qc/0311055](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0311055).
- *Dimension from the order alone.* For an interval of a sprinkling into d-dimensional Minkowski space, the ordering fraction r = R / C(n, 2) (R the number of related pairs) depends only on d (Myrheim 1978; Meyer 1988; formula as given in Surya §4):
$$
r(d)=\frac{\Gamma(d+1)\,\Gamma(d/2)}{2\,\Gamma(3d/2)},\qquad r(2)=\tfrac12,\ \ r(3)\approx0.229,\ \ r(4)=\tfrac1{10}.
$$
  The midpoint-scaling dimension is a second estimator (Bombelli 1987; Reid, [arXiv:gr-qc/0207103](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0207103); not fetched). Time is read from the longest chain, which scales as (ρV)^{1/d} in an interval (Brightwell & Gregory, *PRL* 66, 260 (1991); from standard listings, not fetched). Abajian and Carlip (2018) find the Myrheim–Meyer estimate falls to about 2 at small volumes (Surya §4).
- *The wave operator.* A local nearest-neighbour operator does not work on a sprinkling. The causal-set d'Alembertian is an alternating sum over layers of past neighbours; in d = 4 (Benincasa & Dowker)
$$
B\phi(x)=\frac{4}{\sqrt6\,\ell^2}\Big[-\phi(x)+\Big(\sum_{L_1}-9\sum_{L_2}+16\sum_{L_3}-8\sum_{L_4}\Big)\phi(y)\Big],
$$
  with L_k the elements with k − 1 elements between them and x (the 2D operator: Sorkin, [arXiv:gr-qc/0703099](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0703099); d = 4: Benincasa & Dowker, *PRL* 104, 181301 (2010); other dimensions: Dowker & Glaser 2013; coefficients as quoted in Surya Eq. 27). Its fluctuations are tamed only by smearing over a nonlocality scale ℓ_k ≫ ℓ; Sorkin's argument is that "physics cannot remain local" if discreteness and Lorentz invariance are both kept, and that the nonlocality need not be confined to the discreteness scale.
- *Spectral dimension.* Random walks on sprinkled causal sets give a spectral dimension that *increases* at small scales, against most other approaches, and this is tied to the same nonlocality (Eichhorn & Mizera, [arXiv:1311.2530](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.2530)).

**Causal sets: dynamics, and the hard problem.**
- *Entropy.* Almost all n-element orders are Kleitman–Rothschild orders: three layers of sizes about n/4, n/2, n/4, with log₂ of their number n²/4 + O(n), and nothing like a manifold (Kleitman & Rothschild, *Trans. AMS* 205, 205 (1975); Dhar 1978, 1980; Surya §3.1). Any dynamics must suppress them.
- *Classical sequential growth.* Elements are born one at a time; the probability of each birth depends only on the past order, with discrete general covariance and Bell causality imposed (Rideout & Sorkin, *PRD* 61, 024002 (2000), [arXiv:gr-qc/9904062](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9904062)). Its simplest member, transitive percolation (each earlier element becomes an ancestor independently with probability p, then transitive closure), has a candidate continuum limit along a curve p(N) (Rideout & Sorkin, *PRD* 63, 104011 (2001), [arXiv:gr-qc/0003117](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0003117)), but at fixed p it almost surely produces infinitely many "posts" (elements related to everything), giving a cyclic universe (Alon, Bollobás, Brightwell & Janson, *Ann. Appl. Prob.* 4, 108 (1994); Ahmed & Rideout, *PRD* 81, 083528 (2010), [arXiv:0909.4771](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.4771)). Ahmed and Rideout give evidence that the era after a post is de Sitter-like, while saying the orders are "not readily approximated by smooth manifolds". Surya's summary: CSG's typical causal sets "are very tall … not quite manifold-like".
- *Path-integral suppression.* With the Benincasa–Dowker action, 2D Monte Carlo over 2-orders shows a first-order transition between a manifold-like phase and a layered phase (Surya 2012; Glaser & Surya 2016; Glaser, O'Connor & Surya 2018). Bilayer and KR-type orders are suppressed under dimension-dependent conditions (Loomis & Carlip, [arXiv:1709.00064](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.00064); P. Carlip, S. Carlip & S. Surya, "Path integral suppression of badly behaved causal sets", *CQG* (2023), doi:10.1088/1361-6382/acc50c, [arXiv:2209.00327](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.00327)). This *uses* a discretised Einstein–Hilbert action as input and does not choose a dimension dynamically.

**Other grid-free programs.**
- *Causal dynamical triangulations.* Gluing 4-simplices with a causal (foliated) rule and the Regge action gives an extended four-dimensional universe, with a spectral dimension falling to about 2 at short scales (Ambjørn, Jurkiewicz & Loll, *PRL* 93, 131301 (2004), [arXiv:hep-th/0404156](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0404156); *PRL* 95, 171301 (2005), [arXiv:hep-th/0505113](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0505113)). The building blocks are four-dimensional, so the dimension of the simplices is an input; what emerges is the extended phase, not the number four.
- *Quantum graphity.* A dynamical graph on a fixed number of vertices, complete at high energy, condensing at low temperature to a low-valency, lattice-like graph (Konopka, Markopoulou & Smolin, [arXiv:hep-th/0611197](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0611197); low-temperature properties: Caravelli & Markopoulou, [arXiv:1008.1340](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.1340)). Its condensed graphs carry a global time and are not Lorentz-invariant; the target valency and dimension are tuned in the Hamiltonian.
- *Hypergraph rewriting (Wolfram model).* Claims of emergent relativity rest on "causal invariance" of the rewriting order (Gorard, [arXiv:2004.14810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14810)); no specific rule has been shown to give 3+1 dimensions, Lorentz-invariant statistics, or a known particle spectrum. Aaronson showed that Wolfram's earlier deterministic proposal "cannot be made compatible with both special relativity and Bell inequality violation" ([arXiv:quant-ph/0206089](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0206089)). Treated here as a cautionary example: a large rule space searched for resemblance is not a test.

**Which is closest to CO.** Classical sequential growth. Its elements are events, born irreversibly one at a time, with time read as a count along chains — CO's commitment and "time at a place is the count of commitments there" (with a place read as a chain, C3). Its Lorentz invariance comes from exactly the property ruling 3 now asserts for places: finite number per region, no fixed spacing. Quantum graphity is closest to CO's *places and relations* as a spatial graph, and inherits its global time. CDT is furthest: it builds in the dimension of its simplices and GR's action.

### 4. CO's primitives as a grid-free model: the choices a simulation needs

Each choice is graded **derived** (follows from the record's own statements), **reading** (one of several readings the record allows) or **new statement** (needs a ruling).

**C1. What an element is.** The order's elements are commitments (commitment is "one irreversible update"). But "the vacuum never commits" means commitments occur only in matter, so an order of commitments alone has no elements in empty space and cannot carry the vacuum's geometry. Two readings:
- **C1a** — the order is the commitments only; the vacuum's geometry lives elsewhere (in the places and relations). Then the causal-set route gives geometry only inside matter. *Reading.*
- **C1b** — possibility's hops are also events of the order: uncommitted, recording nothing (the engine's hops are one-way, stated of the engine at a steady state). The vacuum's order is then the order of hops. *New statement.* It keeps "the vacuum never commits" (a hop is not a commitment) but makes hops the substrate's events.

**C2. Whether the elements form a partial order.** Irreversibility gives acyclicity; transitivity holds if "precedes" means "could have been influenced by"; local finiteness is the no-infinities principle. *Reading, under C1* (precedence read as influence; under R4 the engine's clock field is fixed afresh each tick, so influence through the field is instantaneous there, and an order defined by influence is a further choice). Under that reading CO's history is a causal set.

**C3. What a place is.** A place persists and accumulates a count. In an order, the natural persistent object is a chain (a worldline), and "time at a place is the count of commitments there" is the length of that chain — the Brightwell–Gregory reading of proper time. *Reading. Coherent only with C1b:* under C1a, vacuum places would have no elements, yet they hold P∞ and patterns cross them. Places as primary objects with a separate spatial graph (quantum graphity's picture) is the alternative, and brings back a global ordering parameter (the engine's tick, which R4 says "is not physical time").

**C4. What a relation is.** Three options:
- **C4a** — the order's links (the Hasse diagram: a precedes b with nothing between). *Reading.* C4a meets the first clause of Run 13's contrast principle (no triangles) automatically, since three mutually linked elements would form a chain a < b < c and then a–c is not a link; and it fails the second (no two short loops sharing more than one link): any interval with ≥ 3 incomparable linked middle elements gives K₂,₃ (the check counts 4,132 such pairs in a 2D sprinkling and 176,208 in 4D, N = 1200). Run 13's graphs are spatial; Hasse links are timelike.
- **C4b** — a spatial graph among places, separate from the order. *Reading.* BHS rules out finite-valency graphs built equivariantly from a sprinkling; a primitive spatial graph is not of that kind, but a finite-valency spatial graph defines a foliation (as in quantum graphity), so C4b brings a preferred frame (an argument, not a theorem). Reading I11's exclusion of a plain lattice term (on Run 100's ladder) would then apply without the borrowed boost relation to rescue it.
- **C4c** — relations defined by possibility's flow: a relates to b if possibility passed from a to b. *New statement.*

**C5. How possibility flows on the order: the valency fork.** The fork rests on three premises, none in the record:
- **(P1)** the order is the transitive closure of unit transfers: e precedes f exactly when a chain of transfers runs from e to f;
- **(P2)** each unit leaves an element to exactly one later element;
- **(P3)** each element carries finitely many units, P_e < ∞.

Given (P1), every link is a transfer edge, since transitive closure only adds relations that have something between them. So
$$
\#\{\text{future links of }e\}\ \le\ \#\{\text{transfer edges out of }e\}\ \le\ P_e ,
$$
a **finite future valency (at most the units the element releases)**; past links are bounded the same way by the units an element gathers. A Poisson sprinkling of Minkowski space gives every element infinitely many links almost surely (in d = 2 the count diverges slowly). So an order satisfying (P1)–(P3) is almost surely not a sprinkling of infinite Minkowski space. *Grade: a restatement of a causal-set result (BHS 2009; the infinite valency of sprinklings), applied to a reading the record does not yet contain.* The record's hops run between places, not commitments, and nothing in it says possibility's transfers generate the order.

The exploratory pilot (§6) shows the bound: maximum future links ≤ H in all five runs, = H in four ((M, H) = (300, 10) gives 8).

*How large a cap would hide the frame* (the check's closed form, verified there by Monte Carlo to about 6%). Counting future links within rapidity η of a chosen frame, the expected number per element does not depend on ρ:
$$
\langle L(\eta)\rangle=\frac{\Omega_{d-2}}{d\,c_d}\int_0^{\eta}\sinh^{d-2}\eta'\,d\eta',\qquad
d=2:\ 2\eta;\quad d=3:\ 8(\cosh\eta-1);\quad d=4:\ 24\Big(\frac{\sinh2\eta}{4}-\frac{\eta}{2}\Big)\approx3e^{2\eta}\approx12\gamma^2 .
$$
Escapes from the fork:
- **A large cap.** P∞ is bounded only from below. In 3+1 a cap P is reached at γ* ≈ √(P/12): 7×10⁴ at P = 6.4×10¹⁰ (η* = 11.89). A frame invisible up to observed boosts needs P ≳ 1.1×10²⁰ (Crab synchrotron electrons, γ ≈ 3×10⁹) to 1.2×10²³ (ultra-high-energy protons, γ ≈ 10¹¹); a full sprinkled Hubble volume at ℓ ≈ 10³ ℓ_P needs about 10¹¹⁵. The growth is exponential in rapidity, not slow. Which count is the cap depends on C1: under C1a, the units a commitment releases (a pattern's stock q = 2E/E_pair, or a pair's units); under C1b, the units moved per hop (R1's mean outflow f(P) = c ln(1 + P/p_s), about 12c per tick, far below P∞). No CO rule yet says what an observer beyond γ* would see, so this is a required cap, not a data exclusion.
- **Longer-range transfers:** possibility hops along relations that are not the order's links (C4c with longer-range flow, as the causal-set d'Alembertian's layers are), so (P1) fails.
- **A unit is not one hop to one successor:** it is shared, so (P2) fails.
- **A finite universe:** whether the universe has finitely many places is open (`ONTOLOGY_full.md` §7). If it does, every element has finitely many links anyway and BHS does not apply; the conflict is then quantitative, set by the formula above.

So the fork is: either the cap is at least about 10²⁰–10²³ (or about 10¹¹⁵ for a full sprinkling), or (P1) fails.

**C6. The beginning.** The One Being, "committing everywhere at once", is in order terms a large initial antichain (M mutually unrelated first commitments). *Reading.* Transitive percolation starts from nothing and grows a tree era from one origin element (Ahmed & Rideout); the One Being proposal explicitly rejects a single seed. Any order grown from M first elements has a wide bottom layer; the Kleitman–Rothschild signature is having only three layers. Whether growth from a wide antichain avoids that regime is open, and is CO-specific.

**C7. A candidate dynamics: hold-rule growth.** A new commitment is born when it has gathered H units, its parents are the elements the units came from, and the units move to it. With no space, *which* units it gathers must be chosen without geometry: uniformly at random from all available units is the only choice that adds nothing. *New statement*, suggested by R2 but not the record's own rule. C7 changes R2 in four ways: no ticks; units absorbed, not present (R2 counts the possibility present at a commitment's places summed over ticks, and does not use it up; Reading I4 §1); one site instead of two places; H = O(1–16) instead of 2ħ_t P∞.
- **C7 is not a Rideout–Sorkin sequential growth model.** Its transition probabilities depend on the units each element still holds, which the order does not determine. Two unit histories with the same order give different probabilities for the next birth (the check's example: M = 2, H = 2; seed a, b; c takes {a₁, b₁}; then d takes either {a₂, c₁} or {c₁, c₂}, both giving a, b < c < d; the chance that the next element sits above a or b only is 0 in the first case and 1/6 in the second). C7 is a Markov growth on order plus units. Rideout–Sorkin's discrete general covariance and Bell causality are also unchecked.

### 5. The sharp questions, and what the literature already settles

| question | what is already established | would CO be restating? | what would be new to CO |

The inputs CO could turn into outputs in a way that would be noticed are **dimension** and **manifoldlikeness**, and those are the field's open problem.

### 7. A possible first registered test: T-I14a, the hold-rule growth from an antichain seed

**Question.** Does the hold-rule growth (C6 + C7: an initial antichain of M elements, each holding H units; each new commitment takes H units uniformly at random from all units; parents = their holders; transitive closure) produce orders with a scale-stable dimension that does not depend on (M, H)? It tests C7, a new statement, and is framed as a check of the instruments, with transitive percolation as a reference arm.

**Design (feasible on 2 cores / 7 GB, though not cheap).**
- N up to 2×10⁴ elements, stored as bitsets (about 50 MB per order); interval sizes counted by bitset popcount of future(a) AND past(b) on sampled pairs (the pilot's dense matrix product does not scale to this N); 10 seeds per point; a grid of (M, H) spanning at least one decade in each: M ∈ {100, 300, 1000, 3000}, H ∈ {2, 4, 8, 16}. About 200 orders in all, with calibrations and the reference arm.
- Instruments, all calibrated in advance on sprinklings in d = 2, 3, 4 at the same N, with the pass bands set from those calibrations:
  1. Myrheim–Meyer dimension d_MM on intervals at three sizes (n ≈ 50, 300, 2000);
  2. midpoint-scaling dimension d_mid on the same intervals;
  3. longest chain against interval size, fit to (ρV)^{1/d} for d_chain;
  4. layer statistics (the fraction of elements in the largest antichain) against the Kleitman–Rothschild signature;
  5. future-link count against N (the valency question).
- Analysis code and pass bands hashed before the run, as from Run 22; an independent check, as from Run 29; transitive percolation run with the same instruments as the reference arm.

**Kill rule (registered).** T-I14a **FAILS** if any of:
- (K1) d_MM differs between the smallest and largest interval size by more than 3 times the standard error of the mean d_MM over a fixed number of intervals, calibrated on a sprinkling of the dimension the order estimates (scale instability);
- (K2) d_MM, d_mid and d_chain disagree beyond their calibration spreads (no single dimension);
- (K3) the scale-stable dimension, where one exists, varies across the (M, H) grid by more than the calibration spread (the dimension is put in by the parameters);
- (K4) the largest antichain, measured excluding the seed antichain, holds more than a fixed fraction (set from the calibration) of the elements (layered, KR-like).

It **PASSES** only if K1–K4 all hold off at some region of the grid that is at least a decade wide in M or H. The value of the dimension is reported, not required: any stable integer value would be the finding.

**What passing would mean.** A candidate, not a derivation: C7, with nothing spatial put in, would make orders with a definite dimension. It would not show the order is a sprinkling of Minkowski space (the Hauptvermutung is unproved), would not show Lorentz invariance (C7 satisfies (P1)–(P3), so its valency is finite), and would not reach physical N, since 2×10⁴ elements cover a 4D region only about 12 elements across.

**What failing would mean.** That refutes C7, a new statement; it does not test R2 or the 23 October statement, which the record already lists as without mechanism.

**Expected outcome, stated in advance.** Failure, most likely by K1 and K3: the pilot drifts with scale and with (M, H), and uniform random sourcing is a mean-field rule with no notion of nearness, which the literature's experience suggests gives either tall or layered orders.

**The valency cap (formerly T-I14b).** Folded into C5: the closed form gives γ* ≈ √(P/12); since P∞ is bounded only below, the output is the cap needed for a frame invisible up to a given boost, not a decision.

### 8. Risks

- **The manifoldlikeness problem is famously hard.** It has been open since 1987. Any growth rule that produced 3+1-dimensional, manifold-like, Lorentz-invariant orders would itself be a major result in quantum gravity, well beyond CO. A first test should be judged as a test of the rule it fixes, not as an attempt at that result.
- **Rule search is not a test.** A rule family searched until it gives 3 or 4 dimensions is the hypergraph programme's failure mode. K3 is there to stop a dimension being put in through parameters; the rule should be fixed by C7 before any search, with variants registered as variants.
- **Falsifying a stand-in.** C7 is built here, not read from the record. Its failure must not be reported as a failure of R2 or of CO's statements on dimension.
- **Small N.** Estimators are biased at small sizes (Abajian & Carlip find d_MM → 2 at small volumes). Calibration on sprinklings at the same N is essential, and a pass at N = 2×10⁴ says nothing about the continuum.
- **Mean-field sourcing.** Drawing units uniformly from the whole universe is the only geometry-free choice, but it makes every element a potential neighbour of every other, which is itself nonlocal; it may be the reason for failure rather than C7's idea.
- **Reinvention.** Transitive percolation, its posts and its continuum-limit curve are known; T-I14a must cite them and report transitive percolation as a reference arm, not as CO's.

### Ruling (30 October 2026)

Allen delegated choice 3: "make the call on decision 1, the one best for CO and moving forward." Ruled on that delegation, as provisional.

**Choice 3, (i): CO's order is generated by possibility's transfers.** (P1)–(P3) are adopted:
- the order is the transitive closure of unit transfers;
- each unit leaves an element to exactly one later element;
- each element carries finitely many units.

**Why this option.** It is the only one that makes CO's history a definite, generated order, and it turns Lorentz invariance from an unexamined borrowed input into a question with a number attached. Option (ii) avoids the fork by leaving the order's links undefined; option (iii) leaves the grid-free line with no dynamics.

**Consequences.**
- Every element has finitely many links, so the order is not a Lorentz-invariant sprinkling (Bombelli–Henson–Sorkin 2009).
- Within a frame, the links an element needs out to boost γ grow as
$$
\langle L(\eta)\rangle\approx12\gamma^2 ,
$$
  so a frame stays hidden up to γ only if the units an element releases satisfy P_e ≳ 12γ²: about 1.1×10²⁰ (Crab synchrotron electrons, γ ≈ 3×10⁹) to 1.2×10²³ (ultra-high-energy protons, γ ≈ 10¹¹).
- This is a required cap, not a data exclusion: no CO rule yet says what an observer beyond γ* ≈ √(P_e/12) would see.
- Under C1b's count per hop (R1's mean outflow, about 12c per tick) the requirement fails unless c ≳ 10¹⁹, so (P1) favours C1a, where the cap is the units a commitment releases. Choice 1 stays open.
- C7's hold-rule growth satisfies (P1)–(P3) and discrete general covariance exactly (Reading I15 §4.3, exploratory).

Choices 1, 2 and 4–6 are left open.
