# Where ED's gravity stands: passed, failed, open

Oct 21, 2026 · Allen Proxmire · Runs 1–80 of the commitment-ontology lattice record

## The short version

The lattice engine matches every gravity test it has been set against, as far as it can resolve, with one new tension. The one failure found along the way, patterns bound inside a body falling wrongly (Run 45), was fixed by the full pattern rule. With matter in exact hydrostatic balance, adding energy to a star raises its far-field mass by that energy, as in GR, to within about 0.5% on the engine's grids (Runs 50, 53, 56, 58). In a medium with no built-in scale, bound matter falls exactly like its energy (Run 48). ED's medium does have a scale, so, taking a body's inertia to be its total energy (a ruling), compact bodies slightly break the strong equivalence principle (Runs 52–57); nothing in ED's papers fixes how much, and the pulsar triple bounds it. **The new tension:** ED's neutron stars hold only about three quarters of GR's maximum mass, so with standard nuclear matter ED cannot reach the measured 2 M☉ pulsars; it can with matter near the causal limit, and that matter passes nuclear theory and QCD (Runs 57, 59, 60). GW170817 and NICER then leave one kind: soft like chiral EFT up to twice nuclear density, then sharply stiff (Run 61); heavy-ion collisions allow that only at the edge of their bounds (Run 62). Run 65 then found that ED's static field equation is GR's own equation for the clock rate; ED differed only in how space curves. Under the provisional choice that space curves as in GR, ED's stars and static black holes become GR's, so this tension goes away. Run 66 showed what that choice keeps: written on the space the medium lives on, GR's static gravity is ED's own field equation, clock and 'vacuum draws nothing' rule, with that space curved by stress instead of flat. Under it, one tension is new: a static black hole's surface is cold, which conflicted with the ruling that black holes evaporate (Run 67). Run 68 confirmed it for a collapse: Hawking's flux lasts only about 8M ln(P₀/p\_s) after formation (at least about 86M, carrying about one quantum), then stops, so ED's holes do not evaporate. It was then ruled (21 October) that ED's holes are stable, replacing the earlier ruling that they evaporate. Run 71 modelled a collapse: the medium's stress carries infalling matter through the hole's throat, and pressureless matter then never emits again; but any pressure turns the matter back and it comes out again within milliseconds to seconds, so for holes to stay, matter must commit before that point. It was then ruled (21 October) that it commits where the medium's possibility per place falls to about twice its crossover scale p\_s, which comes before any matter can turn back. Runs 73–74 confirmed that every causal kind of matter reaches that point first, and that the hole is then silent apart from one brief flash.

- **Matched:** Newton, clocks slowing in gravity, everything falling alike, light bending, Mercury's orbit, the Earth–Moon test, frame dragging, binary-pulsar energy loss, bound energy pulling as mass, black-hole ringdowns and images.
- **How:** about a third come from ED's own rules. The rest build on established physics, as every new theory does: mostly "physics looks the same to a moving observer" (which GR also takes as a starting principle) and GR's black-hole exterior (which GR derives). They are kept as bookkeeping, marking where a later derivation could go deeper.
- **Failed:** many earlier ideas failed and were replaced. That is the record working as designed: each failure is kept, with what replaced it.
- **New predictions:** ED's central claim, that time runs one way at the bottom and the familiar time-symmetric physics is recovered by averaging, passed the engine's own registered test (Run 29). One new prediction already meets data, as a bound: compact bodies slightly break the strong equivalence principle, with a fixed sign (Run 55); the pulsar triple limits its size, which ED does not yet fix. ED's other claims (the arrow, a vacuum that does not pull, no point of infinite density) have not yet been compared with data at physical scales.

## Failed, not yet fixed

One failure is still open.

- **The strong-field mass bookkeeping (Run 40b).** In a very strong field, the engine misses a bound pattern's binding by about one binding. In ordinary gravity what is left is tiny (Run 44). With GR's exterior adopted for black holes, it matters only inside compact objects and possibly in neutron stars.

## Open

Nine questions are open.

1. **Bound energy at neutron-star strength.** *Pulling:* with matter in exact point-by-point hydrostatic balance, ED's static rules come from a single energy principle, and adding a quantum raises a star's far-field mass by its energy. With four grids the registered test passes on the line: the continuum miss is within about 1% of the quantum's energy, and how the grid error shrinks is not settled (Runs 53, 56). The old shortfall was 4%. That is the same first law as GR's; it does not mean a neutron star's mass and radius match GR's, which is still open. *Falling:* in a medium with no built-in scale, bound matter falls exactly like its energy (Run 48). In ED's own medium a compact body is pulled slightly harder than its inertia, Δ ≈ C·p\_s/P₀, with C ≈ 0.33–0.49 for the pulsar in the triple system (Run 55).
2. **What sets the medium's scale, P₀/p\_s.** The draw rule that comes from one action (adopted 20 October) and inertia = total energy (ruled 20 October) turn ED's medium scale into a prediction: compact bodies slightly break the strong equivalence principle, Δ ≈ C·p\_s/P₀ (Runs 55, 57). **A reading of ED's clock and medium papers found nothing that fixes P₀/p\_s or either scale:** bandwidth has no upper bound or quantum, and the vacuum is only ever normalized. One tie turned up inside the engine: its Newton constant scales as 1/p\_s, so ED's identification of the hop with the Planck length would conflict with the engine's clocks unless the hop is about 300–1,000 Planck lengths.
3. **Neutron stars.** At first order, the pulsar triple tightens the medium's scale to P₀/p\_s ≳ 10⁵. A body's own binding passes its registered fall test (Run 46c, within 1 part in 1,000). With realistic equations of state, ED's own medium gives the pulsar C ≈ 0.40–0.49, so the triple needs P₀/p\_s ≳ 1.7–2.1×10⁵ (Run 57). **Maximum mass, a requirement on dense matter:** ED's static field holds about 0.75–0.8 of GR's maximum mass, and it was ruled that ED's interior equations bind for neutron stars. With the standard equations of state SLy, APR4 and MPA1 ED's heaviest stars are 1.65, 1.69 and 1.91 M☉, short of PSR J0740+6620 (2.08 ± 0.07). With matter allowed only by nuclear theory, causality and QCD it passes (Run 60), but only if the sound speed is near the causal limit just above where nuclear theory is trusted: c\_s² ≥ 0.33 from 1.1 times nuclear density, or ≥ 0.8 from twice nuclear density. GR needs neither. With ED's own exterior and tidal Love numbers (Run 61), GW170817 excludes the first route (tidal deformability 1230–1588 against ≤ 720) and PSR J0437's radius disfavours it. The second passes (374–658), provided ED's inspiral matches GR's to 3.5 post-Newtonian orders, which is not yet derived. It fits J0437, but its 2.08 M☉ stars sit 1–2σ below J0740's radius as a GR analysis would read them. So ED needs matter like chiral EFT up to about twice nuclear density, then a sharp rise to c\_s² ≳ 0.7–0.9: a prediction about nuclear matter that can be tested without gravity. Heavy-ion collisions measure that pressure in the laboratory (Run 62). Read for neutron-star matter, they let ED reach 2.08 M☉ only at the +2σ edge of every input together and with a stiff symmetry energy (slope L ≳ 95 MeV); otherwise ED's heaviest stars are 1.88–2.07 M☉. GR keeps ample room. ED's own exterior also gives a different inspiral from GR's at second post-Newtonian order (Run 63, a derivation), so GW170817's GR-based analysis does not strictly apply to ED. It was then ruled that black-hole binaries behave as in GR (option A extended), so ED's second-order tests lie in neutron-star systems. Those need one more ruling: whether a body's own binding tension shears space (Run 64). Chosen provisionally that it does, in GR's form (option 3). Run 65 shows that makes ED's static stars and holes GR's exactly (maximum masses 2.05/2.19/2.46 M☉, a horizon where the clock stops). ED's strong-equivalence-principle prediction survives (C ≈ 0.38–0.44), and the earlier neutron-star results remain as what ED's original rule for space predicts. Run 66 re-derived the strong-equivalence-principle formula under option 3: it carries over unchanged (C ≈ 0.37–0.44, so P₀/p\_s ≳ 1.6–1.9×10⁵). Run 67 found that a static hole's clock stops just outside where GR's horizon would be, at a cold surface with no event horizon, which conflicts with the ruling that black holes evaporate. Run 68: a collapse emits Hawking's flux for only about 8M ln(P₀/p\_s), then nothing, so the holes do not evaporate; provisionally ruled stable (21 October). Run 69: around spinning holes the medium's clock is that of observers moving with the frame dragging, ruling (21 October, that frame dragging is possibility's own flow, so spinning holes are Kerr outside the horizon (the alternative is disfavoured by X-ray spins, a gravitational-wave remnant and jets).
4. **Deriving the inputs:** Lorentz invariance (now extended to strongly self-gravitating bodies, so inertia = total energy), GR's black-hole exterior (option B, long-term), stress pulling, proper size.

## Where ED says something new (not yet tested)

ED makes six claims GR and quantum mechanics do not. Only the last two concern data already in hand: one as a bound, one as a demand on nuclear physics.

- **Time's direction is fundamental.** This is ED's central claim, and it passed the engine's own registered test (Run 29): commitments are strictly one-way, and averaging over time recovers the time-symmetric-looking records that standard physics describes. What remains is comparing it with data at physical scales, where the leftover order (about c/T in a clock's record) is not yet known.
- **The vacuum never commits, so it does not pull.** That matches how small the observed cosmological constant is, but its size has not been computed.
- **No point of infinite density.** Possibility per place is finite, so a well ends where it runs out (Paper 042). Under option 3 (Run 67) the mean-field geometry ends at a surface where an infaller's tides grow without bound; under the ruling, infalling matter commits before reaching it, at areal radius 3m (Runs 73–74).
- **Information is kept as record.** What falls into a black hole becomes part of the history, never lost. Under the ruling that ED's holes are stable (21 October, after Run 68), it never comes back out as radiation.
- **Neutron stars differ from GR in two ways.** (1) A small strong-equivalence-principle violation: ED's medium has a built-in scale, so, with inertia = total energy, a neutron star is pulled slightly harder than its inertia, Δ ≈ C·p\_s/P₀, positive and growing with compactness (C ≈ 0.37–0.44 for the pulsar in PSR J0337+1715 under the provisional option 3, Runs 65–66; 0.40–0.49 under ED's original rule for space, Run 57). GR gives exactly zero. The pulsar triple bounds it (P₀/p\_s ≳ 1.6–1.9×10⁵ under option 3); nothing in ED fixes its size. (2) Under ED's original rule for space only (withdrawn under the provisional option 3, Run 65), heavy stars need very stiff matter: ED holds about three quarters of GR's maximum mass, so the measured 2 M☉ pulsars require nuclear matter near the causal limit above about twice nuclear density (Runs 57, 59). Nuclear theory and neutron-star radii can test that, once their inferences are redone without assuming GR.

One surprising prediction that checks out would matter more than any number of matches.
