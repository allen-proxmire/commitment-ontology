# The state of CO, on one page

**What it is.** An event ontology with two primitives. *Possibility* sits at places and moves between related places, conserved in everything that passes and commits; each new place is born with its share. *Commitment* is an irreversible event that engages possibility. Time is the count of commitments; matter is what keeps re-committing; gravity is the pull toward where commitments run slow. CO is a reconstruction: it reaches known physics from these two and a set of rulings, each graded (candidate, needed, ruled, excluded, shape, silence). Where it reaches, no prediction differs from GR, and it makes no firm prediction of its own.

**What it reaches.**
- *Gravity.* Weak-field gravity and the four classical tests (Mercury as a fit). GR's exterior and tensor waves, given Lovelock's hypotheses. GR's source, Tolman's (ρ + S)N, for dust, radiation, gas stars, white dwarfs and planets (candidate rulings: travel draws twice, field energy as an amount, every draw is record), conditional on the walk on the curved network and on a place's whole possibility at the floor. The matter era and the radiation era (candidate). An inflation-like epoch with its own exit (no expansion count or ripple spectrum); three dimensions as a consistency condition (candidate).
- *Quantum mechanics, up to 1930, for a fixed number of particles, short of the exchange sign.* The amplitude rule (given its premises); Dirac's equation with spin ½ by identification; charge as a U(1) phase (candidate) with g = 2 at tree level; the two-body Coulomb coupling with Breit's terms (candidate); by identification: helium's ground state, positronium's levels and the magnetic part of its hyperfine split, hydrogen's 21 cm line at tree level (the proton's moment imported).

**What goes in.** Two primitives; rulings P1–P8 and C1–C8 (most candidate); three constants of its own (δ, P_max, Λ), plus the birth share P_b (value open), measured masses and 1/137; from quantum mechanics, the Born rule as an identification, Lüders conditioning, the joint law of an entangled pair, and an interaction's coupling and pointer basis; three experimental exclusions (a fixed hop order, a scalar gravitational wave, a one-way hop).
